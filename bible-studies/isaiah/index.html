Introduction

I.The historical situation that existed at the time of Isaiah’s ministry.

A.Kings of Judah (Southern Kingdom) traced their ancestry to David. 2 Sam. 7:1-17 (esp. vs. 16) The Northern Kingdom (10 tribes) had a succession of dynasties.

B.At Sinai, Israel agreed to be Yahweh’s people and to obey His law. Ex. 24:1-8 (esp. vs. 7)

C.Judah interpreted 2 Sam. 7:14-16 to mean that a son of David would be punished if he sinned, but under no circumstances would God allow his people to fall before their enemies or would He allow Jerusalem or His house, the temple, to fall. Isaiah, along with the other prophet to Judah from this period, Micah (Amos and Hosea prophesied to the Northern Kingdom at this time) had to contend with this popular assumption.

D.Uzziah (Azariah 2 Kings 15:1-7), king of Judah, reigned over a prosperous nation (791-740 B.C.) at the same time that Jeroboam 2 reigned over the Northern Kingdom (782-753 B.C.). Material prosperity and spiritual bankruptcy characterized the Northern Kingdom and Uzziah was greatly influenced by Jeroboam 2. Uzziah’s kingdom attained a degree of strength and prosperity which she had not enjoyed since the days of Solomon. He:

1.Developed a large standing army.

2.Built walls, towers, and fortifications for defense.

3.Dismantled the Philistine strongholds of Gath, Jabneh, and Ashdod

4.Built cities among the Philistines.

5.Made the Ammonites and Edomites vassals.

6.Commerce through the port at Ezion-geber brought the products of Africa and India into the markets of Jerusalem and Judea.

E.In the death year of Uzziah (740 B.C.), Isaiah began his prophetic ministry (Isa. 6:1-13). He lived to see the siege of Samaria and the end of the Northern Kingdom. He did not see the fall of Jerusalem, but he did see it approaching the precipice.

F.Temple revenues were at an all time high, but Isaiah saw this as paying service to God. Religious externals were the people’s focus and God was filled up with them (Isa. 1:11-15).

G.Ahaz replaced Jotham as king although not recorded in Isaiah. During his reign, Rezin, the king of Aram and Pekah, king of Israel (2 Kings 15:27-31), joined together to war against Jerusalem. Ahaz sent for Assyrian aid to help him defeat this coalition. Isaiah challenged the king of Judah to trust in God, but to no avail (Isa. 7:1-25).

1.Note: A prophet’s ministry was not constant as an apostle or pastor teacher’s was and is. The Word of the Lord came to the prophet only when God desired to communicate through him. It is also true that not everything that was said and done by the prophet was intended for God’s permanent record, the Bible.

H.In 701 B.C., the armies of Sennacherib, king of Assyria, came up against Jerusalem when Hezekiah was king (Isa. 36:1-22). Isaiah again admonishes the king of Judah to trust in His God and Hezekiah responds with faithfulness. God destroys the threatening army of 185,000 (Isa. 37:1-38). The last historical event recorded by Isaiah is the death of Sennacherib, king of Assyria (Isa. 37:37-38). This death occurred in 680 B.C.

I.Isaiah ministered from the year of Uzziah’s death to at least the year of Sennacherib’s death (740-680 B.C.). He lived during the reigns of Uzziah (Azariah), Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah. 2 Chron. 32:32 may indicate that Isaiah’s ministry ran past Hezekiah’s death.

J.Collateral passages describing the historical events of the time:

1.2 Kings 14:23 – 20:21 (Judah and Israel only)

2.2 Chron. 26:1 – 32:33 (Judah only)

3.Micah (ministered to Judah)

4.Amos (ministered to Israel)

5.Hosea (ministered to Israel)

II.Post Script --

A.Hezekiah’s son, Manasseh, took the throne at the age of 12 and ruled for 55 years. 2 Kings 21:1-18 and 2 Chron. 33:1-20.

B.Manasseh’s son, Amon, was worse than his father. 2 Chron. 33:21-25.

C.Tradition teaches that Manasseh had Isaiah killed by having him “sawn in two.” Heb. 11:37

The Message of the Prophet

Chapters 1 through 6 – God’s indictment of the nation.

Isaiah 1

I.Vs. 1 Isaiah’s prophecies focus on Judah and Jerusalem. Many nations are included in the book (ch. 13-23). All nations stand guilty before the Holy One of Israel and their guilt is clearly seen in their treatment of the people of God; Israel.

A.Vision which he saw – Isaiah both heard and saw the revelation given to him by God, Isa. 2:1 (Ezk. 1:1, 4, 15). This was a common means of receiving God’s revelation for the prophets.

Verses 2 through 23 – The charge

Verses 2 through 10 – The Lord’s accusation – They have broken His covenant.

II.Vs. 2–4 All creation would agree with God’s charge.

A.He first establishes His own innocence.

B.It is His children who have rebelled against Him (They are the transgressors, not Him. Isa. 66:24).

C.Animals know their masters – Israel (12 tribes) not her master

1.Ox – is submissive

2.Donkey – stupid

D.Both animals know the source of their sustenance, Israel neither knows or understands.

E.Holy One of Israel (used 25 times) is in contrast to the people of the nation:

1.sinful nation

2.people weighed down…iniquity

3.offspring of evildoers

4.sons…act corruptly

5.abandoned the Lord

6.despised the Holy One

7.turned away from Him

III.Vs. 5–6 Your bodies are beaten and bruised – untreated wounds…welts and open sores.

IV.Vs. 7 Hostile forces surround them – they are hated and they are losing their territory to foreign nations.

V.Vs. 8 Their condition is desolate. Deut. 28:15-68

A.Their cities are temporary structures.

VI.Vs. 9 Except for God’s grace, they should have been removed from the face of the earth – cleansed off the planet.

A.Note: Daughter of Zion – Jer. 4:31; Lam. 1:6, 2:13; Micah 1:13, 4:8; Zech. 9:9

1.Sodom and Gomorrah – cities which speak. Rom. 9:27-29; Ezk. 16:45-52

VII.Vs. 10 Like rulers, like people

VIII.Vs. 11–15 Israel’s faith has become ritual –

A.multiple sacrifices, vs. 11

B.incense burning, vs. 13

C.festivals and feasts, vs. 13-14

D.prayers, vs. 15

E.rituals cannot cover iniquity. That which God has commanded is worthless if not done with a proper heart. Heb. 11:6; Rom. 14:23

IX.Vs. 16–17 The people had the mistaken idea that they could live like the devil so long as they followed through with their ritual observances. Note that God does not call on Israel to “confess their sin” (give verbal agreement by reciting the wrongs committed) but rather to clean up their act, cease doing evil and begin doing that which is right. James 1:22-25; 2:14-26; Rom. 2:13-16; 17-29

A.Bottom line: Instead of trusting in religious ritual (vs. 10-15), the people are to obey God – right attitudes being manifested in right actions.

B.Seek, reprove, defend, plead -- Isa. 1:23; 10:1-2; Deut. 24:17-22; 26:12; 27:19; Mk. 7:9-13; Rom. 8:1-14

X.Vs. 18–20 Let us reason together – God is not calling for a negotiating session between the people and Himself, He is calling on them to acknowledge that He is right and they are wrong.

A.If they acknowledge the extent of their sins then God in His grace will cleanse them.

B.The obedient will…

C.The disobedient will…

XI.Vs. 21–23 Jerusalem, the faithful city – now a harlot.

A.Full of justice and righteousness – now full of murderers.

B.Silver – dross (residue left in the smelting process after the silver is removed).

C.Fine wine – mixed with water

D.The rulers of the city are leading the people to ruin:

1.theft

2.bribery

3.self enrichment

4.injustice

XII.Vs. 24–31 God’s sentence is delivered.

A.Therefore:

B.God will relieve Himself by avenging Himself. Not to get even but to get back.

XIII.Vs. 25 The dross will be removed so that silver can once again be refined.

XIV.Vs. 26 New judges, new counselors – Jerusalem will once again be called a faithful city full of righteousness.

XV.Vs. 27 Restoration

XVI.Vs. 28 Cleansing

XVII.Vs. 29–30 Oak trees – Isa. 57:4-5

A.Gardens – Isa. 65:3; 66:17

XVIII.Vs. 31 Fire is God’s answer – Heb. 6:7-9

Isaiah 2

I.Vs. 1 The word which Isaiah saw (Isa. 1:1)

A.A time will come when Jerusalem will be the center of the world, Holy to the Lord. Blessings will flow out from her. Isaiah, (God) repeatedly intersperses this promise in the midst of His pronouncements of judgment.

B.Isa. 11:6-10

C.Isa. 25:6-10(a)

D.Isa. 27:12-13

E.Isa. 56:7-8 God’s Holy Mountain will be established (the gates of hell shall not prevent it)

II.Vs. 2 In the last days – the time of the millennial kingdom – Isaiah saw it yet he probably did not know the time of its establishment 1 Pet. 1:10-12.

A.The mountain of the House of the Lord.

1.mountain – Rev. 17:9; Dan. 2:35 of the House Ezk. 40-43

2.will be established – Zech. 14:8-9

3.will be raised – Zech. 14:10-11

B.All nations will stream to it. Isa. 25:6-9; 56:6-8; Jer. 3:17-18; Zeph. 3:9-10; Zech. 8:20-23

III.Vs. 3 Peoples from all nations will be drawn to God’s temple in Jerusalem because the Lord Himself will teach them of His way, Isa. 51:4. People everywhere will realize that God’s teaching is foundational to a meaningful life. They will want to know it (He will teach us His ways) and to live according to it (walk in His paths).

IV.Vs. 4 All nations will have a judge that they can come to who will exercise perfect justice. Every dispute will be settled righteously.

A.There will be no war because the implements of warfare (swords and spears) will be turned into implements of agriculture (plowshares and hooks – opposite of conditions existing during the tribulation, Joel 3:9-17).

V.Vs. 5 Invitation to the “house of Jacob”

A.At the time of Isaiah’s ministry Israel already possessed the teachings of the Lord. If they are so valuable and following those in the coming kingdom age will produce such blessing, why not follow them now. It is not that Israel could not; she would not, Matt. 23:37 (she had far more opportunity than just knowing the Law, Rom. 9:3-5).

VI.Vs. 6–9 Israel’s current condition (all twelve tribes are still reigning. Isaiah is admonishing Judah, but as bad as Judah has become, the northern kingdom was worse because she had separated herself from Jerusalem, the temple, and the Levitical priesthood, 1 Kings 12:25-33).

A.God had abandoned His people, not because He no longer loved them, but because they had become like the pagans who surrounded them:

1.filled with the ways of the people from the east (Assyria)

2.practicing divination like the Philistines, 1 Sam. 6:1-5; 2 Kings 1:1-4; Lev. 19:26 etc.

3.strike bargains with the children of foreigners

4.their land filled with:

a.silver and gold – financial prosperity

b.treasures

c.horses and chariots – military might

d.idols – they assumed that they had what they had because of their idol worship.

B.The result of the above being:

1.the common man has been humbled.

2.the man of importance has been abased.

3.Isaiah cries out “do not forgive them.”

VII.Vs. 10–11 In the end, only the Lord will be exalted! vs. 17

A.When He comes to judge, people will:

1.attempt to hide from His presence. vs. 19, 21; Rev. 6:16

2.they will fear His splendor, realizing their arrogance.

3.find that their wealth cannot save them.

B.Note: These prophecies will find a more immediate fulfillment when the Assyrians and Babylonians execute God’s judgment upon the people. Isa. 10:5-6

1.They will also find the greater fulfillment at the time of the end (tribulation). As stated before, Isaiah had no knowledge of the time span involved in the carrying out of God’s plan.

VIII.Vs. 12–22 When the Lord of Hosts finally speaks, man will listen!

A.The proud and lofty will be humbled. vs. 9, 11, 17

B.The cedars and oaks (used to make idols) will be abased.

C.Mountains – government authorities.

D.Towers and walls – military defenses.

E.Merchant ships – Rev. 18:11-19

IX.Vs. 17 See verse 11

X.Vs. 18 Where are the idols when the Lord of Hosts arises?

XI.Vs. 19 When He arises to make the earth tremble (see vs. 21).

XII.Vs. 20 As they attempt to flee, men will throw aside their useless gods to the moles and bats.

XIII.Vs. 22 Another invitation from Isaiah (God)

A.Stop living in awe or fear before humans whose breath can be snuffed out in a moment.

B.Stop worshipping the creation – look to the creator!

Isaiah 3

I.Isaiah gives examples of the nation’s (peoples’) present sins that needed to be judged by God.

Verses 1 – 3 God is going to take away from Judah and Jerusalem any semblance of order and good government. Because of her sin, the Lord will take away the supplies and people on which she relies.

II.Vs. 1 Food and water.

III.Vs. 2 Mighty man and the warrior.

A.Judge

B.Elder

IV.Vs. 3 Captain of fifty

A.Honorable man

B.Counselor

C.Expert artisan

D.Skillful enchanter

V.Vs. 4 He will replace them with inexperienced boys and children.

VI.Vs. 5 As a result:

A.The people will be oppressed.

B.The youth will storm against the elder.

C.The inferior against the honorable.

VII.Vs. 6 Anybody who owns a cloak will be grabbed in an effort to place him in charge. Ruins will be all that there is to be ruled over.

VIII.Vs. 7 No one will take the position because no one will have an answer for the condition that the land is in.

IX.Vs. 8–9 The people have defied God and are open about their sin much like the people of Sodom. Gen. 18:20; 19:1-11; Isa. 1:9-10. Therefore, the coming disaster will be brought on by themselves.

A.Woe to them — Woe to the wicked

1.to you

2.to those

3.to me

4.to Assyria

5.to the land

6.to the proud crown of the drunkards to the obstinate children to the shepherds who are destroying

B.The word appears 22 times in this prophetic book. More than in any other book! They have brought evil upon themselves.

X.Vs. 10 When God judges, the righteous need not fear, they will be justly rewarded for their deeds.

XI.Vs. 11 To the wicked – what he deserves will be done to him.

XII.Vs. 12 What has happened to God’s people?

A.They are oppressed by children.

B.Ruled over by women.

C.Led astray by all who lead them.

D.They have lost their way and no longer even know the path.

XIII.Vs. 13 The Lord is ready to judge both the people and the leaders.

XIV.Vs. 14–15 Two charges are leveled against the leaders:

A.They have devoured God’s vineyard. Isa. 5:1-10; Ps. 80:8-19; Jer. 2:21, 12:10-13; Ezk. 15:6-8

B.They have plundered the poor taking home their goods, grinding their faces.

C.Note: The poor are always in our midst and they are there to test us! James 1:27; 2:1-9

XV.Vs. 16 Daughters of Zion (Jerusalem) are proud (before God’s judgment).

A.Walk with heads high

B.Seductive eyes

C.Mincing steps (to walk with short steps or affected preciseness, walking in artificial manner)

D.Their jewelry tinkles as they move.

E.After God’s judgment:

1.Scalp will be covered with scabs.

2.Bald headed. 1 Cor. 11:14-15

XVI.Vs. 18–26 In that day – looks forward to the Babylonian captivity (that is the judgment which comes upon Judah and Jerusalem).

A.The Babylonian soldiers will take away all the women’s fine jewelry and wardrobes.

B.Instead of fragrance they will stink.

C.The women will be pulled by a rope and wear sackcloth (Assyrians lined their captives up and strung them using fishhooks which they ran through the lower lip). Amos 4:2

D.Branding as a slave instead of beauty.

E.They will mourn because their husbands, uncles, brothers, and sons will die in battle.

F.Jerusalem will be a place of lament and mourning, deserted, and down cast.

Isaiah 5

I.Vs. 1–7 The worthless vineyard.

A.A common picture when describing Israel. Isa. 3:14; Ps. 80:8-18; Jer. 2:21, 12:10; Hosea 10:1

II.Vs. 1–2 God’s care for His vineyard. Heb. 6:7

A.God planted a vineyard on a fertile hillside.

B.He removed the stones (no small job).

C.He planted only the best vines.

D.He built a watchtower (a stone structure from which to guard the vineyard).

E.He made a winepress with anticipation and waited.

F.It produced only worthless grapes. Heb. 6:8

III.Vs. 3–4 God asks those who are the vineyard, two questions:

A.Was it His fault that things turned out the way they did?

B.Why did His vineyard produce worthless grapes?

IV.Vs. 5–6 There is but one thing left for God to do – burn it. Heb. 6:8

A.Three actions will be forthcoming from God:

1.He will remove the protective hedge/wall surrounding it.

a.wild animals will consume it.

b.all who surround it will trample it down.

2.He will turn it into wasteland.

a.Without pruning and hoeing, briars and thorns will overrun the hill.

B.He will stop the rains from falling on it.

C.Without God’s protection Judah will be ruined. Note: John 15:1-11

V.Vs. 7 The vineyard is identified.

A.Make no mistake – Israel and Judah are not right! Ezk. 18:25, 29

B.He looked for justice – bloodshed. Isa. 1:21, 3:14-15

C.For righteousness – a cry of distress. Isa. 1:23

VI.Vs. 8–30 Woe, Woe, Woe to those who…

VII.Vs. 8 Woe to those who accumulate real estate, acquiring it until they own it all. Micah 2:1; Lev. 25:13-17, 23-31

VIII.Vs. 9 In my ears the Lord of hosts has sworn…

IX.Vs. 10 Ten acres of vineyard will yield a bath (6 gallons).

A.A homar of seed (normally yields many bushels).

B.Will yield an ephah of grain (1/12 of the seed sown).

X.Vs. 11 Woe to those who start drinking early in the morning and end their evenings sipping wine (note verse 22 – must have been a real problem).

XI.Vs. 12 Their banquets are filled with music and void of any remembrance of the work of their God’s hands!

XII.Vs. 13 Therefore

A.Their abandonment of God will result in His abandonment of them – they don’t know of Him, He doesn’t remember them either.

B.Their greatest men will die of hunger.

C.The drunkards will die of thirst.

XIII.Vs. 14 Therefore – again refers back to verses 8-12

A.Sheol will accommodate the need. Rev. 6:8

B.Jerusalem’s finest will descend into it.

XIV.Vs. 15 No matter what position the Israelite had occupied in their pre-judgment life they will be humbled by the judgment which God will bring down upon them.

XVIII.Vs. 20 Woe to those who call evil good and good evil:

XIX.Vs. 21 Who needs God when I have Me!

A.Are heroes in drinking.

B.Justify the wicked for a bribe.

C.Take away righteousness of the righteous.

XXI.Vs. 24 Therefore as … for they have

XXII.Vs. 25 On this account (reasons given above):

A.God’s anger burns.

B.He has stretched out His hand…struck them down.

C.Their corpses lay like garbage in the middle of the streets.

D.With all this, He is not finished. Further judgments are yet to fall.

XXIII.Vs. 26–30 When the time comes for the judgment to fall, it will be as if God had raised a banner as a signal for war.

A.God’s armies will seem to come from the ends of the earth.

B.Their speed and ferociousness will completely devastate Judah.

C.The light will go out.

Isaiah 6

I.Vs. 1 Isaiah ministered during King Uzziah’s reign. Isa. 1:1

A.Isaiah’s vision recorded here occurred within the 12 calendar months before or after the king’s death, which occurred in 740 B.C.

B.Isaiah’s vision of God (the King, vs. 5) would be in contrast to what Isaiah saw when looking upon King Uzziah, the man.

C.He reigned for 52 years. 2 Chron. 26:3

D.He sinned…being struck with leprosy. 2 Chron. 26:16-21

E.He was cut off from the house of the Lord. 2 Chron. 26:21

F.Three things struck Isaiah about the “King of Israel.” He was:

1.Seated on a throne – rather than on the Ark of the Covenant. Ezk. 1:25-28; Rev. 4:1-3

2.High and exalted – do we see Jesus the way He was 2000 years ago or the way He is now?

3.The train of His robe filled the temple – a sign of His royalty and majesty.

II.Vs. 2 Seraphim

A.Only mentioned here by name and in Rev. 4:7-8 by description if they are both the same angelic being (Ezekiel saw beings with 4 wings – Ezk. 1:4-14. The angelic beings in Ezekiel are later said to be Cherubim – Ezk. 10:15, 20).

B.Note: See notes on the angels.

III.Vs. 3 God’s holiness should be appreciated in contrast to the character and actions of Uzziah the King and even Isaiah and the people of Israel (at their best). 1 John 1:5-10.

A.Though God “dwelt” between the Cherubim in the Holy of Holies (Ex. 25:17-22), the whole earth cannot contain Him. 1 Kings 8:27 Ezekiel had a similar vision of God (Ezk. 1). Unlike the Cherubim, the number of Seraphs is not given.

IV.Vs. 4 The foundation stones on which the doorposts stood (Amos 9:1) shook at the voice of the Seraphs while the temple filled with smoke. 1 Kings 8:10-13

V.Vs. 5 Isaiah’s response to what he was seeing!

A.When seen next to the purity of God’s holiness, the impurity of man is striking. Similar responses are recorded for:

1.Ezekiel – “I fell on my face” Ezk. 1:28; 3:23

2.Job – “I repent in dust and ashes” Job 42:5-6

3.Peter – “Go away from me Lord, for I am a sinful man” Lk. 5:8

4.John – “I fell at His feet like a dead man” Rev. 1:17

B.Under different circumstances Isaiah had pronounced “woes” to the nation of Israel ( Isa. 5). Standing in the presence of God, his realization changes to “woe is me”!

C.Isaiah’s response makes it clear that he sees himself as not only a man, but also part of a people – family of Israel.

VI.Vs. 6–7 A Seraph’s response – Isaiah acknowledged his sin and God responded to that acknowledgement. 1 John 1:9; Ps. 78:38-39

VII.Vs. 8 Us – Gen. 1:26; 11:7. The doctrine of the “trinity” is not explicitly taught in the O.T., but it is there; for God is the same God in both testaments.

A.Asking the question “who will go” is for Isaiah’s sake. Opportunity exists for service and opportunity for Isaiah only requires him responding. His response “Here am I. Send me!” Eph. 2:10

VIII.Vs. 9–10 God’s commissioning of Isaiah.

A.Isaiah, in volunteering, probably expected a positive outcome to his ministry. God tells him otherwise.

B.The people had not listened before and they would not listen now.

C.The Lord did not delight in judging His people.

D.Discipline is necessary when disobedience is man’s choice.

E.The people, on hearing Isaiah’s message, are going to be even more hardened to the Lord. John 12:37-41

IX.Vs. 11–13 Isaiah was ready to speak whatever God wanted him to say. He did wonder “how long will I have to go on preaching to this non-responsive people?”

A.Until judgment comes (Babylon carries them off).

B.Until the people are deported.

C.Until their cities and fields are ruined.

D.(All this happened long after Isaiah’s death, meaning “for as long as he lived he preached receiving a negative or no response.”)

E.The tenth who remained in the land were the poor. 2 Kings 24:14

F.God then gives Isaiah a word of encouragement:

G.Though 9/10 of the people will be removed, the remnant will again grow in to a tree.

Isaiah 7

Verses 1-2 – The historical situation.

I.Vs. 1 Rezin, king of Aram; and Pekah, king of Israel (10 tribes) {752-732 B.C.} had made an alliance.

A.After Jeroboam II (793-753 B.C.) of Israel died, the Northern Kingdom became increasingly weak. Rezin ended up being Aram’s last king and Pekah was Israel’s next-to-last king. 2 Kings 15:27-16:20 Rezin convinced Pekah to join with him against Judah. A.Rely on God’s protection.

I.Vs. 1 During the time that God was/is being rejected by Israel, He has been open and receiving those Gentiles who come to Him. Rom. 10:20-21; Rom. 9:30-31; Jn. 1:11-13

II.Vs. 2 Judges 17:6; 21:25

III.Vs. 3–5 Israel had become a people who continually provoke Me to My face by:

A.Offering sacrifices in gardens — Isa. 1:29; 66:17

1.oak groves and gardens connected with adulterous, heathen worship

B.Burning incense on bricks — Jer. 11:11-15

C.Sit among graves — Isa. 8:19

1.necromancy – consulting the dead, while sitting among the graves

D.Spend the night in secret places

E.Eat swine’s flesh

1.disregarding His dietary laws

F.Eat the broth of unclean meat

G.Who are self-righteous

1.so religiously arrogant they became repulsive

H.Israelites that do the above are as irritating and painful as smoke in one’s nose.

IV.Vs. 6–7 God keeps it written before Him

A.“I will repay”

B.At the time of Isaiah, Assyria, and later Babylon, followed by Rome and the world at the time of the anti-Christ, were all used by God to judge Israel.

C.There are always consequences of sin.

D.The sin of adultery is stressed here. Jer. 31:31-32; Isa. 57: 3-7

V.Vs. 8–12 Judgment will be directed at the nation yet a remnant will be saved out of it. That remnant will be given the Land as their possession. Deut. 30:3-5

A.Valley of Achor — Hosea 2:13-23

B.The destiny of those who follow the God of Israel and those who do not are opposite destinies!

C.Fortune and Destiny were names of gods Israel worshipped in her attempt to know the future. Gad (the Aramean god of luck) and Meni (god of destiny)

D.God called – they did not answer

1.they did not hear — Rom. 10:14

2.they did evil

3.they chose

F.with a glad heart — with a heavy heart

G.called by another name — your name will become a curse

H.(live) — God will slay you

A.forgetting their former troubles

B.their sins (former troubles) become hidden from His sight

A.the millennial time — Isa. 2:3-4; Zech. 14:9

Isaiah 66

I.THE CONCLUSION OF THE MATTER

II.Vs. 1 God is sitting in Heaven.

A.Rev. 4:1-11; Isa. 6:1-4 — quoted in Acts 7:48-50

B.with the earth as His footstool.

C.No one can build Him a dwelling place because of Who He Is!

D.1 Kgs. 8:10-29ff

III.Vs. 2 Man cannot build for God without using materials which God made for man. Heb. 11:3; Rom. 4:17

A.God is not looking for a house to dwell in!

B.He is looking for a people who:

1.are humble

2.contrite of spirit — Isa. 57:15

3.tremble at His Word (Bible)

C.Put simply: God is interested in “who we are” more so than in what we do! What we do SHOULD be a reflection of who we are! This puts all humans on a level playing field.

IV.Vs. 3 Ritual – which their soul delights in, is what makes them an abomination. Isa. 1:11-17

A.Ritual is a way that men are drawn to – but not God!

V.Vs. 4 So – because of the way man is (including Israel, and more so), God will choose their eternal place

A.I called

B.I spoke — Rom. 3:1-4a

C.This verse is almost identical to Isa. 65:12

VI.Vs. 5 There are in the visible gatherings of “God’s people”

A.tremble at His Word

B.hate those who tremble at His Word

C.Those who “hate” those who tremble at His Word, who exclude such from the assembly...will be put to shame.

VII.Vs. 6 When the Lord returns to the city – Jerusalem, and the Temple within it, He will render recompense (pay back) to His enemies. Heb. 12:2; Matt. 25:41-46; Rev. 20:11-15

A.Note: Enemies of His children are enemies of His!

VIII.Vs. 7–9 Israel’s ultimate rebirth, at the Second Advent, will happen so quickly that the timing can be compared to a woman who gives birth at the same time as her first labor pain.

A.God does not start something and not finish it.

B.Can a land be born in one day?

C.Can a nation be brought forth all at once?
