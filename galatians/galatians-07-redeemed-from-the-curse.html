Redeemed from the Curse

Galatians 3:6–14  ·  NASB95

Key Verse

“Christ has redeemed us from the curse of the law, having become a curse for us…that the blessing of Abraham might come upon the Gentiles in Christ Jesus, that we might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith.”

Galatians 3:13–14

Long before Moses and the Law existed, Abraham simply believed God for something impossible — a son, from a body as good as dead — and “it was accounted to him for righteousness.” No law-keeping involved; the Law wouldn’t exist for another 430 years. The Judaizers claimed Abraham as their father too, but Paul draws the line sharply: only those who share Abraham’s faith are truly his sons. And that’s where the good news, foreseen even in Abraham’s own story, gets stated as a promise centuries in advance: “In you all the nations shall be blessed.” The blessing was never going to come through law-keeping. It was always going to come by faith — to Jew and Gentile alike.

But the Law brings something else entirely to anyone who tries to use it as a path to righteousness: a curse. Anyone relying on law-keeping is under obligation to keep all of it perfectly, and no one ever has. That leaves the whole human race under judgment — until Christ does something almost too costly to comprehend. He “redeemed us from the curse of the law, having become a curse for us,” taking on Himself the very curse the law pronounced, hanging on a tree in the place of everyone who deserved it. The result is a double gift: Gentiles receive the blessing promised to Abraham, and everyone who believes — Jew or Gentile — receives the promised Spirit, both entirely through faith, with nothing else added.

Questions to Carry Into the Passage

  1. Abraham was declared righteous by simply believing God, 430 years before the Law existed. Does your own confidence before God rest on that same simple believing, or has performance quietly crept back in as a requirement?
  2. Christ became a curse in your place so you would never have to bear it. Is there guilt or self-punishment you're still carrying that Christ already fully absorbed at the cross?
  3. The blessing of Abraham includes both justification and the promised Spirit, both received the same way — through faith. Are you treating your ongoing spiritual growth as another gift to receive by faith, or as a separate project to earn?
