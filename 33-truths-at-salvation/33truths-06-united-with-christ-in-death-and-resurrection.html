33 Truths at Salvation for Every Believer

United with Christ in Death and Resurrection

Key Verse: Romans 6:4  ·  NASB1995

Key Verse

“Therefore we have been buried with Him through baptism into death, so that as Christ was raised from the dead through the glory of the Father, so we too might walk in newness of life.”

Romans 6:4

Your history with sin didn’t just get forgiven — in God’s reckoning, it ended. Your new life didn’t start from nothing; it began in a borrowed tomb, on the far side of a death you shared with Christ.

What This Means for You

I.

Crucified with Christ

“knowing this, that our old self was crucified with Him, in order that our body of sin might be done away with, so that we would no longer be slaves to sin;”

Romans 6:6 · NASB1995

“I have been crucified with Christ; and it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me; and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave Himself up for me.”

Galatians 2:20 · NASB1995

Romans 6:6 states it as a completed fact: “our old self was crucified with Him, in order that our body of sin might be done away with, so that we would no longer be slaves to sin.” This isn’t something you have to accomplish; it already happened at the cross. Paul makes it intensely personal in Galatians 2:20 — “I have been crucified with Christ; and it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me” — and grounds the whole thing not in effort but “faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave Himself up for me.”

II.

Dead with Christ

“Now if we have died with Christ, we believe that we shall also live with Him,”

Romans 6:8 · NASB1995

“and He Himself bore our sins in His body on the cross, so that we might die to sin and live to righteousness; for by His wounds you were healed.”

1 Peter 2:24 · NASB1995

Because you died with Him, sin’s ownership over you as a master has been broken. “If we have died with Christ, we believe that we shall also live with Him” (Rom. 6:8) — death and resurrection are a package, never one without the other. Peter connects the purpose directly to the cross: Christ bore your sins in His body “so that we might die to sin and live to righteousness” (1 Pet. 2:24). Sin can still tempt you, but it no longer owns you.

III.

Buried with Christ

“Therefore we have been buried with Him through baptism into death, so that as Christ was raised from the dead through the glory of the Father, so we too might walk in newness of life.”

Romans 6:4 · NASB1995

“having been buried with Him in baptism, in which you were also raised up with Him through faith in the working of God, who raised Him from the dead”

Colossians 2:12 · NASB1995

Burial marks a death as final and complete. Romans 6:4 pictures baptism as burial “into death,” with no ambiguity left about whether the old life is really over. Colossians 2:12 adds that this burial “in baptism” is inseparable from being “raised up with Him through faith in the working of God, who raised Him from the dead” — there’s no going back to the old life; that chapter is closed for good.

IV.

Raised with Christ

“Therefore we have been buried with Him through baptism into death, so that as Christ was raised from the dead through the glory of the Father, so we too might walk in newness of life.”

Romans 6:4 · NASB1995

“Therefore if you have been raised up with Christ, keep seeking the things above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God.”

Colossians 3:1 · NASB1995

The same power that raised Jesus from the grave — “through the glory of the Father” (Rom. 6:4) — now walks you into an entirely new kind of life. Because that’s true, Colossians 3:1 gives the natural next step: “keep seeking the things above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God.” You’re not just forgiven; you’re alive in a way you weren’t before, with your attention now free to be pointed somewhere new.

Reflect

  1. What part of your “old self” do you most need to remember is already dead?
  2. What would it look like this week to actually walk in “newness of life” rather than old habits?
