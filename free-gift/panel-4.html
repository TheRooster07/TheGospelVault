Panel 4 of 5 · No Additions

No hidden fine print — faith alone in Christ alone.

John 4:10, 14 (NASB)

Jn 4:10 Jesus answered and said to her, "If you knew the gift of God, and who it is who says to you, 'Give Me a drink,' you would have asked Him, and He would have given you living water." Jn 4:14 But whoever drinks of the water that I will give him shall never thirst; but the water that I will give him will become in him a well of water springing up to eternal life.
One either believes or he does not believe.

Look for qualifiers on "believe" in John's Gospel, and you won't find them. He never writes "whoever really believes" or "whoever truly believes." He never speaks of "genuine faith," "real faith," or "effectual faith." That's not because John avoids the word "true" — he uses it elsewhere: the true light (1:9), the true bread (6:32), the true vine (15:1), true worshippers (4:23), the true God (17:3), disciples indeed (8:31). He simply never attaches it to believing. One either believes, or he does not believe. There's no hidden second tier of "believing enough."

There's also no separate condition standing next to faith. Take repentance. In John 4, the Samaritan woman had had multiple husbands and was living with a man not her husband, and she tried to deceive Jesus about it. Jesus never told her to repent. Instead He offered her eternal life for the asking — if you knew the gift of God… you would have asked Him, and He would have given you living water (John 4:10) — and for the drinking: whoever drinks of the water I give will never thirst (John 4:14). Both are simply pictures of believing. This isn't a problem for anyone who understands repentance as a change of mind — the mind changes as a person believes on Jesus for eternal life; it isn't a separate work added on top of faith.

Nor is there a commitment clause. Salvation is absolutely free and isn't conditioned on any human merit — it's what one receives, not what he earns or bargains for. He gives nothing for it, not even his own life. A believer gives his life to Christ only after believing; commitment follows salvation, it doesn't purchase it. John even shows us "secret disciples" who believed in Jesus but, out of fear, didn't confess Him publicly — Joseph of Arimathea (19:38), Nicodemus (19:39), and some of the Jewish rulers (12:42). John still counts them among those who believed.

If we're going to defend the faith, we have to start by defending faith alone in Christ alone — a simple, unconditional, non-meritorious response of accepting and trusting God's promise. Nothing hidden. Nothing added.

For reflection

  1. Have you ever heard salvation described with an added qualifier — "really" believe, "truly" surrender, "fully" commit? How does removing that qualifier change your own confidence?
  2. The Samaritan woman received eternal life without a call to repentance or reform. What does that tell you about what — and who — actually saves?

Faith alone in Christ alone. Not faith plus anything.
