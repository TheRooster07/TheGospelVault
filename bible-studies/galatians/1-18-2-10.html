Paul's Gospel Is Independent of the Judean Churches

① The Biblical Text

NKJV · Read together before anything else

Galatians 1:18-24

18 Then after three years I went up to Jerusalem to see Peter, and remained with him fifteen days.

19 But I saw none of the other apostles except James, the Lord's brother.

20 (Now concerning the things which I write to you, indeed, before God, I do not lie.)

21 Afterward I went into the regions of Syria and Cilicia.

22 And I was unknown by face to the churches of Judea which were in Christ.

23 But they were hearing only, “He who formerly persecuted us now preaches the faith which he once tried to destroy.”

24 And they glorified God in me.

1 Then after fourteen years I went up again to Jerusalem with Barnabas, and also took Titus with me.

2 And I went up by revelation, and communicated to them that gospel which I preach among the Gentiles, but privately to those who were of reputation, lest by any means I might run, or had run, in vain.

② Devotional

Heart preparation · read in the days before your group meets

“There is one Gospel, and it's all of grace, all of God. The ground is level at the cross.”

Paul kept a meticulous, sworn account of every trip he made to Jerusalem — “before God, I do not lie” (1:20). That mattered because the Judaizers were suggesting his gospel had really come from Jerusalem's blessing. It hadn't. Paul's independence from human approval wasn't pride; it was precision, protecting a gospel that belonged to Christ alone.

When Paul finally did return to Jerusalem years later, he didn't go to get his message checked or corrected. He went to carry relief to hungry believers, and only along the way — almost as an aside — did he compare notes with the leaders there, to be sure they were laboring toward the same goal, not working against each other.

When pressure came to add just one requirement to simple faith — in this case, circumcision for Titus — Paul would not yield, “not even for an hour.” And the very apostles some assumed would side against him instead recognized the same grace at work in him that was at work in Peter, and gave him the right hand of fellowship. One gospel. One grace. Level ground for everyone who comes to the cross.

Reflect

  1. Where might you feel pressure to seek human approval for something God has already made clear to you?
  2. Is there a “just this one thing” you've been tempted to add to simple faith in Christ — for yourself, or for someone you're discipling?
  3. “The ground is level at the cross.” How does that truth change how you view people you consider more — or less — spiritual than yourself?

Lord, thank You for one gospel, freely given, with nothing left for me to add.

PropositionPaul's gospel and apostleship were independent of, and never subordinate to, the churches and leaders in Jerusalem — a fact confirmed not by Paul's own claim alone, but by the Judean churches' own testimony and the Jerusalem apostles' own recognition.

Introduction: Five Visits to Jerusalem

The text today has to do with five times in which Paul went to Jerusalem. Now, we know that not only from Galatians, but also from the book of Acts, which we studied prior to this study. So the book of Acts gives us five Jerusalem visits. They are, first of all, the visit after he left Damascus. And we see that recorded in Acts 9, 26-30, but also in Galatians 1, 18-20, which is part of our text today. The second visit is the famine visit in Acts 11, 27-30, and then in Galatians 2, 1-10, which we'll cover today as well. And then he visited after that. He visited for the Jerusalem Council in Acts 15. We read about that. That's the longest record that we have of his time in Jerusalem. And then he visited, at the end of his second missionary journey, he went up and just greeted the church, then went back up to, down to Antioch. And then finally, he went to Jerusalem and it was there that he was taken into custody. Remember, in the temple, they took him into custody. So they also recognized the power in both Peter and Paul was not their own power. Look what it says. For he who worked effectively in Peter for the apostleship to the circumcised also worked effectively toward the Gentiles, in me. So the same power that worked in Peter was the same power working in Paul. It's one church. It's one salvation. It's one gospel, no matter from whom it comes, so that one power. It's the power of God. Now, remember what Paul said in 1 Corinthians where he says, I planted, and Apollos watered. Oh, isn't that wonderful? Big Paul, big Apollos. Then the next verse he says, And all the while, God was giving the increase. See? That's it. Apart from the power of God, see, the Gospel is the power of God to salvation. It isn't me. It isn't you. It isn't this church or that church. It's the power of God working through the Gospel as we present it to someone.

d. They Gave Paul the Right Hand of Fellowship — 2:9

He says, When James and Peter and John, who seemed to be pillars, perceived the grace that had been given to me, they gave me and Barnabas the right hand of fellowship. That's a great thing. You're one of us and we're together. We'll do this thing together. And that they should go to the Gentiles and Peter should go to the circumcised. They go separate ways, to separate people, but with the same gospel. That's what he's saying.

e. The Apostles Requested That They Should Remember the Poor — 2:10

Then the apostles requested that they should remember the poor. This is almost laughable, you know. What had Paul come there for? To bring money for the poor. Oh, Paul, remember the poor. Oh, duh. I just did it. But Paul said, I'm used to doing that, of course. So, one thing to leave out. He says, remember the poor. But there's nothing here about things strangled and things and blood. In Jerusalem Council, at the end of that, in the letter, they said, You Gentiles, remember the poor, but also stay away from things strangled and away from blood, drinking blood, ceremonially, you know, false religions. That's not here. That's why I say this is not the Jerusalem Council he's talking about.

Conclusion

Now, the key teaching here is this. There is one Gospel. Now, I know, why am I laboring it here? Well, because we need to be reminded of it. There is one Gospel and it's all of grace, all of God. The ground is level at the cross. Have you ever heard anybody say that? I think Billy Graham said it first, to my knowledge. At the cross, when you come to the cross, it isn't oh, the Pharisees are up here on this high plain, you know. They're right close to God. No, the ground is level. And when you come, you stand on the same level I do. And I stand on the same level as everybody else, you know, and you do too. The ground is level. And no matter who you are, no matter whom you approach, you can say this to them, you only have to do one thing. They leave on the Lord Jesus Christ and you'll be saved. This is a great passage for that. We're going to learn more about that in this book.

Part 2 — Study Questions

1. Why does Paul take a “solemn oath” (v. 20) about his account of visiting Jerusalem, and why does it matter for dating the events of Galatians 2?

Show Leader Answer

Paul is swearing that his account of every Jerusalem visit is complete. That completeness is what allows us to identify the visit in Galatians 2 as the famine visit of Acts 11, not the Jerusalem Council of Acts 15 — if the Council had already happened, Paul would surely have cited its decision.

2. Paul says he went to Jerusalem “by revelation” (2:2) and discussed his gospel almost as an aside (“and communicated…”). What does this order of events protect?

Show Leader Answer

It protects the fact that Paul's gospel didn't originate with, or depend on, Jerusalem's approval. He went for famine relief; discussing his gospel was incidental to that trip, not the reason for it — the opposite of what the Judaizers implied.

3. What two-fold purpose did the false brothers have in Galatians 2:4, and how does καταδουλόω (“to enslave”) describe the stakes?

Show Leader Answer

They wanted to spy out believers' liberty in Christ and then bring them into bondage by requiring circumcision. καταδουλόω means to reduce completely to slavery — showing that adding one requirement to grace doesn't improve the gospel, it enslaves those who receive it.

4. Why does Paul say “those who seemed to be something added nothing to me” (2:6), and what does that phrase protect against?

Show Leader Answer

It confirms the Jerusalem apostles didn't supplement, correct, or improve Paul's gospel in any way — protecting against any claim that Paul's message was really Jerusalem's message in disguise, or that it needed apostolic amendment to be complete.

5. The apostles asked Paul to “remember the poor” (2:10) — the one request they made. Why is it significant that this request was practical rather than doctrinal?

Show Leader Answer

It shows the only thing “added” to Paul's gospel mission was a call to compassion, not a new doctrinal requirement — confirming that the gospel itself remained untouched, by grace through faith alone, while still expecting genuine care for others as its fruit.

⑤ Application

Personal and corporate response — draws from all sections above

A Gospel That Answers to God, Not to Committees

Galatians 1:18-24

Source: Commentary §I; Devotional

Paul's early ministry unfolded largely apart from, and unknown to, the very churches some assumed had authorized him. Our confidence in the gospel does not depend on human certification either — it rests on what God has revealed and already validated in changed lives.

Reflection: Where do you look for validation of your faith — to human approval, or to what God has already done and said?

Practical Step: This week, thank God specifically for one piece of evidence of His grace in your life that no one else gave you credit for.

Bring the Relief, Discuss the Gospel Along the Way

Galatians 2:1-2

Source: Commentary §II.A-B

Paul's trip to Jerusalem was first an act of practical compassion — famine relief for suffering believers — and only secondarily a theological conversation. Grace-shaped ministry often looks like showing up to serve first, with gospel clarity following naturally out of relationship.

Reflection: Is there a person or need in front of you right now that simply needs practical care before it needs a theological conversation?

Practical Step: Identify one practical need you can meet this week for someone in your church family, without waiting for a “spiritual” reason to do it.

Not Yielding, Not Even for an Hour

Galatians 2:3-6

Source: Commentary §II.C; Self-Study Guide (καταδουλόω, ἐλευθερία)

When pressure came to add just one requirement to faith, Paul didn't negotiate, delay, or compromise “for an hour.” The truth of the gospel — grace alone, through faith alone — was worth that firmness.

Reflection: Where might you be tempted to soften or qualify the simplicity of “believe and you will be saved,” even with good intentions?

Practical Step: Practice stating the gospel this week in one sentence, with nothing added to “believe.”

The Ground Is Level at the Cross

Galatians 2:7-10

Source: Commentary §II.C; FGR.docx — Charles C. Ryrie, So Great Salvation: What It Means to Believe in Jesus Christ (Wheaton: Victor Books, 1989)

Peter and Paul went to different peoples with the same gospel, under the same grace, empowered by the same God — proof that no status, background, or calling changes the terms of salvation. Ryrie's classic Free Grace study makes the same case for today's reader: the gospel is one, and it is level ground for everyone who comes to it.

Reflection: Is there anyone you unconsciously treat as needing a “different,” harder path to God than the one you walked?

Practical Step: Identify one person you've mentally placed on different spiritual footing than yourself, and pray this week that you'd see them as standing on the same level ground at the cross.
