Galatians · Study 14 · Leader / Learner Guide

Galatians 6:1–5 — Leader / Learner Guide

Brothers and Burdens · a one-page guide for leaders and learners

TheGospelVault.com  ·  @iJohn.316 (Instagram)

Download PDF Full Study 14

Big Idea and Context

Big idea: Spirit-led believers gently restore a brother caught in sin and help carry what is too heavy for him. Each believer still carries his own load and will give account for it before the Lord.

Chapter 6 puts walking in the Spirit (5:16–26) to work in the church body. Liberty now shows up as care for one another.

  • 5:13–26: walk in the Spirit, not the flesh.
  • 6:1–5: restore the fallen; carry burdens and your own load.
  • 6:6–10: sow to the Spirit; do good to all.

Burdens and Loads — Three Anchor Verses

VerseWhat it saysTeaching point
6:1You who are spiritual restore such a one in a spirit of gentleness"Restore" means setting a bone or mending a net — bringing a believer back to usefulness.
6:2Bear one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of ChristBaros: a weight too heavy to carry alone. The law of Christ is love (John 13:34; 14:15).
6:5Each one shall bear his own loadPhortion: a soldier's pack — what each believer carries and will answer for at the judgment seat.

Believers still sin: James 3:2; 1 John 1:8–10. The remedy is confession, not penance.

Examine your work, not your salvation: assurance rests on God's promise. Self-examination is about service and reward (1 Cor 3; 2 Cor 5:10).

Teaching Flow

  1. Bear One Another's Burdens (1–3). The sinning believer, overtaken by a trespass; the spiritual brother — one walking in the Spirit — goes to him, not to others; the goal is restoration; the posture is gentleness, watching yourself (1 Cor 10:12–13). Don't think yourself something.
  2. Bear Your Own Burden (4–5). Examine your own work, not someone else's; rejoice in finishing your own course (2 Tim 4:7); each one carries his own load to the judgment seat.

Clarify While Teaching

  • The body passage: the hand, foot, and eye are in 1 Corinthians 12:14–25. Ephesians 4:16 and Colossians call the church a body too.
  • "Yield Not to Temptation": Horatio R. Palmer wrote this hymn (1868), not Fanny Crosby.
  • Healed bones: the idea that a healed break can't break again in the same place is a common belief, not a medical fact.
  • "Caught" (6:1): Sellers takes this as overtaken by sin, not discovered in the act. Both are possible readings.
  • Prayer requests: his warning about gossip disguised as prayer is about going to the person first, not about never sharing a need.

Discussion and Close

  1. When a fellow believer falls, what is your first instinct — to tell others, or to go help restore him?
  2. What makes it hard to restore someone "in a spirit of gentleness"?
  3. If you examined your own work this week, what would you see? Are you using the gifts God gave you?

Close: Point ahead to 6:6–10: God is not mocked — whatever a man sows, that he will also reap. The Christian army should not shoot its wounded.
