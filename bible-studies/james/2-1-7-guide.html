James · Study 07 · Leader / Learner Guide

James 2:1–7 — Leader / Learner Guide

Favorites in the Pew · a one-page guide for leaders and learners

James: Living Wisely Under Pressure  ·  TheGospelVault.com  ·  @iJohn.316 (Instagram)

Full Study 07

Big Idea and Context

Big idea: Believers who trust in the glorious Lord Jesus Christ must not show favoritism, because it contradicts His glory, dishonors those God chose to be rich in faith and heirs of His kingdom, and honors those who oppose Him.

After pure religion (1:27), James exposes favoritism in the worship assembly. It is part of being “quick to hear”: doing the Word in how we treat people.

  • Forbidden (2:1): faith in the Lord of glory cannot coexist with judging by the face.
  • Illustrated (2:2–4): the gold-fingered man gets the seat; the poor man gets the floor.
  • Inconsistent (2:5–7): rejecting those God approved; approving those who oppose God.

No Favorites — Three Anchor Verses

VerseWhat it saysTeaching point
James 2:1“Do not hold your faith in our glorious Lord … with … favoritism.”Human glory fades next to the Lord of glory.
James 2:5“Rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom.”Inheriting means sharing Christ’s rule, promised to those who love Him.
2 Corinthians 8:9“Though He was rich, yet for your sake He became poor.”Our Lord’s humility rebukes our favoritism.

Same verb, two settings: diakrinō is “doubting” in 1:6 and “made distinctions” in 2:4.

Entering vs. inheriting: every believer enters the kingdom by faith; heirs who reign are those who love Him (Romans 8:17; 2 Timothy 2:12).

Teaching Flow

  1. Introduction. From pure religion (1:27) to favoritism in worship.
  2. Favoritism identified. Definition; its many faces (appearance, age, affluence, ancestry…).
  3. Forbidden (2:1). Your faith; the Lord of glory; “receiving the face”; He became poor.
  4. Illustrated (2:2–4). The synagogue scene; the reception; divided judgment and evil motives.
  5. Inconsistent (2:5–7). Chosen to be rich in faith and heirs; oppressors honored; what James is not saying.

Clarify While Teaching

  • Not salvation by poverty: God chose the poor to be rich in faith and heirs, not saved because poor.
  • No reverse discrimination: Leviticus 19:15 forbids partiality both ways.
  • The “Lincoln” saying in the outline (“God must love the poor…”) is not used; its source is uncertain.
  • “Called upon you” (2:7) may refer to the Lord’s name spoken at baptism; the text does not say so directly.

Discussion and Close

  1. Which kind of favoritism is most common in churches today?
  2. How does the glory of Christ cure favoritism?
  3. What could our church do to welcome everyone equally?

Close: Favoritism breaks God’s royal law of love. Study 08 (James 2:8–13) shows why, and why mercy triumphs over judgment.
