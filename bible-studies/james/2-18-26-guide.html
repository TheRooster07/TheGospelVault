James · Study 10 · Leader / Learner Guide

James 2:18–26 — Leader / Learner Guide

The Objector, the Patriarch, and the Harlot · a one-page guide for leaders and learners

James: Living Wisely Under Pressure  ·  TheGospelVault.com  ·  @iJohn.316 (Instagram)

Full Study 10

Big Idea and Context

Big idea: James refutes the claim that faith and works are unrelated: Abraham and Rahab, already right with God by faith, were justified before others by works that matured their faith and made it useful; faith without works is as useless as a body without the spirit.

After the thesis (2:14–17), an objector says faith and works have no connection (2:18–19). James replies (2:20) and proves his point from Abraham (2:21–24) and Rahab (2:25), closing with the body-and-spirit picture (2:26).

  • The objection (2:18–19): neither can show the other; demons believe and only shudder.
  • Abraham (2:21–24): justified by faith before God (Genesis 15), by works before men decades later (Genesis 22); called God’s friend.
  • Rahab (2:25–26): faith put to work saved her life; idle faith is a lifeless body.

Two Justifications — Three Anchor Verses

VerseWhat it saysTeaching point
Genesis 15:6“He believed in the Lord; and He reckoned it to him as righteousness.”Justified before God by faith alone.
James 2:21“Justified by works when he offered up Isaac.”Shown righteous before others, decades later.
James 2:23“He was called the friend of God.”Friendship is the reward of obedience (John 15:14).

“Alone” is an adverb (2:24): “justified by works, and not only justified by faith.”

“Useless” (argē) in 2:20: idle, workless, a wordplay on “works.”

Teaching Flow

  1. Introduction. The thesis under fire; modern versions of the objection.
  2. The objection (2:18–19). The teaching device; where it ends; the demons and what they do not prove.
  3. James’s reply (2:20). “Foolish fellow”; faith without works is idle.
  4. Abraham (2:21–24). Genesis 15 and 22; justified before men; faith perfected; friend of God; two justifications.
  5. Rahab and the body (2:25–26). Faith, works, deliverance; a body without the spirit.

Clarify While Teaching

  • Isaac’s birth is Genesis 21:5 (the outline has Genesis 20); Ishmael was born when Abraham was 86 (16:16), not 85.
  • Abraham’s age at Moriah (the outline: 125, Isaac 25) is not stated in Scripture; it is an estimate.
  • Hebrews 11:17–19 (the outline has “11:17–10”); Rahab’s faith is Joshua 2:11.
  • Where the objection ends is debated; this study follows Hodges (2:18–19).

Discussion and Close

  1. Why does it matter that 2:19 is the objector’s argument?
  2. What is the difference between a child of God and a friend of God?
  3. Where is God asking you to put your faith to work?

Close: Faith at work shows up first in our words. Study 11 (James 3:1–12) turns to the tongue: bits, rudders and sparks.
