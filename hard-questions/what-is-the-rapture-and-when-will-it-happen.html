What Is the Rapture, and When Will It Happen?

Translation: NKJV · Free Grace / Dispensational · Source: The Gospel Vault

In Brief

Christ will come for His church before the Tribulation, raising believers who have died and transforming the living in a moment. It can happen at any time.

Key verses1 Thessalonians 4:16–17 · 1 Corinthians 15:51–52 · 1 Thessalonians 5:9

The Rapture refers to the moment Christ returns to remove His church from the earth prior to the Tribulation, described most fully by Paul: “The Lord Himself will descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of an archangel, and with the trumpet of God. And the dead in Christ will rise first. Then we who are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air. And thus we shall always be with the Lord” (1 Thessalonians 4:16–17). Paul calls this a “mystery” — new revelation, not previously disclosed in the Old Testament (1 Corinthians 15:51–52): living believers will be instantly transformed, receiving resurrection bodies without passing through death, “in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet.”

The dispensational and Free Grace tradition holds that this event is pretribulational — occurring before the seven-year Tribulation period described in Daniel 9:27 and Revelation 6–19 — distinguishing the Rapture (Christ coming for His church, in the air) from the Second Coming (Christ returning with His church, to the earth, at the end of the Tribulation, described in Revelation 19:11–16). Paul’s promise to the Thessalonian church that “God did not appoint us to wrath, but to obtain salvation through our Lord Jesus Christ” (1 Thessalonians 5:9) is read as assurance that the church will be removed before, not merely protected through, the specific outpouring of divine wrath the Tribulation describes (Revelation 3:10, promising the church at Philadelphia deliverance “from the hour of trial which shall come upon the whole world”).

The timing of this event is explicitly, repeatedly described as unknown and imminent — capable of occurring at any moment, with no preceding signs required to be fulfilled first, unlike the Second Coming, which Scripture ties to specific, observable end-time events. “Of that day and hour no one knows” (Matthew 24:36); believers are called to be “waiting for His Son from heaven” (1 Thessalonians 1:10) as a present, active posture rather than a distant, calculable date. This imminence is precisely why the New Testament repeatedly uses the Rapture as a present motivation for holy living and readiness rather than as a subject for date-setting: “everyone who has this hope in Him purifies himself, just as He is pure” (1 John 3:3).

John F. Hart’s exegetical series on Matthew 24:36–44 engages the question of how the Olivet Discourse’s language about being “taken” and “left” relates to this event, working through the case for a pretribulational understanding in detail against alternative readings.1 The differing views held by other Christian traditions on the timing of the Rapture relative to the Tribulation — mid-tribulational, pre-wrath, and post-tribulational positions — represent an ongoing area of disagreement among Bible-believing Christians; this answer states the pretribulational position without attempting to settle every detail of that debate.

Whatever the precise sequence, the pastoral thrust of the doctrine throughout the New Testament is consistent: the Rapture is presented not as a subject for anxious speculation but as “this hope” (1 John 3:3) — an imminent, joy-producing expectation meant to comfort grieving believers (1 Thessalonians 4:18, “comfort one another with these words”) and to motivate holy, watchful living in the present.
