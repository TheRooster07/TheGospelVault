Is Abortion Addressed in Scripture?

Translation: NKJV · Free Grace / Dispensational · Source: The Gospel Vault

In Brief

Scripture treats the unborn child as a person known by God. That grounds the church’s call to protect life — and complete forgiveness is available to anyone carrying a past abortion.

Key versesPsalm 139:13–16 · Jeremiah 1:5 · 1 John 1:9

Scripture does not contain a modern legal or medical term for abortion, but it speaks clearly and repeatedly to the underlying question that matters most: the status and value of unborn human life before God. The consistent testimony of both Testaments is that the unborn child is a person, known and valued by God, not merely potential or partial human tissue awaiting some later threshold of personhood.

David’s reflection on his own prenatal formation is explicit: “You formed my inward parts; You covered me in my mother’s womb ... My frame was not hidden from You, when I was made in secret ... Your eyes saw my substance, being yet unformed” (Psalm 139:13, 15–16). God’s calling of Jeremiah rests on the same conviction: “Before I formed you in the womb I knew you; before you were born I sanctified you” (Jeremiah 1:5) — God’s personal knowledge of Jeremiah predates not only his birth but his very formation in the womb. Luke records that when the pregnant Mary visited the pregnant Elizabeth, “the babe leaped in her womb” (Luke 1:41, 44) — using the same Greek word (brephos) for the unborn John the Baptist that Luke uses elsewhere for the newborn, already-delivered infant Jesus (Luke 2:12, 16), treating both stages of development with the identical vocabulary of personhood.

The broader biblical principle undergirding the sanctity of all human life, born or unborn, is the doctrine of the image of God (see Q29, “What Does It Mean That Humans Are Made in the Image of God?”): every human being, at every stage, bears a likeness to God that grounds the seriousness of taking innocent human life (Genesis 9:6; Exodus 20:13). Because Scripture locates a person’s value in being made in God’s image rather than in any later-acquired capacity — the ability to reason, to survive independently, or to be wanted — that value is present from the earliest stages Scripture describes, not conferred gradually as development proceeds.

This conviction carries real pastoral weight and real pastoral tenderness together. Scripture calls believers to “open your mouth for the speechless, in the cause of all who are appointed to die ... plead the cause of the poor and needy” (Proverbs 31:8–9), language easily and appropriately applied to the most voiceless and vulnerable human beings of all. At the same time, consistent with the Free Grace framework of this entire paper, no past decision — including a past abortion — places a person beyond the reach of grace or outside the possibility of complete forgiveness and settled peace with God; the gospel’s offer of eternal life and its promise that “if we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness” (1 John 1:9) apply here exactly as they apply to every other sin this paper addresses.

Scripture, then, speaks to abortion not by using the modern term but by establishing, with unusual clarity and consistency across both Testaments, that the life in the womb is a real person known to God — a conviction that grounds both the church’s call to protect the vulnerable and its equally urgent call to extend complete grace to anyone carrying the weight of a past decision made in that light.
