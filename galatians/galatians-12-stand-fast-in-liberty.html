Stand Fast in Liberty

Galatians 5:1–12  ·  NASB95

Key Verse

“Stand fast therefore in the liberty by which Christ has made us free, and do not be entangled again with a yoke of bondage.”

Galatians 5:1

Paul’s command is deliberately active: “stand fast” isn’t a passive hope that freedom will simply happen to you — it’s a decision you make and keep making. Christ has already made you free; the liberty is real and settled. But standing in it, refusing to be dragged back into whatever yoke of bondage tempted you before, whether that’s legalistic religion or something else entirely — that takes deliberate resolve. Paul makes the stakes starkly clear: if the Galatians accept circumcision as a requirement for salvation, “Christ will profit you nothing.” Not because their relationship to Christ changes, but because trying to add one requirement means you’re now obligated to keep the entire Law perfectly — an impossible debt no one has ever paid.

What Paul offers instead isn’t just an absence of rules — it’s a completely different way of relating to God altogether. “Neither circumcision nor uncircumcision avails anything, but faith working through love.” Under law, people work out of fear to try to earn what they don’t have. Under grace, people work out of love, because of what’s already been freely given. Paul reminds them they “ran well” once — before someone cut in and diverted them — and he’s confident the Lord will bring them back, while those who troubled them “shall bear his judgment, whoever he is.” That’s worth remembering whenever legalism, in any form, tries to reattach a yoke you were never meant to wear again.

Questions to Carry Into the Passage

  1. Standing fast in liberty is described as something you actively do, not something automatic. What specific yoke of bondage — legalism, performance-based worth, or something else — are you currently being tempted to re-accept?
  2. Paul distinguishes working from fear (law) versus working from love (grace) — same actions, entirely different motive. Which one more honestly describes your own obedience lately?
  3. The Galatians "ran well" until someone cut in on their race. Who or what has "cut in" on your own spiritual progress recently, and what would it look like to get back to running well?
