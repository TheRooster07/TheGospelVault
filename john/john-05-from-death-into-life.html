Chapter 05 of 21

From Death Into Life

John 5:1–47  ·  NASB95

Key Verse

“Most assuredly, I say to you, he who hears My word and believes in Him who sent Me has everlasting life, and shall not come into judgment, but has passed from death into life.”

John 5:24

A man lies by the pool of Bethesda for thirty-eight years, waiting for a chance he can never quite reach in time. Jesus doesn’t wait for the water to stir. He simply asks, “Do you want to be made well?” and then commands, “Rise, take up your bed and walk” — on the Sabbath, deliberately. The healing is instant. The controversy is instant too. When the religious leaders confront Jesus for working on the Sabbath, He answers with a claim that changes the whole conversation: “My Father has been working until now, and I have been working.” Not a father. My Father. They understood exactly what He meant — He was calling Himself equal with God — and they wanted Him dead for it.

What follows is one of the most direct assurance statements in the entire Bible. Jesus says that whoever hears His word and believes has everlasting life, will not come into judgment, and has already passed from death into life — not will pass, has passed. It’s a completed transaction, stated in the past tense about your present standing. The Son has been given authority to give life and to judge, exactly as the Father has life in Himself. And Jesus points to multiple witnesses who confirm who He is — John the Baptist, His own works, the Father Himself, and even Moses, whose writings the religious leaders claimed to trust but refused to actually believe when it came to Him. The chapter ends with a haunting line: “If you believed Moses, you would believe Me; for he wrote about Me.” They had the Scriptures memorized and still missed the One the Scriptures were about.

Questions to Carry Into the Chapter

  1. Verse 24 says the believer “has passed” from death to life — already, completely, past tense. Does that match how settled your own assurance feels, or does some part of you still feel like you’re waiting to cross that line?
  2. The man at the pool had been disabled for 38 years and still couldn’t get to the water in time on his own. Jesus didn’t improve his odds — He simply healed him. Where in your life are you still trying to improve your odds instead of simply receiving what only He can give?
  3. The religious leaders knew the Scriptures thoroughly and still missed Jesus, the very One those Scriptures pointed to. What would it look like for you to read Scripture this week specifically looking for Him, rather than just for information?
