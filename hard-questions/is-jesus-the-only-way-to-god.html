50 Hard Questions · Question 15 of 50

Is Jesus the Only Way to God?

Translation: NKJV · Free Grace / Dispensational · Source: The Gospel Vault

In Brief

Jesus claimed to be the only way to the Father, and only He, as God incarnate, could bear sin’s penalty and rise again. There has only ever been one way: grace received through faith.

Key versesJohn 14:6 · Acts 4:12 · John 3:16

In a religiously plural culture, the exclusive claims of Christianity are treated as its most embarrassing feature. But the exclusivity is Jesus’ own claim, stated as plainly as language allows: “I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through Me” (John 14:6). The apostles sharpened it further: “there is no other name under heaven given among men by which we must be saved” (Acts 4:12).

This follows directly from who Jesus is and what He did. If sin separates humanity from a holy God (Isaiah 59:2), and if that separation can only be resolved by a sinless substitute bearing sin’s penalty (2 Corinthians 5:21), then the question is not whether other paths might also work, but whether any other path meets those requirements at all. No other claimed savior has claimed to be God incarnate, lived sinlessly, died as a substitute, and been vindicated by a bodily resurrection witnessed by hundreds (1 Corinthians 15:3–8).

What about those who never hear the gospel? Scripture’s answer — that God holds every person accountable for the light of creation and conscience (Romans 1:20) and takes extraordinary steps to bring the gospel to those who seek Him (Acts 10) — is taken up in Q16, “What Happens to Those Who Never Hear About Jesus?”.

The dispensational reading adds a clarifying dimension: Old Testament believers were saved by faith in the God who had revealed Himself, looking forward to a redemption not yet accomplished, just as New Testament believers look back to one already accomplished. Abraham “believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness” (Genesis 15:6) centuries before the cross existed in history. There has only ever been one way — grace received through faith in what God has revealed about His provided Redeemer — administered differently as more of that revelation was given across the ages.1 What saving faith actually requires is simpler than many assume: not sincerity in general, but believing what Jesus promises — that whoever trusts Him receives everlasting life as a gift (John 3:16; 5:24).

None of this diminishes the discomfort the claim produces in a pluralistic setting. But the alternative — that many equally valid roads lead to God — requires denying either that sin truly separates humanity from God, or that any remedy was ever truly necessary. Jesus did not claim to be one signpost among several; He claimed, and demonstrated by His resurrection, to be God arrived among us, which is exactly why no substitute could be offered in His place.
