50 Hard Questions · Question 20 of 50

Can a Genuine Christian Lose Their Salvation?

Translation: NKJV · Free Grace / Dispensational · Source: The Gospel Vault

In Brief

No. Eternal life is a gift, sealed by the Holy Spirit and kept by Christ. A believer’s sin brings discipline and loss of reward, never loss of eternal life.

Key versesJohn 10:28–29 · Ephesians 1:13–14 · 2 Timothy 2:13

The Free Grace answer is unambiguous: no. Once a person has believed in Jesus Christ for eternal life, that life is permanently secure — not because the believer holds on tightly enough, but because it was never the believer’s grip that secured it. “I give them eternal life, and they shall never perish; neither shall anyone snatch them out of My hand” (John 10:27–28). Paul’s confidence in Romans 8 runs the same direction: nothing, “nor things present nor things to come ... shall be able to separate us from the love of God which is in Christ Jesus our Lord” (Romans 8:38–39) — a list that would need to include the believer’s own future failures to be anything less than exhaustive.

Ephesians grounds security in a completed transaction, not ongoing performance: believers are “sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise, who is the guarantee of our inheritance” (Ephesians 1:13–14) — sealed at the moment of belief, not progressively as a reward for endurance. Joseph Dillow’s exhaustive study, The Reign of the Servant Kings, works through nearly every relevant New Testament passage to show that warnings against apostasy concern a believer’s present fellowship and future reward, not the retroactive loss of eternal life already granted.1 Zane Hodges made the same case in Grace in Eclipse: Scripture’s warnings about “loss” most often concern lost reward and inheritance-rank in the coming kingdom — real and sobering, but categorically different from losing eternal life itself.2

This resolves what otherwise looks like an unbearable tension in the warning passages. Hebrews 6:4–6 describes those who “fall away” as impossible to renew again to repentance — alarming until read, as the writer himself frames it just afterward, as an agricultural warning about unproductive land “near to being cursed” (Hebrews 6:7–8): a warning about wasted potential and lost reward for genuine believers, not a claim that true believers can be un-saved.3 John Hart’s study of Philippians 1:6 similarly argues Paul’s confidence describes God’s commitment to His own saving work, not a guarantee of uniform visible sanctification that would call salvation into question if absent.4

What of a believer who falls into serious sin, or even stops believing for a season? Scripture is sobering in a different direction: real consequences follow — loss of present fellowship (1 John 1:6), loss of reward at the Judgment Seat of Christ (1 Corinthians 3:15, “he himself will be saved, yet so as through fire”), even severe fatherly discipline (1 Corinthians 11:30). What such a person does not face is the reversal of a gift God does not repossess, because it was never conditioned on later behavior. “If we are faithless, He remains faithful; He cannot deny Himself” (2 Timothy 2:13).

This doctrine is not license for careless living — Paul firmly rejected that misreading (Romans 6:1–2) — but it is the necessary foundation for genuine assurance. A gift that could be lost through failure was never, by definition, a gift. Scripture calls eternal life a gift precisely because it cannot be repossessed (Romans 6:23), grounded not in the believer’s continuing worthiness but in the unchanging character of the One who gave it.
