James · Study 16 · Leader / Learner Guide

James 5:19–20 — Leader / Learner Guide

Bring Them Back Alive · a one-page guide for leaders and learners

James: Living Wisely Under Pressure  ·  TheGospelVault.com  ·  @iJohn.316 (Instagram)

Full Study 16

Big Idea and Context

Big idea: Any believer can stray from the truth, and any believer can turn him back; the one who does saves the wanderer’s life from the premature death sin brings and covers a multitude of his sins.

James ends his letter without greetings, with a call to rescue the brother who strays (5:19–20). It completes his theme of saving the life (1:21; 2:14; 4:12; 5:15).

  • The wanderer (5:19a): a brother who strays from the truth, doctrinally or practically.
  • The restorer (5:19b): anyone who turns him back, gently and humbly.
  • The rescue (5:20): his life saved from death; a multitude of sins covered.

Restoring the Wanderer — Three Anchor Verses

VerseWhat it saysTeaching point
James 5:19“If any among you strays from the truth and one turns him back.”Anyone can wander; anyone can help.
Galatians 6:1“Restore such a one in a spirit of gentleness.”The way to go after a brother.
James 5:20“Will save his soul from death.”Physical life spared, not eternal life regained.

“Soul” (psychē): often physical life or the person (Acts 20:10).

“Strays” (planaō): the root of “planet”; wandering stars.

Teaching Flow

  1. Introduction. The last word: a rescue mission; saving the life in James.
  2. The wanderer (5:19a). A brother; strays; from the truth; doctrinal and practical wandering.
  3. The restorer (5:19b). Anyone; why it is hard; gently, humbly, privately; the shepherd’s heart.
  4. The rescue (5:20). Meanings of save and soul; physical death; love covers sins.
  5. Review and close. The road through James; “anyone.”

Clarify While Teaching

  • Not eternal death: the wanderer is a believer with eternal life (John 5:24; 10:28).
  • “Sin leading to death” is 1 John 5:16–17 (the outline has “5:6, 17”).
  • “Anyone” (tis) appears twice in 5:19 (the outline says verse 18).
  • Whose sins are covered: most naturally the wanderer’s; some apply it to the restorer.

Discussion and Close

  1. Who comes to mind when you read James 5:19?
  2. What would it take for you to go to that person gently this week?
  3. Looking back over all sixteen studies, what has James changed in you?

Close: James began with joy in trials and ends with love that goes after the wanderer. Faith that works receives the Word, bridles the tongue, waits patiently, prays, and brings brothers home.
