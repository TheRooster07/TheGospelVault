Declaring the End from the Beginning · Study 3 · Leader / Learner Guide

Jeremiah 31:35–37 — Leader / Learner Guide

The Nation That Would Not Disappear · a one-page guide for leaders and learners

TheGospelVault.com  ·  @iJohn.316 (Instagram)

Download PDF Full Study 3

Big Idea and Context

Big idea: Moses foretold Israel’s scattering, the prophets its preservation and regathering — all fulfilled — and Israel’s Scriptures point to the Messiah. The God who keeps faith with Israel keeps faith with every believer.

The covenant warned of worldwide exile (Leviticus 26; Deuteronomy 28) while promising God would not destroy Israel. Jeremiah ties that promise to the sun, moon and stars.

  • Scattered: Assyria (722 BC), Babylon (586 BC), Rome (AD 70, 135) — nearly two thousand years of dispersion.
  • Preserved: conquerors vanished; Israel remained a distinct people (Jeremiah 30:11; Romans 11:1–2).
  • Regathered: a worldwide “second” gathering (Isaiah 11:11–12); the State of Israel, 1948.

God’s Faithfulness to Israel — Three Anchor Verses

VerseWhat it saysTeaching point
Deuteronomy 28:64The LORD will scatter you among all peoples.Foretold before Israel entered the land.
Jeremiah 31:35–37If the fixed order departs, Israel will cease; it will not.Preservation rests on God’s faithfulness, “for all that they have done.”
Ezekiel 37:21–22I will gather them … and make them one nation in the land.In stages: bones before breath (37:8–10).

Hosea 3:4–5: “many days” without king or sacrifice — and without idols — then a return: Jewish life since AD 70.

Luke 24:44: the Law, the Prophets and the Psalms all point to Jesus; summary table of six strands in ③ IV.B.

Teaching Flow

  1. Scattered as Moses said (Leviticus 26:33; Deuteronomy 28:64–65; Hosea 3:4–5).
  2. Preserved as God promised (Leviticus 26:44; Jeremiah 30:11; 31:35–37; Genesis 15).
  3. Regathered as the prophets said (Isaiah 11:11–12; Ezekiel 36–37; Amos 9:15) — a stage, not the end.
  4. The Messiah at the center (Luke 24:27, 44; the six-strand table).
  5. If God keeps faith with Israel, He keeps faith with you (Romans 11:29; John 6:47).

Clarify While Teaching

  • 1948 is a stage: dispensationalists see a return largely in unbelief, awaiting Zechariah 12:10 and Romans 11:26.
  • No politics as gospel: fulfilled prophecy does not put any modern government beyond criticism.
  • Reformed counterpoint: covenant theology often sees promises to Israel fulfilled in the church; this study holds Israel and the church distinct (Romans 11).
  • Speak with sorrow: dispersion and persecution, including the Holocaust, are tragedies — never triumphs.

Discussion and Close

  • Why is Israel’s survival as a distinct people such a striking fulfillment?
  • How can we speak about modern Israel without overclaiming?
  • How does Romans 11:29 strengthen your assurance of eternal life?
