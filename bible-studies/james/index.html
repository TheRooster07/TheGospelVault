Vault 3 · New Testament

James

James: Living Wisely Under Pressure — a small group Bible study series through the letter of James, the Lord’s brother, on a faith that holds steady in trials and shows itself in how we hear, speak, and love. Each study includes the biblical text, a devotional, a full commentary, a self-study guide with Greek word study, and personal application. Ten of sixteen studies are posted so far; more are on the way.

Studies 1–10 of 16

Choose a Passage

  1. 01The Brother Who Became a ServantJames 1:1James, once an unbeliever in Jesus’ own household, writes as a servant of the risen Lord to Jewish believers scattered by persecution, and his one-verse greeting sets the tone for a letter about living wisely when life is hard.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  2. 02Joy in the FurnaceJames 1:2–4Because God uses trials to prove our faith and build endurance, believers can meet every hardship with a settled joy, letting Him finish the work of making them mature, complete, and lacking nothing.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  3. 03Wisdom from Above, Wealth from BelowJames 1:5–11When trials reveal what we lack, God invites us to ask Him for wisdom with an undivided heart, and that wisdom teaches rich and poor alike to measure life by what lasts forever.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  4. 04The Crown, the Trap, and the Good GiverJames 1:12–18Believers who endure trials will be approved and rewarded with the crown of life, but they must never blame God for temptation, which springs from their own desires, because God is the unchanging Giver of every good gift, as their new birth proves.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  5. 05The Word That Saves a LifeJames 1:19–21Believers who were born by the Word must keep receiving it, with a listening, humble and peaceful heart, putting away moral filth, because the implanted Word is able to rescue their lives from the deadly consequences of sin.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  6. 06Mirror, Mirror: Hearing That Becomes DoingJames 1:22–27Hearing God’s Word is where growth begins but not where it ends; the believer who looks intently into the Word and does it will be blessed, and his religion will be seen in a controlled tongue, care for the helpless, and a life unstained by the world.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  7. 07Favorites in the PewJames 2:1–7Believers who trust in the glorious Lord Jesus Christ must not show favoritism, because judging by outward appearance contradicts His glory, dishonors those God has chosen to be rich in faith and heirs of His kingdom, and honors those who oppose Him.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  8. 08The Royal Law and the Triumph of MercyJames 2:8–13Believers fulfill the King’s royal law when they love their neighbor; favoritism breaks that law as truly as any other sin, and since believers will give an account at the Judgment Seat of Christ, they should speak and act with mercy, because mercy triumphs over judgment.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  9. 09What Good Is Faith That Does Nothing?James 2:14–17James does not teach that works are needed to receive or prove eternal life, which is a free gift received by faith alone; he teaches that a believer’s faith without works is useless, “dead,” unable to rescue a needy brother or the believer’s own life from sin’s ruin.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  10. 10The Objector, the Patriarch, and the HarlotJames 2:18–26James answers the objection that faith and works have no connection by showing, from Abraham and Rahab, that believers already declared righteous by faith are justified before others by works, which bring their faith to maturity and make it useful; faith without works is as useless as a body without the spirit.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
