James · Study 03 · Leader / Learner Guide

James 1:5–11 — Leader / Learner Guide

Wisdom from Above, Wealth from Below · a one-page guide for leaders and learners

James: Living Wisely Under Pressure  ·  TheGospelVault.com  ·  @iJohn.316 (Instagram)

Full Study 03

Big Idea and Context

Big idea: When trials reveal what we lack, God invites us to ask Him for wisdom with an undivided heart, and that wisdom teaches rich and poor alike to measure life by what lasts forever.

Trials make us “lacking in nothing” (1:4), but in the middle of them we find we lack wisdom (1:5). James adds two keys for trials: believing prayer (1:5–8) and an eternal perspective (1:9–11).

  • Ask: God gives to all, generously and without reproach (1:5).
  • Trust: ask in faith, not double-minded, or you will be tossed like the surf (1:6–8).
  • Look up: the poor boast in their position in Christ; the rich in their humbling, for wealth fades (1:9–11).

Wisdom and Perspective — Three Anchor Verses

VerseWhat it saysTeaching point
James 1:5“Let him ask of God, who gives to all generously and without reproach.”God gives single-heartedly and never scolds us for asking.
James 1:8“A double-minded man, unstable in all his ways.”Divided prayer leads to an unsettled life.
Isaiah 40:8“The grass withers … but the word of our God stands forever.”Wealth fades; what God says lasts.

Generously vs. double-minded: haplōs (single-heartedly) in 1:5 contrasts with dipsychos (two-souled) in 1:8.

Not about salvation: “He will not receive anything” (1:7) concerns answered prayer for believers, not eternal life.

Teaching Flow

  1. Introduction. From “lacking nothing” (1:4) to “lacks wisdom” (1:5); two new keys for trials.
  2. Pray for wisdom (1:5). The need, the command to keep asking, the request, the Giver, the promise (Solomon; Christ our wisdom).
  3. Ask with one heart (1:6–8). Faith vs. doubting; the surf; the double-minded man (Peter on the water).
  4. The eternal perspective (1:9–11). God’s left-handed threads; the poor brother’s high position; the rich brother’s humbling; the fading flower.
  5. Wisdom and wealth together. Both poverty and riches are trials; store treasure in heaven; contentment (Philippians 4:12–13).

Clarify While Teaching

  • The rich man of 1:10: some take him as an unbeliever (cf. 5:1–6); this study reads “brother” as carrying over from 1:9.
  • James is not against wealth. He warns against trusting it (1 Timothy 6:17).
  • Faith in 1:6 is confidence in God’s promise to answer, not the faith by which we receive eternal life.
  • “God’s delays are not denials”: persistence (present tense) does not mean God is reluctant.

Discussion and Close

  1. Why should we go to God first when we lack wisdom?
  2. What does praying with a “plan B” look like for you?
  3. How does the fading flower change the way you think about money?

Close: Faith asks; hope looks ahead. Study 04 (James 1:12–18) shows the reward of endurance, the source of temptation, and the goodness of the God who gives every perfect gift.
