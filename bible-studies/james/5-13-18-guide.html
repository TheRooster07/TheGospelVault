James · Study 15 · Leader / Learner Guide

James 5:13–18 — Leader / Learner Guide

Prayer for Every Season · a one-page guide for leaders and learners

James: Living Wisely Under Pressure  ·  TheGospelVault.com  ·  @iJohn.316 (Instagram)

Full Study 15

Big Idea and Context

Big idea: James calls believers to pray in every season: personally in suffering and joy, pastorally through the elders for the sick, and congregationally by confessing to and praying for one another, because the earnest prayer of a righteous person accomplishes much, as Elijah proved.

After calling suffering believers to patience (5:7–12), James tells them what to do while they wait: pray (5:13–18).

  • Personal (5:13): suffering? pray; cheerful? sing.
  • Pastoral (5:14–15): the sick call the elders, who pray and anoint; the Lord raises him up.
  • Congregational (5:16–18): confess and pray for one another; Elijah’s example.

Prayer in Every Season — Three Anchor Verses

VerseWhat it saysTeaching point
James 5:13“Is anyone among you suffering? Then he must pray.”Prayer is the first resource, not the last resort.
James 5:15“The prayer offered in faith will restore the one who is sick.”God can heal; the elders pray in faith.
James 5:16“The effective prayer of a righteous man can accomplish much.”Earnest prayer from a life in fellowship.

“Restore” (sōzō, 5:15): physical restoration, as in Matthew 9:22.

“Like ours” (homoiopathēs, 5:17): Elijah was an ordinary man (1 Kings 19:3–4).

Teaching Flow

  1. Introduction. Three circles of prayer: personal, pastoral, congregational.
  2. Personal prayer (5:13). Why and what to pray; singing in joy.
  3. Healing (5:14–15). Jesus and the apostles; God can heal but does not always; the elders’ pattern; sickness and sin.
  4. One another (5:16). Confession, intercession, healing; the prayer of a righteous man.
  5. Elijah (5:17–18). A man like us; no rain, then rain.

Clarify While Teaching

  • Not all sickness comes from sin (John 9:3), and lack of healing does not prove lack of faith.
  • Gifts of healing existed (1 Corinthians 12:9), yet Paul left Trophimus sick (2 Timothy 4:20).
  • Elijah did fear: he ran for his life (1 Kings 19:3); the outline’s “he had no fears” is corrected.
  • Confession is to God and to those wronged, not to a priest (1 Timothy 2:5).

Discussion and Close

  1. In which season of 5:13–16 are you right now, and how should you pray?
  2. How can our church better carry out James 5:14–16?
  3. What does Elijah teach you about your own prayers?

Close: Prayer for one another leads to the last word of the letter. Study 16 (James 5:19–20) calls us to go after the brother who wanders and bring him back alive.
