Grace and Peace from the One Who Gave Himself

Galatians 1:1–5  ·  NASB95

Key Verse

“Who gave Himself for our sins, that He might deliver us from this present evil age, according to the will of our God and Father, to whom be glory forever and ever. Amen.”

Galatians 1:4–5

“Grace to you” is not a wish — it is Paul’s reminder that everything good in your life with God begins as a gift, and stays a gift.

Before Paul says one more word to the Galatians, he has to settle a question: who sent him? The Judaizers had spent months quietly chipping away at his authority, because they could not out-argue his message. So Paul opens with a parenthesis that is really the whole book in miniature — his apostleship, like your salvation, did not come from any man. It came “through Jesus Christ and God the Father who raised Him from the dead.” Before you can rest in what Paul teaches, rest first in the fact that his message carries the authority of the risen Christ Himself.

Then comes the greeting every one of Paul’s letters opens with — grace and peace. It would be easy to hear “grace” and think only of the moment you first believed. But Paul is not writing to unbelievers here; he is writing to Christians who already have eternal life and are being tempted to think they now need the law to finish what grace started. His greeting quietly answers them before the argument even begins: grace is not just how you got in, it is how you go on. And “peace” — the deep, Hebrew shalom kind of wholeness — is only ever the possession of someone standing on grace, not law.

Paul closes these five verses the way his heart always closes when he thinks about the cross — in worship. Christ “gave Himself for our sins” — an act He chose, not one He suffered — “that He might deliver us from this present evil age.” That deliverance did not stop at the empty tomb. It reaches into this week, this struggle, this temptation to earn what has already been freely given. No wonder Paul cannot help but break into praise: “to whom be glory forever and ever. Amen.”

Reflection Questions

  1. Where in your life right now are you tempted to treat grace as only the doorway in, rather than the road you walk on every day?
  2. Paul’s authority rested entirely on Christ, not on human approval. Whose approval are you most tempted to seek instead of resting in what God has already said about you?
  3. “Deliver us from this present evil age” describes a rescue that is already underway. What would it look like this week to actually live like someone being delivered, rather than someone still trying to earn deliverance?

Lord, thank You that grace was never meant to stop at the door.
