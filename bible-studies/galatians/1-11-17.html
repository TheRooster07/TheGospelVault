Galatians 1:11–17 · Small Group Bible Study

Paul's Independent Revelation

Source: Dr. C. Norman Sellers, ThD · SellersBibleInstitute.com · used by permission

① The Biblical Text

NKJV · Read together before anything else

Galatians 1:11-17

11 But I make known to you, brethren, that the gospel which was preached by me is not according to man.

12 For I neither received it from man, nor was I taught it, but it came through the revelation of Jesus Christ.

13 For you have heard of my former conduct in Judaism, how I persecuted the church of God beyond measure and tried to destroy it.

14 And I advanced in Judaism beyond many of my contemporaries in my own nation, being more exceedingly zealous for the traditions of my fathers.

15 But when it pleased God, who separated me from my mother's womb and called me through His grace,

16 to reveal His Son in me, that I might preach Him among the Gentiles, I did not immediately confer with flesh and blood,

17 nor did I go up to Jerusalem to those who were apostles before me; but I went to Arabia, and returned again to Damascus.

② Devotional

Heart preparation · read in the days before your group meets

“Salvation is free. If you believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, you have eternal life — and nothing is added to that. Not one thing.”

Paul was not defending an opinion in these verses — he was defending a revelation. Judaizers had slipped into the churches of Galatia insisting that grace plus law-keeping made the real gospel. Paul reminds these believers that his message never came from a man, a committee, or a classroom — it came straight from the risen Christ. Every man-made religion, he says, carries its own “you must” column, some penalty attached for failing to keep it. Paul's gospel carried none.

That claim carries even more weight once you remember who is making it. This was a man who had hunted down Christians, dragged them from their homes, and stood in approval while they died — about as far from “gracious” as a person could be. If God could reach a man like that with pure, unearned grace in Christ, He can reach you, on your worst day, too.

God did not wait for Paul to clean himself up first. He set him apart before Paul had done a single thing to deserve it, called him by grace alone, and sent him — of all people — to the very Gentiles he had once despised. Grace runs ahead of our worthiness every time.

Reflect

  1. Where in your life are you tempted to quietly add a “must” to God's free gift?
  2. Paul's transformation began before he had done anything to earn it. Where do you see God's grace running ahead of your own effort?
  3. Is there a person or group you've written off, the way Paul once despised the Gentiles? How might grace be calling you toward them?

Lord, thank You that Your grace reaches people long before they deserve it — including me.

Part 2 — Study Questions

1. Paul says his gospel “did not come from man” in three ways. What are they, and why does the word ἀποκάλυψις (“revelation”) matter here?

Show Leader Answer

It was not designed by man, not handed to him through any human agency, and not taught to him through a course of instruction — it was revealed directly by Jesus Christ. ἀποκάλυψις means an unveiling of what was previously hidden; it grounds Paul's authority in God's initiative, not his own study or a teacher's instruction.

2. How does Paul's former conduct — persecuting and trying to destroy the church (v. 13) — actually strengthen his testimony rather than weaken it?

Show Leader Answer

A man so committed to destroying the church (note the imperfect tense of ἐπόρθουν — an ongoing campaign) had every reason to reject grace, not preach it. His transformation cannot be explained by human persuasion; it points to a real, divine encounter.

3. God “set apart” Paul “from his mother's womb” (v. 15) before Paul had done anything. What does ἀφορίσας (“having set apart”) teach about the timing of God's grace?

Show Leader Answer

The setting apart came before merit, before decision, before service — grace precedes and does not depend on the one being called (compare Jeremiah 1:5 and Ephesians 1:4).

4. Why did Paul deliberately avoid Jerusalem and the apostles after his conversion (vv. 16b-17), going instead to Arabia?

Show Leader Answer

So it would be unmistakable that his gospel was not shaped, corrected, or supplemented by human teachers — exactly what the Judaizers were denying. His message came from Christ alone (προσανεθέμην, “I conferred” — something he deliberately did not do), later confirmed but never originated by human counsel (Acts 15).

5. The sermon calls salvation “a free gift” that costs the receiver nothing. Based on Paul's own experience in this passage, how is that gift received?

Show Leader Answer

By faith alone — Paul received revelation, not wages; grace, not credentials. The same gospel he received without human help is offered to us without any addition of works: believe, and receive eternal life as a gift.

⑤ Application

Personal and corporate response — draws from all sections above

Resting in a Gospel You Didn't Invent

Galatians 1:11-12

Source: Commentary §I; Devotional

Paul's confidence rested on the fact that his gospel came from revelation, not invention. The same is true for us: our standing before God is not something we composed, negotiated, or improved upon — it is something we received.

Reflection: Where are you tempted to treat your own reasoning, feelings, or effort as the real foundation of your assurance, rather than what God has revealed and finished in Christ?

Practical Step: This week, write down one thing you tend to lean on for assurance besides Christ's finished work, and replace it in prayer with a simple statement of what God has revealed.

Grace Reaches the Least Likely

Galatians 1:13-14

Source: Commentary §II

Paul was the church's most committed enemy before he became its most tireless advocate. No one is too far gone, too hostile, or too zealous in the wrong direction for grace to reach.

Reflection: Is there someone you have quietly decided is beyond reach — too hardened, too hostile, too far along in the wrong direction?

Practical Step: Name that person this week and pray specifically for them, asking God to do for them what He did for Paul.

Called Before You Could Earn It

Galatians 1:15-16a

Source: Commentary §III; Self-Study Guide (ἀφορίσας, χάριτος)

God set Paul apart from the womb, before Paul had believed, obeyed, or achieved anything. Every believer's calling rests on the same foundation — grace that precedes performance.

Reflection: How does it change your view of your own past, present, and future when you remember God's purpose for you did not begin with your qualifications?

Practical Step: Thank God specifically today for a purpose in your life that began before you could have earned it.

Letting the Gospel Stand on Its Own

Galatians 1:16b-17

Source: Commentary §IV; FGR.docx — Zane C. Hodges, Absolutely Free: A Biblical Reply to Lordship Salvation (Grand Rapids: Zondervan, 1989)

Paul refused to let his gospel be reshaped by human committee or expectation. Hodges's classic Free Grace defense makes the same point for today's reader: a gospel of grace alone, received by faith alone, needs no additional human requirement to be genuine — adding one does not protect the gospel, it changes it.

Reflection: Where might you be tempted — for yourself or in what you teach others — to quietly add a requirement to simple faith in Christ?

Practical Step: Reread Galatians 1:16b-17 this week and identify one place where you can state the gospel to someone without adding anything to “believe.”
