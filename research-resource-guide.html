Research & Strategic Planning

Research Resource Guide

Large-scale, publicly available data sources for foreign missions planning — Bible translation status, unreached people groups, religious persecution, digital connectivity, Deaf ministry, and general demographics.

Prepared for internal research and strategic planning · August 2, 2026

Purpose

This guide compiles the large-scale, publicly available data sources most useful for foreign missions planning — covering Bible translation status, unreached people groups, religious persecution, digital/mobile connectivity, Deaf ministry, and general demographics. It is organized by category, with the specific value of each source noted so it can be matched to the right question rather than treated as interchangeable with the others.

Headline Numbers

Updated Figures From This Research Pass

Worth Noting Up Front

  1. Global internet penetration (ITU, 2025): 74% of the world online (~6.04 billion people); 2.2 billion remain fully offline. By region: Europe 92%, CIS 93%, Asia-Pacific 77%, Arab States 70%, Africa 36% (rural Africa only 21%). High-income countries: 94%; low-income countries: 23%. Urban 85% vs. rural 58%.
  2. Mobile internet usage (GSMA, 2025): 58% of the world (4.7 billion people) actually use mobile internet on their own device, even though 96% of the global population lives within mobile network coverage — meaning the "usage gap" (3.1 billion people covered but not using it) is roughly nine times larger than the "coverage gap" (350 million people with no network at all). This is a critical distinction for app-based strategy: for most unconnected people, the barrier is not infrastructure but affordability, literacy, relevance, or device access, not signal availability.
  3. Least-connected region: Sub-Saharan Africa — only 27% mobile internet usage, a 60% usage gap and a 13% coverage gap, confirming this as the highest-priority zone for the offline-device track rather than the apps track.
  4. Living languages (Ethnologue, 29th edition, 2026): 7,170 living languages worldwide; 3,078 (44%) classified as endangered. Papua New Guinea alone accounts for 840 languages, 12% of the global total, confirming its outsized weight in the translation-gap picture already identified.
01 · Language Data

Bible Translation & Language Data

SIL International

Ethnologue: Languages of the World

ethnologue.com

The master global reference for language counts, speaker populations, geographic distribution, and vitality/endangerment status (EGIDS scale). Wycliffe's own ProgressBible figures are built on Ethnologue data. More granular than any summary source used so far — searchable by country, language family, or status.

New edition roughly annually (29th edition current as of 2026). Core lookup free; full statistical summaries require subscription.

Wycliffe Global Alliance / SIL

ProgressBible

progress.bible

The live database behind the translation-status figures cited throughout this research (544 languages waiting, 1,676 with no Scripture and no active work, etc.). Going to the database directly rather than Wycliffe's blog summaries allows filtering by country or region.

Updated continuously; public dashboard with downloadable snapshots.

UNESCO

Atlas of the World's Languages in Danger

en.wal.unesco.org

Independent cross-check on endangered and dying languages, useful for identifying where "never translated" and "going extinct" overlap — a distinct urgency signal beyond simple translation-waiting-list status.

Periodically updated by UNESCO; successor World Atlas of Languages platform is the current version.

02 · Missiology

Unreached People Groups & Missiology

Joshua Project

joshuaproject.net

The primary reference used throughout this research for unreached/frontier people group counts, the 10/40 Window, oral-preference statistics, and progress-scale classification. Includes a free REST API and downloadable datasets by country, language, and religion.

Continuously updated; ministry of the U.S. Center for World Mission.

Finishing the Task (FTT)

finishingthetask.com

An independent unreached/unengaged people group list, useful as a second source since it does not always agree with Joshua Project — triangulating between lists reduces reliance on any single methodology. Referenced earlier regarding the Deaf population in Nepal.

Coalition of mission agencies; periodically updated list.

International Mission Board

IMB People Groups

peoplegroups.org

A third independent people-group database, maintained by the IMB's global research team. Useful as an additional cross-check alongside Joshua Project and FTT, since the three lists use different definitions and field sourcing.

Updated by IMB regional and national field staff on an ongoing basis.

Joshua Project / Pray1040

The 10/40 Window Resource Hub

joshuaproject.net & pray1040.com

Country lists, maps, and statistics for the 10/40 Window band, already the geographic backbone of this research's zone framework.

Periodically revised country list; static reference with periodic updates.

03 · Religious Freedom

Persecution & Religious Freedom

Open Doors World Watch List

opendoors.org

Already in use in this research — the annual ranking of the 50 countries where Christian persecution is most severe. The primary prioritization tool for the "restricted access" half of the framework.

Published annually, typically in January.

World Watch Monitor

worldwatchmonitor.org

News and country-level reporting specifically on Christian persecution, complementing Open Doors' annual ranking with ongoing incident-level detail.

Continuously updated news reporting.

Release International

releaseinternational.org

UK-based persecution-monitoring and support organization; useful secondary source for cross-checking Open Doors' country assessments.

Ongoing country updates and annual reporting.

Freedom House

freedomhouse.org

Independent (non-faith-based) annual assessments of political freedom and internet freedom by country. Useful for a secular cross-check on which "closed" countries are closed for general political reasons vs. specifically religious ones, and for internet censorship severity by country.

Two annual reports: Freedom in the World and Freedom on the Net.

04 · Technology

Connectivity & Technology

International Telecommunication Union

ITU — Facts and Figures

itu.int

The authoritative UN source for global and country-level internet penetration, more current and more granular than the CIA World Factbook for this specific data. Provides the urban/rural and income-level breakdowns cited above.

Annual flagship report ("Facts and Figures"), published each fall.

GSMA Mobile Connectivity Index

mobileconnectivityindex.com & gsma.com/somic

Covers 170 countries across 32 indicators; the key value-add beyond ITU is the coverage-gap vs. usage-gap distinction — directly informs whether a region needs infrastructure (satellite) or adoption/content work (apps, training, affordability).

Annual report series (State of Mobile Internet Connectivity), published each fall.

see also Freedom House

OpenNet Initiative / Freedom on the Net

freedomhouse.org/report/freedom-net

Country-by-country internet censorship and restriction severity — the specific data point needed to judge whether an app-based approach is viable versus likely to be blocked or monitored in a given restricted country.

Annual report.

05 · Deaf Ministry

Deaf & Sign Language Ministry

World Federation of the Deaf (WFD)

wfdeaf.org

The source organization behind most global Deaf population and sign-language figures cited in this research (the ~70 million estimate, 200–300+ sign languages). Primary source rather than secondary aggregation.

Ongoing; periodic position papers and statistics updates.

Deaf Bible Society

deafbiblesociety.com

Publisher of the Deaf Bible App and tracker of sign-language Scripture translation status specifically — more granular than general Bible-translation sources for this population.

Ongoing; app and translation-status updates.

DOOR International

doorinternational.org

Deaf-led Bible translation and church-planting ministry; publishes its own Deaf-specific unreached statistics and translation progress, and was the primary source for the Deaf figures used earlier in this research.

Ongoing ministry updates and annual reporting.

06 · Demographics

General Demographics

CIA World Factbook

cia.gov/the-world-factbook

Best for population age structure, literacy rate, internet/mobile penetration (though ITU/GSMA are more current for connectivity specifically), government type, and religious composition by country. Not a source for mission-specific or Deaf-specific data.

Updated on a rolling basis, country by country.

UN Population Division — World Population Prospects

population.un.org/wpp

Authoritative source for population totals, age-structure projections, and urban/rural splits by country — more rigorous than the Factbook for demographic modeling and projections.

Major revision approximately every two years.

WHO — Deafness and Hearing Loss Fact Sheet

who.int

Primary source for the global hearing-loss and Deaf population figures used in this research (430 million with disabling hearing loss, 34 million children, age-distribution data).

Periodically updated fact sheet, most recently March 2026.

07 · Accountability

Nonprofit & Organizational Accountability

Evangelical Council for Financial Accountability

ECFA

ecfa.org

Already in use for org-level financial data. Confirms an organization meets defined financial-accountability standards — useful as a basic trust filter before deeper partnership evaluation.

Member profiles updated on each organization's fiscal-year cycle.

MinistryWatch

ministrywatch.com

Already in use throughout this research for org profiles, financials, and staffing data. Independent evaluator with its own donor-confidence scoring, useful as a secondary check alongside ECFA.

Profiles updated periodically; donor-confidence scores recalculated regularly.

Candid / GuideStar

guidestar.org

The largest U.S. nonprofit database, useful for Form 990 financial detail on any US-registered mission organization not already profiled in this research.

Updated as organizations file annual Form 990s with the IRS.

Summary

How to Use This List

As a rule of thumb: Joshua Project and Ethnologue/ProgressBible answer "who and where"; Open Doors and Freedom House answer "how restricted"; ITU and GSMA answer "can they be reached digitally, and how"; WHO/WFD/DOOR answer Deaf-specific questions; and ECFA/MinistryWatch/Candid answer "can this partner organization be trusted with resources." No single source in any category is fully authoritative on its own — cross-checking two sources per question is the more defensible approach for any figures used in external-facing planning documents.
