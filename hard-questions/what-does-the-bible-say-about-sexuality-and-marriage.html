What Does the Bible Say About Sexuality and Marriage?

Translation: NKJV · Free Grace / Dispensational · Source: The Gospel Vault

In Brief

God designed marriage as a lifelong union of one man and one woman, with sex reserved for that covenant. Every sexual sin can be forgiven; a believer’s choices bear on reward and inheritance, not on eternal life.

Key versesGenesis 2:24 · Matthew 19:4–6 · 1 Corinthians 6:9–11

The Bible’s teaching on sexuality and marriage begins with a positive design at creation, before sin entered the world at all. “So God created man in His own image ... male and female He created them” (Genesis 1:27), and marriage is instituted in the same account as the answer to God’s own assessment that “it is not good that man should be alone” (Genesis 2:18). God’s own definition follows: “a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and they shall become one flesh” (Genesis 2:24) — leaving, cleaving, and one-flesh union, the pattern for every marriage in Scripture that follows: one man and one woman in lifelong covenant.1

Jesus, asked about marriage’s permanence, reached directly back to this creation account rather than to later legislation: “He who made them at the beginning ‘made them male and female’ ... Therefore what God has joined together, let not man separate” (Matthew 19:4–6). The New Testament elevates marriage further as a living picture of Christ and the church (Ephesians 5:25, 31–32), and the Song of Solomon devotes an entire book to the goodness of romantic and physical love within it, without embarrassment.

The Fall introduced disorder into this design, and Scripture is candid about the resulting distortions, naming sexual expression outside the creational pattern — including the behaviors described in Romans 1:26–27 and 1 Corinthians 6:9–10 — as a departure from it, alongside greed, drunkenness, and other sins.2 Paul warns that those marked by such a pattern “will not inherit the kingdom of God” (1 Corinthians 6:9–10). In the Free Grace reading this speaks not of entering the kingdom, which is by faith alone, but of inheriting it: a pattern of inconsistent living will have bearing on the extent of a believer’s inheritance and reward in the kingdom (see Q39, “Will There Be Rewards in Heaven, and What Are They For?” and Q22, “What Is the Difference Between Salvation and Discipleship?”). It is essential not to stop at diagnosis: immediately after that list, Paul writes, “such were some of you. But you were washed, but you were sanctified, but you were justified” (1 Corinthians 6:11). The gospel’s answer to every form of sexual sin is redemption, freely available by faith, regardless of the past — a believer’s standing before God is never made contingent on achieving sexual purity, since eternal life is a gift received by faith alone (Ephesians 2:8–9), and struggles here, like struggles with any sin, are a discipleship matter addressed through grace and sanctification.

Scripture also honors singleness as a legitimate, even sometimes preferable, calling rather than a deficient state (1 Corinthians 7:7; Matthew 19:12). The biblical vision is therefore neither prudish suppression nor unrestrained autonomy: marriage as a covenantal, one-flesh union between a man and a woman, sexual expression reserved for that covenant, singleness honored as a genuine calling, and grace freely extended to everyone who has fallen short and turns to Christ.
