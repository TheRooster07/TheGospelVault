50 Hard Questions · Question 31 of 50

Are All Sins Equal in God’s Eyes?

Translation: NKJV · Free Grace / Dispensational · Source: The Gospel Vault

In Brief

Sins differ in seriousness and consequence, but every sin separates us from a holy God and needs the same remedy: Christ’s death, received by faith.

Key versesJohn 19:11 · James 2:10 · Romans 3:22–24

This question is often asked to make a pastoral point — that no one’s sin disqualifies them from grace any more than anyone else’s — and that pastoral point is entirely correct. But the specific claim that all sins are equally serious in God’s sight is not what Scripture actually teaches, and collapsing the real distinctions Scripture makes can create its own confusion.

Scripture is explicit that sins vary in gravity. Jesus tells Pilate that the one who delivered Him over “has the greater sin” (John 19:11), implying degrees of guilt even among those complicit in the same crime. He tells the unrepentant cities of His own ministry that “it will be more tolerable for the land of Sodom and Gomorrah in the day of judgment than for you” (Matthew 10:15) — a direct statement that final judgment accounts for degrees of culpability rather than treating every sin identically. The Mosaic law itself distinguished sins requiring different remedies and penalties, and Scripture consistently treats some sins — murder, sexual exploitation, oppression of the vulnerable — as weightier than others, such as an unkind word spoken in a moment of frustration, even while calling both sinful.

At the same time, Scripture is equally clear that every sin, without exception, is serious enough to separate a person from a holy God and to require the same remedy. “Whoever keeps the whole law, and yet stumbles in one point, he is guilty of all” (James 2:10) — not because every violation is equally weighty, but because any violation at all breaks fellowship with a God whose standard is perfect holiness, and no amount of comparative “better” sinning meets that standard. This is why the smallest sin and the greatest sin require exactly the same solution — faith in Christ’s atoning death — since no sin, regardless of its comparative severity, can be resolved by human effort or moral improvement.

This distinction resolves the apparent tension. Sins differ in their temporal consequences, their damage to other people, and even, Scripture indicates, in the degree of judgment they incur — but they do not differ in their basic disqualifying effect before a holy God, and they do not differ in the remedy that addresses them. The person guilty of what culture regards as a minor fault and the person guilty of the gravest public sin stand in exactly the same need of grace, and both are offered exactly the same free gift on exactly the same terms (Romans 3:22–24, “for all have sinned ... being justified freely by His grace”).

For the believer struggling with guilt over what feels like a uniquely disqualifying failure, this doctrine is freeing: no sin is too small to need grace, and — just as importantly, and consistent with the doctrine of eternal security (see Q20, “Can a Genuine Christian Lose Their Salvation?”) — no sin is too large for grace already received in Christ to cover completely (1 John 1:9; Colossians 2:13).
