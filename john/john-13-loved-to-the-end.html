Chapter 13 of 21

Loved to the End

John 13:1–38  ·  NASB95

Key Verse

“A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another; as I have loved you, that you also love one another. By this all will know that you are My disciples, if you have love for one another.”

John 13:34–35

John opens this chapter with a sentence that sets the tone for everything that follows: Jesus, “having loved His own who were in the world, loved them to the end.” Not almost to the end. Not until they disappointed Him one too many times. To the end — which meant, that very night, getting up from supper, wrapping a towel around His waist, and washing the feet of men who would abandon Him within hours, including the one already planning to betray Him. When Peter objects — “You shall never wash my feet!” — Jesus makes an important distinction: “He who is bathed needs only to wash his feet, but is completely clean.” Salvation is the bath — done once, completely, settled. Ongoing fellowship with Him is the feet — the daily dust that needs washing, not because your standing is in question, but because walking through the world gets you dirty.

After Judas leaves — John notes simply, “it was night” — Jesus gives the disciples what He calls a new commandment: love one another as He has loved them. Not a vague sentiment, but a specific, costly standard, measured against a love that had just knelt down with a basin and towel. He says this love will be the identifying mark of His followers to a watching world — not doctrinal precision alone, not religious performance, but visible love for one another. The chapter ends on a sobering note: Peter, full of confidence, insists he’ll lay down his life for Jesus, and Jesus tells him plainly that before the rooster crows, he’ll deny Him three times. Even the most devoted heart in the room was about to fail spectacularly — and Jesus washed his feet anyway, before he failed, knowing exactly what was coming.

Questions to Carry Into the Chapter

  1. Jesus distinguished being “bathed” (complete, once-for-all cleansing) from needing your “feet washed” (ongoing fellowship). Do you sometimes confuse the two — treating a fresh confession as though it re-secures a salvation that was never actually in question?
  2. Jesus said love for one another, not correct doctrine alone, would be what identifies His disciples to the world. Would someone watching your life this week actually see that kind of love?
  3. Jesus washed Peter’s feet knowing Peter would deny Him hours later. Does that change how you think about His willingness to keep serving you even in the moments you know you’re about to fail Him?
