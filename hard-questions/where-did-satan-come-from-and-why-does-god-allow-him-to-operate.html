Where Did Satan Come From, and Why Does God Allow Him to Operate?

Translation: NKJV · Free Grace / Dispensational · Source: The Gospel Vault

In Brief

Satan is a created angel who fell through pride. He operates only within limits God sets, was defeated at the cross, and will be confined forever.

Key versesEzekiel 28:14–15 · Hebrews 2:14 · Revelation 20:10

Scripture does not present Satan as God’s rival or as an eternal evil counterpart to an eternal good God. Satan is a created being — a powerful angel, originally good, who fell through pride. Two Old Testament passages addressed originally to earthly kings (the king of Tyre and the king of Babylon) are widely understood, because their language transcends any human ruler, to describe the spiritual reality behind those rulers: a created “anointed cherub” who was “blameless ... till iniquity was found” in him, whose heart was “lifted up because of your beauty,” and who said in his heart, “I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above the stars of God ... I will be like the Most High” (Ezekiel 28:14–15; Isaiah 14:12–14). Pride — the desire to be worshiped rather than to worship — is the root of his fall.

Jesus Himself confirms that this fall was real and moral, not merely metaphorical: “I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven” (Luke 10:18), and He calls Satan “a murderer from the beginning” and “the father of lies” who “does not stand in the truth, because there is no truth in him” (John 8:44). Paul warns against putting a new convert in leadership too quickly, “lest being puffed up with pride he fall into the same condemnation as the devil” (1 Timothy 3:6) — treating Satan’s fall as a settled fact of biblical history, not speculation.

Why would God allow such a being to continue operating rather than destroying him immediately? Scripture gives a partial but consistent answer: Satan’s activity, real and dangerous as it is, remains entirely within limits God sets and never exceeds. In Job, Satan cannot touch Job without God’s explicit permission, and even then only within boundaries God fixes (Job 1:12; 2:6). Jesus tells Peter, “Satan has asked for you, that he may sift you as wheat” — language of a request God could grant or deny — while promising, “I have prayed for you, that your faith should not fail” (Luke 22:31–32). Satan is a defeated enemy operating on a leash, not a competing sovereign.

His defeat, in fact, has already been accomplished at the cross, even though its final enforcement is still future. “Through death He might destroy him who had the power of death, that is, the devil” (Hebrews 2:14). “Now is the judgment of this world; now the ruler of this world will be cast out” (John 12:31). Satan continues to “walk about like a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour” (1 Peter 5:8) during this present age, but his ultimate end is already fixed: everlasting confinement in the lake of fire (Revelation 20:10). His continued activity in the present is not evidence that God’s victory is incomplete; it is the outworking of a war whose outcome is already decided, in a period during which God is still gathering His church.

For the believer, the practical response Scripture commends is neither obsession nor dismissal, but sober resistance: “Resist the devil and he will flee from you” (James 4:7); “Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil walks about ... resist him, steadfast in the faith” (1 Peter 5:8–9). The believer’s security in Christ is never in question in this struggle (1 John 4:4), even while the struggle itself remains real until Christ returns to finish what the cross has already guaranteed.
