Chapter 02 of 21

Water Into Wine, Zeal for His Father’s House

John 2:1–25  ·  NASB95

Key Verse

“This beginning of signs Jesus did in Cana of Galilee, and manifested His glory; and His disciples believed in Him.”

John 2:11

Chapter 2 opens at a wedding, not a synagogue — because Jesus meets people in the middle of ordinary life, even its awkward moments. When the wine runs out, His mother simply says, “They have no wine,” and tells the servants, “Whatever He says to you, do it.” Notice the pattern: God asks for what you already have (six stone waterpots, filled with plain water), He doesn’t do the filling for you (the servants had to carry every jar themselves), and then He surprises everyone with the result. The miracle isn’t the point — verse 11 tells you the point: He “manifested His glory,” and “His disciples believed in Him.” That’s the whole aim of every sign in this Gospel.

Then the scene shifts hard — from a wedding to a whip. Jesus drives the money-changers and animal-sellers out of the temple, and His disciples remember the line, “Zeal for Your house has eaten Me up.” When the Jewish leaders demand a sign to justify this, He gives them one they won’t understand for three years: “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up” — speaking of His own body. The chapter ends with a sentence worth sitting with: many believed in His name because of the signs, using the exact same word for “believe” that shows up in John 1:12, 3:16, and 3:36 — real, saving belief. And yet Jesus “did not commit Himself to them,” because He knew what was in their hearts. That’s not a contradiction of assurance; it’s a distinction between it. Salvation is settled the moment you believe. Whether you go on to walk closely and openly with Him is a separate question — one the rest of John will keep asking.

Questions to Carry Into the Chapter

  1. God used what the servants already had (six jars, plain water) rather than doing everything Himself. Where is God asking you to bring what you already have, however small, rather than waiting for Him to do it all?
  2. Verse 23 uses the same “believe” that saves in 1:12 and 3:16 — yet Jesus didn’t entrust Himself to those who believed there. What’s the difference, in your own life, between believing in Jesus for salvation and walking openly with Him as a disciple?
  3. Mary’s instruction to the servants was simple: “Whatever He says to you, do it.” Is there something specific He’s currently saying to you that you’ve been slow to just do?
