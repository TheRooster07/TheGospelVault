Chapter 06 of 21

Bread from Heaven, Words of Eternal Life

John 6:1–71  ·  NASB95

Key Verse

“All that the Father gives Me will come to Me, and the one who comes to Me I will by no means cast out.”

John 6:37

Five thousand men, plus women and children, eat until they’re full from a boy’s lunch — five barley loaves, two small fish, and twelve baskets left over. The crowd draws exactly the wrong conclusion: they try to force Jesus to be their king by force, missing entirely that a kingdom without the cross was never the plan. That night He walks out to the disciples on a storm-tossed sea and simply says, “It is I; do not be afraid” — and immediately the boat is at the shore. Both signs point to the same reality: Jesus can meet any need, physical or otherwise, but the crowd keeps mistaking the sign for the substance. When they track Him down the next day still thinking about bread, He tells them plainly: “You seek Me, not because you saw the signs, but because you ate of the loaves and were filled.”

So Jesus gives them something the manna in the wilderness never could: Himself, called “the bread of life,” the first of John’s seven great “I AM” statements. “He who comes to Me shall never hunger, and he who believes in Me shall never thirst” — coming and believing treated as one and the same act. And then one of the strongest security statements in Scripture: everyone the Father gives to the Son will come to Him, and Jesus will by no means — a double negative in the Greek, about as emphatic as language gets — cast that person out. Not surprisingly, many who had followed Jesus for the free bread walk away when the teaching gets hard. Jesus turns to the twelve and asks if they’ll leave too. Peter’s answer is the only sane response left once you’ve actually understood who you’re talking to: “Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life.”

Questions to Carry Into the Chapter

  1. The crowd wanted what Jesus could give them more than they wanted Jesus Himself. Be honest: is there a way you sometimes want Jesus’s blessings more than you want Him?
  2. Verse 37 promises that anyone who comes to Jesus will “by no means” be cast out — one of the strongest guarantees in the whole Bible. Does your day-to-day confidence in your own salvation actually rest on a promise this strong?
  3. When the teaching got hard, many disciples left, but Peter stayed because “You have the words of eternal life.” When following Jesus gets difficult or confusing for you, what keeps you from walking away?
