James · Study 06 · Leader / Learner Guide

James 1:22–27 — Leader / Learner Guide

Mirror, Mirror: Hearing That Becomes Doing · a one-page guide for leaders and learners

James: Living Wisely Under Pressure  ·  TheGospelVault.com  ·  @iJohn.316 (Instagram)

Full Study 06

Big Idea and Context

Big idea: Hearing God’s Word begins growth but must not end it; the believer who looks intently and does the Word is blessed, and his religion shows in a controlled tongue, care for the helpless, and a life unstained by the world.

After receiving the Word (1:19–21), James turns to responding to it. This passage is the hinge to faith and works in 2:14–26: the hearer who does not do is like faith without works.

  • Three hearers (1:22–25): self-deceived, forgetful, faithful.
  • The law of liberty (1:25): the New Covenant Word, written on the heart.
  • Two religions (1:26–27): worthless (untamed tongue) and pure (care and holiness).

Hearing and Doing — Three Anchor Verses

VerseWhat it saysTeaching point
James 1:22“Prove yourselves doers of the word, and not merely hearers.”Become a doer, a lifestyle, not a single deed.
James 1:25“Looks intently at the perfect law, the law of liberty.”Stoop down and look closely (parakyptō), then keep on.
James 1:27“Visit orphans and widows … unstained by the world.”Pure religion reaches out and keeps clean.

Not a salvation test: the hearer is a “brother”; the issue is profit and blessing, not eternal life (2:14, “What use is it?”).

Three questions: What does it say? What does it mean? What does it mean for me?

Teaching Flow

  1. Introduction. Receive and respond; the hinge to chapter 2.
  2. The self-deceived hearer (1:22). An old lesson (Luke 11:28; Ezekiel 33:32); become a doer; his false syllogism.
  3. The forgetful hearer (1:23–24). The mirror, the walk away, the forgetting.
  4. The faithful hearer (1:25). The law of liberty; looking intently; continuing; blessed in doing.
  5. Worthless and pure religion (1:26–27). The tongue; Isaiah 1 and Micah 6:8; orphans and widows; unstained by the world.

Clarify While Teaching

  • James 2:19, not 2:1, is “even the demons believe” (the outline cites 2:1).
  • Luke 7:16, not Luke 2:16, is “God has visited His people.”
  • Luke 13:3–5 concerns physical destruction, not hell, per the context of the tower of Siloam.
  • Helping the helpless has limits: those unwilling to work (2 Thessalonians 3:10); families first (1 Timothy 5:4).

Discussion and Close

  1. What did the Word last show you that you have not acted on?
  2. Why does James make the tongue the first test of real religion?
  3. Who are the “orphans and widows” near you?

Close: Pure religion cares for the helpless. Study 07 (James 2:1–7) shows how easily believers can do the opposite: favor the rich and slight the poor.
