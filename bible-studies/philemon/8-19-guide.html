Philemon · Study 02 · Leader / Learner Guide

Philemon 8–19 — Leader / Learner Guide

A Comprehensive Appeal · a one-page guide for leaders and learners

The Epistle to Philemon  ·  TheGospelVault.com  ·  @iJohn.316 (Instagram)

Download PDF Full Study 02

Big Idea and Context

Big idea: Paul, now the soul of tact, does not command Philemon but appeals along three lines — necessity, responsibility, and liability — to receive Onesimus as a beloved brother, as he would Paul, with Paul paying whatever is owed.

“A man who would have friends must himself be friendly” (Prov. 18:24). Paul had his moments with Barnabas and John Mark, but he learned; here he is walking on eggshells.

  • Necessity (vv. 8–13): Paul gives his rights, then gives them up for higher ground, gains, and goals.
  • Responsibility (vv. 14–17): Paul’s, Onesimus’, and Philemon’s — no longer a slave, more than a slave, as Paul himself.
  • Liability (vv. 18–19): “Put that on my account” — a picture of imputation.

Put It on My Account — Three Anchor Verses

VerseWhat it saysTeaching point
v. 9“yet for love’s sake I rather appeal to you”Paul could command as an apostle; he appeals as “Paul the aged” and a prisoner of Jesus Christ.
v. 11“once … unprofitable … but now is profitable”Paronomasia: Onesimus means “useful”; achrēstos → euchrēstos.
v. 18“put that on my account”A non-theological picture of imputation: Christ paid a debt He did not owe (2 Cor. 5:21).

Negative, positive, superlative (vv. 16–17): no longer a slave · more than a slave, a beloved brother · receive him as you would me.

Iron fist, velvet glove (v. 19b): “not to mention … you owe me even your own self besides.”

Teaching Flow

  1. Introduction. Proverbs 18:24; Paul, Barnabas, and John Mark (Acts 15:36; Col. 4:10; 1 Cor. 9:6).
  2. Necessity (vv. 8–13). Rights given, then given up — higher ground, higher gains (son, slave), higher goals.
  3. Responsibility (vv. 14–17). Voluntary, not by compulsion; a changed life; receive him as a brother, as Paul.
  4. Liability (vv. 18–19). The debit side (“put it on my account”) and the credit side (“you owe me … your own self”).
  5. Conclusion. Paul appealed rather than demanded — and they were friends. A friend who sticks closer than a brother.

Clarify While Teaching

  • 1 Corinthians 9:6: the sermon as preached says “who have the right”; the text reads “who have no right to refrain from working.”
  • Paul’s age: 57 now and 62 at death are the sermon’s estimates; Scripture does not give Paul’s age.
  • ”My son”: Paul also calls Timothy and Titus his sons (1 Tim. 1:2; Titus 1:4), more than twice in all.
  • ”Aged” (v. 9): NKJV reads “aged”; some scholars read “ambassador.” The sermon follows “aged.”

Discussion and Close

  1. Where are you tempted to use your rights instead of making an appeal for love’s sake?
  2. Who is the “Onesimus” in your life — someone you need to receive as a brother or sister?
  3. How is v. 18 a picture of what Christ did for us?

Close: Point ahead to vv. 20–25, “A Compelling Appendix”: Paul expects Philemon to do even more than he asks. “I owed a debt I could not pay; He paid a debt He did not owe.”
