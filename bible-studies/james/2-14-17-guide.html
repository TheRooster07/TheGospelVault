James · Study 09 · Leader / Learner Guide

James 2:14–17 — Leader / Learner Guide

What Good Is Faith That Does Nothing? · a one-page guide for leaders and learners

James: Living Wisely Under Pressure  ·  TheGospelVault.com  ·  @iJohn.316 (Instagram)

Full Study 09

Big Idea and Context

Big idea: James does not teach that works are needed to receive or prove eternal life; he teaches that a believer’s faith without works is useless, “dead,” unable to help a needy brother or to rescue the believer’s own life from sin’s ruin.

The most debated passage in James. Read in context, the question is “What use is it?” (2:14, 16), addressed to “my brethren,” about profit, not eternal destiny.

  • Three readings: works to receive life; works to prove life; works for a useful life (this study).
  • Key words: use, faith, works, save. “Save” in James means rescue of life (1:21; 5:15, 20).
  • The illustration (2:15–17): kind words to a hungry brother; faith by itself is “dead,” inactive.

Faith and Works — Three Anchor Verses

VerseWhat it saysTeaching point
Romans 4:5“To the one who does not work, but believes … his faith is credited as righteousness.”Eternal life is received by faith alone.
James 2:14“What use is it … Can that faith save him?”A question of profit; saved from a wasted life and sin’s ruin.
James 2:17“Faith, if it has no works, is dead.”Real but inactive, like a dead battery.

The article (“that faith”): common with abstract nouns; it does not create a second, fake kind of faith.

“Dead” figuratively: Romans 4:19; Luke 15:24; Hebrews 9:14; Revelation 3:1; James 2:26.

Teaching Flow

  1. Introduction. The most misunderstood passage; Paul and James agree (Galatians 2:9).
  2. Three readings of James 2. Front-loading, back-loading, Free Grace; Romans 4:5; John 6:47.
  3. Key words (2:14). Use, faith, works (Ephesians 2:8–10), save (five uses in James).
  4. “Can that faith save him?” The article argument; saved from what; what faith at work gains.
  5. The illustration and thesis (2:15–17). Famine; words without help; what “dead” means; two kinds of salvation.

Clarify While Teaching

  • Titus 3:5 (the outline has “Titus 5:5”).
  • 2:15, 2:21, 2:25 are the verses for the needy brother, Abraham and Rahab (the outline’s “1:15, 1:21, 1:25”).
  • Luther questioned the letter’s apostolic standing and called it “an epistle of straw”; avoid overstating what he said.
  • Name positions, not people: describe the views fairly; many who hold them are sincere believers.

Discussion and Close

  1. How does settling Romans 4:5 first change how you read James 2?
  2. What need have you answered with words only?
  3. What does “dead faith” look like in daily life?

Close: Someone will object, “You have faith and I have works.” Study 10 (James 2:18–26) answers the objection with Abraham and Rahab.
