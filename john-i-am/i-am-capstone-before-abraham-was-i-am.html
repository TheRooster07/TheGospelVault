Capstone

Before Abraham Was, I AM

John 8  ·  The I AM Statements

Key Verse

“Truly, truly, I say to you, before Abraham was born, I am.”

John 8:58

In a heated exchange with religious leaders proud of their lineage from Abraham, Jesus makes a claim that has nothing to do with age or timeline and everything to do with identity. When they scoff that He isn’t even fifty years old and claims to have seen Abraham, Jesus answers with one of the most staggering sentences in Scripture: “Truly, truly, I say to you, before Abraham was born, I am” (v.58). Not “I was.” Not “I existed before him.” I AM — the exact phrase God used to identify Himself to Moses at the burning bush: “I AM WHO I AM... Thus you shall say to the sons of Israel, ‘I AM has sent me to you’” (Exodus 3:14). Everyone standing there understood exactly what He was claiming. They picked up stones on the spot (v.59).

Every other I AM statement in John — bread, light, the door, the shepherd, the resurrection, the way, the vine — borrows its authority from this one. They’re all true because this is true first. This is the claim underneath all the others: not just that Jesus does godlike things, but that He simply, eternally is God — the same I AM who spoke from the fire to Moses, unchanged, uncreated, without beginning. Later, when soldiers come to arrest Him in the garden, He says the same words — “I am He” — and they fall backward to the ground before He’s even resisted (18:5–6). The identity was never in question. What was in question is whether the people around Him — then and now — would actually believe it.

Questions to Carry

  1. Jesus didn’t say “I existed before Abraham.” He said “I AM,” reaching straight back to God’s own name at the burning bush. What does it change about the other seven I AM statements to realize they all rest on this claim of deity?
  2. The soldiers arresting Jesus fell backward simply at the sound of “I am He” (18:5–6). Does your view of who Jesus actually is carry that same weight, or has familiarity made the claim feel smaller than it is?
  3. Having walked through all seven I AM statements and this capstone, which one landed on you most directly — and what would it look like to actually believe it today rather than just admire it?
