50 Hard Questions · Question 24 of 50

Is God Really in Control of Everything That Happens?

Translation: NKJV · Free Grace / Dispensational · Source: The Gospel Vault

In Brief

God rules over all, and human choices are real and accountable. Scripture holds both together, as Joseph’s story and the cross show.

Key versesPsalm 103:19 · Genesis 50:20 · Acts 2:23

Scripture affirms both realities that this question sets in tension without collapsing either one: God is absolutely sovereign, and human choices are real and morally accountable. “The Lord has established His throne in heaven, and His kingdom rules over all” (Psalm 103:19). “He does according to His will in the army of heaven and among the inhabitants of the earth; no one can restrain His hand” (Daniel 4:35). Nothing that happens — including what human agents freely choose to do — ultimately escapes God’s overarching purpose.

The clearest biblical picture of how these two truths hold together without contradiction is Joseph’s own summary of his brothers’ betrayal: “you meant evil against me; but God meant it for good” (Genesis 50:20). The brothers acted freely and were held morally responsible for real sin, yet God’s sovereign purpose was accomplishing something they neither intended nor controlled. The cross displays the same pattern at its highest pitch: wicked men crucified Jesus by their own genuine choice, and yet this happened “by the determined purpose and foreknowledge of God” (Acts 2:23). Both statements are true at once; Scripture never treats them as competitors.

This is why Free Grace and dispensational theology insists that God’s sovereignty does not erase human responsibility, particularly at the point of the gospel invitation. God desires all people to be saved (1 Timothy 2:4; 2 Peter 3:9) and issues a real, sincere offer that a real human choice can accept or refuse (John 1:12; Revelation 22:17). Ryrie’s Basic Theology treats God’s sovereignty and human freedom as compatible facts revealed side by side in Scripture rather than as a philosophical puzzle to be resolved by minimizing one or the other.1 On the basic doctrine of providence — that God governs all events, great and small, toward His own purposes — Lewis Sperry Chafer gives the doctrine extended treatment.2

Practically, this doctrine is meant to produce confidence rather than fatalism. Paul’s confidence that “all things work together for good to those who love God” (Romans 8:28) is not a claim that everything that happens is good, but that a sovereign God is at work even through what is evil, weaving it toward a purpose He alone fully sees. It also grounds the believer’s freedom to make real decisions, pray real prayers, and take real responsibility for obedience, knowing that none of it occurs outside the care of a God who is never surprised, never overruled, and never absent from the outcome.

God’s control, then, is not the erasure of human agency but its context. Every choice a person makes is a real choice with real consequences, and every one of those choices unfolds inside a sovereignty that guarantees God’s ultimate purposes will not fail — “declaring the end from the beginning ... saying, ‘My counsel shall stand, and I will do all My pleasure’” (Isaiah 46:10).
