Colossians · Study 11 · Leader / Learner Guide

Colossians 4:2–6 — Leader / Learner Guide

Perfecting the Prayer Life · a one-page guide for leaders and learners

Christ Is All — A Study of Colossians  ·  TheGospelVault.com  ·  @iJohn.316 (Instagram)

Download PDF Full Study 11

Big Idea and Context

Big idea: Devote yourselves to alert, thankful prayer, including prayer for open doors for the gospel, and walk and talk wisely toward outsiders.

Having prayed for the Colossians (1:3, 9), the imprisoned Paul now asks for their prayers, focused on evangelism, and urges wisdom in their own witness.

  • Supplication: devoted, alert, thankful prayer.
  • Presentation: pray for an open door and clear words.
  • Implementation and conversation: wise walk, redeemed opportunities, gracious and salty speech.

Pray, Walk, Talk — Three Anchor Verses

VerseWhat it saysTeaching point
4:2"Devote yourselves to prayer, keeping alert in it with an attitude of thanksgiving"Prayer as breathing: inhale by prayer, exhale by thanksgiving.
4:3"that God will open up to us a door for the word"Pray rather than push; God is the expert in doors.
4:6"speech always be with grace, as though seasoned with salt"Sweetness, seasoning, and Scripture for each person.

Watch three times: before, during, and after prayer.

"Opportunity" (*kairos*): the right moment, not mere clock time; buy it up.

Teaching Flow

  1. Devoted and guarded prayer (4:2a–b) — Persistent; alert like Gethsemane's "watch and pray."
  2. Grateful prayer (4:2c) — Thanksgiving throughout Colossians.
  3. An open door (4:3–4) — Prayer for opportunity and clarity, even in chains.
  4. Wise walk (4:5) — Conduct toward outsiders; redeem the opportunity.
  5. Gracious talk (4:6) — Grace, salt, and the Word for each person.

Clarify While Teaching

  • Quotations: the McGee and Emerson lines are commonly cited; present them as attributed sayings, not verified quotations. The Emerson line is often misattributed.
  • "Mystery of Christ": the gospel as revealed to Paul, including Jew and Gentile in one body (Eph. 3:4–6).
  • Closed doors (Acts 16:6–7): God's redirection, not Paul's failure; avoid overstating it as Paul's error.
  • Salt: keep the emphasis on wholesome, attractive speech; sharp words are the exception.

Discussion and Close

  1. Is your prayer life more like breathing or like an emergency kit you open only in a crisis?
  2. Paul asked for an open door rather than an open cell. What does that reveal about his priorities?
  3. How can speech be both "with grace" and "seasoned with salt"?

Close: Next, the closing portrait of Paul's ministry team and his final instructions (4:7–18). "Devote yourselves to prayer."
