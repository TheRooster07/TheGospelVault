Hard Questions · Part IX, Question 7

“This Generation Will Not Pass Away”: What Did Jesus Mean?

Translation: NKJV · Free Grace / Dispensational · Source: The Gospel Vault

In Brief

Jesus meant the generation that sees the signs He had just described: the abomination of desolation, the great tribulation, and His coming in glory (Matthew 24:15–31). That generation will see it all through to the end. It is still future.

Key versesMatthew 24:30–34 · Matthew 24:36 · Jeremiah 31:35–37

The other reading. Preterists say Jesus meant the people listening to Him, so everything was fulfilled when Rome destroyed Jerusalem in AD 70. But Jesus also said all the tribes of the earth would “see the Son of Man coming on the clouds of heaven with power and great glory” (24:30), and His angels would gather His elect from the four winds (24:31). None of that happened in AD 70.

Read it in context. Just before, Jesus said, “when you see all these things, know that it is near” (24:33). “This generation” is the one that sees “all these things”: the abomination of desolation Daniel spoke of (24:15; Daniel 9:27), a tribulation unlike any before it (24:21), and signs in the sun, moon and stars (24:29). Once those signs begin, they will not stretch on for ages. The people who see them will also see His return.

Another view. Some take “generation” (Greek genea) to mean a people or race: the nation of Israel will not pass away before these things happen. God made the same promise in Jeremiah 31:35–37. Charles Ryrie, for one, favors the first reading and also mentions this second one. Either way, the words point to the future, not to AD 70.

Why it matters. “Heaven and earth will pass away, but My words will by no means pass away” (24:35). What Jesus promised will happen just as He said. Yet “of that day and hour no one knows” (24:36), so we watch without setting dates. The Church waits for the Lord to come for her before the Tribulation begins (1 Thessalonians 4:13–18).
