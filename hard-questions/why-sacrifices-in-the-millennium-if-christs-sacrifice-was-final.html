Hard Questions · Part IX, Question 6

Why Sacrifices in the Millennium, If Christ’s Sacrifice Was Final?

Translation: NKJV · Free Grace / Dispensational · Source: The Gospel Vault

In Brief

The sacrifices in Ezekiel 43–46 do not take away sin. Christ’s one offering did that once for all (Hebrews 10:10–14). In the kingdom they look back to the cross, the way the Lord’s Supper does today.

Key versesHebrews 10:4 · Hebrews 10:12–14 · 1 Corinthians 11:26

The objection. Hebrews says Christ “offered one sacrifice for sins forever” and that “there is no longer an offering for sin” (Hebrews 10:12, 18). How could sacrifices return?

They never took away sin, even under Moses. “It is not possible that the blood of bulls and goats could take away sins” (Hebrews 10:4). The Old Testament sacrifices pointed ahead to Christ, so sacrifices in the kingdom cannot compete with the cross. They point back to it.

A memorial. The Lord’s Supper does not repeat the cross; it proclaims “the Lord’s death till He comes” (1 Corinthians 11:26). Millennial sacrifices serve the same purpose for Israel and the nations, in Messiah’s presence.

“To make atonement” (Ezekiel 45:15, 17). Some dispensational teachers take this as outward, ceremonial cleansing that keeps the temple holy while mortal people live in the kingdom. It is not forgiveness or eternal life. Either way, the cross alone saves.

The bottom line. In every age eternal life is received the same way: by faith in Christ alone, a free gift that can never be lost.
