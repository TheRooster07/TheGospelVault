Chapter 10 of 21

No One Can Snatch Them Out of My Hand

John 10:1–42  ·  NASB95

Key Verse

“And I give them eternal life, and they shall never perish; neither shall anyone snatch them out of My hand. My Father, who has given them to Me, is greater than all; and no one is able to snatch them out of My Father’s hand.”

John 10:28–29

Jesus paints two competing pictures of shepherding. There’s the hireling — hired help who doesn’t own the sheep, and the moment a wolf shows up, he’s gone, because he was never actually invested. And there’s the good shepherd, who says plainly, “I lay down My life for the sheep,” not as a hypothetical risk but as a settled commitment. He knows His sheep individually — calls them by name — and they know His voice well enough to follow Him and flee from a stranger’s. This isn’t generic religious language. It’s a description of a relationship, not a transaction.

Then comes one of the strongest security statements anywhere in Scripture. Jesus gives His sheep eternal life, and says they will “never perish” — a double negative in the Greek, deliberately stacked for maximum force, essentially “never, ever perish.” And no one will snatch them out of His hand. As if that weren’t enough, He adds that the Father, who is greater than all, holds them too — so a believer is held by two hands at once, the Son’s and the Father’s. It’s worth sitting with the picture: not gripped by your own strength, but held by both hands of the Godhead simultaneously. When Jesus goes on to say, “I and the Father are one,” His hearers understood exactly what He meant and reached for stones. They rejected the very claim that, once believed, becomes your deepest security.

Questions to Carry Into the Chapter

  1. Jesus contrasts a hireling who flees at danger with a shepherd who lays down his life. Do you tend to relate to God more like a hired hand fulfilling obligations, or like sheep who know and trust the Shepherd's voice?
  2. Verse 28 uses one of the strongest double negatives in the Greek New Testament to promise you will never perish. Does your day-to-day sense of security match the strength of that promise?
  3. You are held by two hands at once — the Son’s and the Father’s. When anxiety about your standing with God creeps in, which hand do you tend to forget is holding you?
