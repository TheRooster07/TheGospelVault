33 Truths at Salvation for Every Believer

Within God’s “Much More” Care

Key Verse: Romans 5:9–10  ·  NASB1995

Key Verse

“Much more then, having now been justified by His blood, we shall be saved from the wrath of God through Him. For if while we were enemies we were reconciled to God through the death of His Son, much more, having been reconciled, we shall be saved by His life.”

Romans 5:9–10

Romans 5 runs on a “much more” logic: if God did the hardest thing already — dying for His enemies — He will certainly do everything else needed to keep and care for His friends. This truth gathers seven ways Scripture says that care actually reaches you.

What This Means for You

I.

Objects of His Love

“But God, being rich in mercy, because of His great love with which He loved us,”

Ephesians 2:4 · NASB1995

“and walk in love, just as Christ also loved you and gave Himself up for us, an offering and a sacrifice to God as a fragrant aroma.”

Ephesians 5:2 · NASB1995

God’s love for you isn’t a reaction to your goodness. Ephesians 2:4 says it was “because of His great love with which He loved us” that He acted while you were “dead in your trespasses.” Ephesians 5:2 shows the shape that love took — Christ “gave Himself up for us, an offering and a sacrifice to God as a fragrant aroma” — love proven at cost, not merely claimed in words.

II.

Objects of His Grace

“For by grace you have been saved through faith; and that not of yourselves, it is the gift of God;”

Ephesians 2:8 · NASB1995

“through whom also we have obtained our introduction by faith into this grace in which we stand; and we exult in hope of the glory of God”

Romans 5:2 · NASB1995

“But to each one of us grace was given according to the measure of Christ's gift.”

Ephesians 4:7 · NASB1995

“For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation to all men, instructing us to deny ungodliness and worldly desires and to live sensibly, righteously and godly in the present age,”

Titus 2:11-12 · NASB1995

Grace didn’t stop the moment you were saved. Ephesians 2:8 anchors your salvation itself in grace “through faith...the gift of God,” and Romans 5:2 says you now “stand” in that same grace day by day. Ephesians 4:7 promises grace was given to each believer “according to the measure of Christ’s gift,” and Titus 2:11-12 shows grace doing double duty — it both brings salvation and instructs you “to live sensibly, righteously and godly in the present age.”

III.

Objects of His Power

“and what is the surpassing greatness of His power toward us who believe. These are in accordance with the working of the strength of His might”

Ephesians 1:19 · NASB1995

“for it is God who is at work in you, both to will and to work for His good pleasure.”

Philippians 2:13 · NASB1995

Ephesians 1:19 prays that believers would grasp “the surpassing greatness of His power toward us who believe” — the same power, Paul goes on to say, that raised Christ from the dead. Philippians 2:13 brings that power inside you: “it is God who is at work in you, both to will and to work for His good pleasure.” This isn’t borrowed strength you have to summon; it’s resurrection power already at work.

IV.

Objects of His Faithfulness

“Make sure that your character is free from the love of money, being content with what you have; for He Himself has said, 'I will never desert you, nor will I ever forsake you,'”

Hebrews 13:5 · NASB1995

“For I am confident of this very thing, that He who began a good work in you will perfect it until the day of Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 1:6 · NASB1995

God is not fickle. Hebrews 13:5 records His own promise, “I will never desert you, nor will I ever forsake you,” as the ground for contentment rather than anxiety. Philippians 1:6 extends that faithfulness across your whole life: “He who began a good work in you will perfect it until the day of Christ Jesus.” What He began, He has committed Himself to finish.

V.

Objects of His Peace

“Let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts, to which indeed you were called in one body; and be thankful.”

Colossians 3:15 · NASB1995

God doesn’t just forgive the conflict between you and Him — He actively rules your heart. Colossians 3:15 says “let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts, to which indeed you were called,” a peace meant to settle every anxious thought, not merely to end an old hostility.

VI.

Objects of His Consolation

“Now may our Lord Jesus Christ Himself and God our Father, who has loved us and given us eternal comfort and good hope by grace,”

2 Thessalonians 2:16 · NASB1995

Whatever comfort you’ve needed in grief or hardship, God is described as its actual source. Second Thessalonians 2:16 says “our Lord Jesus Christ Himself and God our Father...loved us and given us eternal comfort and good hope by grace” — “eternal consolation” isn’t a figure of speech, but a settled possession you carry into every hard season.

VII.

Objects of His Intercession

“Therefore He is able also to save forever those who draw near to God through Him, since He always lives to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25 · NASB1995

“who is the one who condemns? Christ Jesus is He who died, yes, rather who was raised, who is at the right hand of God, who also intercedes for us.”

Romans 8:34 · NASB1995

“For Christ did not enter a holy place made with hands, a mere copy of the true one, but into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us;”

Hebrews 9:24 · NASB1995

Right now, Christ is in heaven representing you before the Father. Hebrews 7:25 says He “is able also to save forever those who draw near to God through Him, since He always lives to make intercession for them.” Romans 8:34 asks who could possibly condemn you when Christ Himself “is at the right hand of God” and “also intercedes for us,” and Hebrews 9:24 says He entered heaven itself “to appear in the presence of God for us.” Your case is never unattended.

Reflect

  1. Which of these seven — love, grace, power, faithfulness, peace, comfort, or intercession — do you most need to remember is true of you this week?
  2. Romans 5 argues “much more” — if God did the hardest thing for you already, what does that settle about whether He’ll do the rest?
