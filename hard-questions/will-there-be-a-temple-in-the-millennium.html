Hard Questions · Part IX, Question 5

Will There Be a Temple in the Millennium?

Translation: NKJV · Free Grace / Dispensational · Source: The Gospel Vault

In Brief

Yes. Ezekiel saw a literal temple in Jerusalem during Christ’s thousand-year reign, with the glory of the Lord returning to it. Its measurements, priests and land boundaries are too exact to be only a symbol.

Key versesEzekiel 43:1–5 · Ezekiel 37:26–28 · Zechariah 6:12–13

What Ezekiel saw. In the twenty-fifth year of the exile (Ezekiel 40:1), Ezekiel was given a detailed vision: the temple’s measurements, the glory of the Lord returning through the east gate (43:1–5), a river flowing out from the temple (47:1–12), the land divided among the twelve tribes (47:13–48:29), and a city named “THE LORD IS THERE” (48:35).

It has never been built. It matches neither Solomon’s temple nor the second temple that Herod enlarged, so it is still future. Isaiah 2:2–3 and Zechariah 14:16 describe the nations going up to Jerusalem to worship in that day, and Zechariah 6:12–13 says the Branch, the Messiah, will build the temple of the Lord.

Why it matters. God promised Israel, “I will set My sanctuary in their midst forevermore” (Ezekiel 37:26–28). A literal temple shows that God keeps His promises as He made them.
