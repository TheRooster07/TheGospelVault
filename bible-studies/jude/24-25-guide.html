Jude · Study 6 · Leader / Learner Guide

Jude 24–25 — Leader / Learner Guide

The Doxology of Confidence: He Is Able · a one-page guide for leaders and learners

TheGospelVault.com  ·  @iJohn.316 (Instagram)

Download PDF Full Study 6

Big Idea and Context

Big idea: Jude’s letter ends in worship: God is able to keep believers from stumbling and to make them stand before His glory blameless with great joy, and to Him belong glory, majesty, dominion, and authority forever.

The doxology is not an afterthought. It is the ground under every imperative in the letter, and it answers the letter’s anxiety with God’s ability.

  • v. 1: called, beloved, kept.
  • vv. 3–23: contend, remember, build, pray, keep, rescue.
  • vv. 24–25: able, blameless, great joy, forever.

Three Anchor Phrases in the Doxology

VerseWhat it saysTeaching point
v. 24aable to keep you from stumblingdynamenō, aptaistous (NT hapax). The final stumble is not available to the kept.
v. 24bmake you stand… blameless with great joystēsai (causative), amōmous (sacrificial acceptability), agalliasis (exuberant gladness).
v. 25glory, majesty, dominion and authorityWhat He is (glory, majesty); what He wields (dominion, authority).

The bookends: Jude 1: “kept for Jesus Christ.” Jude 24: “able to keep you from stumbling… blameless with great joy.” The whole letter lives inside those two declarations.

Teaching Flow

  1. He is able (v. 24a). The ability that answers the letter’s warnings.
  2. To make you stand (v. 24b). God’s doing; blameless; with great joy.
  3. To the only God our Savior (v. 25). Four attributes, two pairs; before all time, now, forever.

Clarify While Teaching

  • Does “keep from stumbling” mean we never sin? No. First John 1:9 deals with ordinary stumbling. This is the final fall.
  • Reformed and Free Grace: Reformed theology grounds final glory in the perseverance of the saints; Free Grace grounds it in God’s ability and promise.
  • Whose joy? The grammar does not say; likely both ours and His.
  • The text of v. 25: the longer “before all time” reading is followed by the NASB.

Discussion and Close

  1. Where do you most need to remember “He is able”?
  2. What is different when you contend from victory rather than for it?
  3. How could your group end its meetings with Jude 24–25 as a doxology?

Close: Close with the doxology itself. Read Jude 24–25 aloud together and say “Amen.”
