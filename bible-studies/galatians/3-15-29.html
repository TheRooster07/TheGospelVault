Galatians 3:15–29 · Small Group Bible Study

The Superiority of the Promise to the Law

Source: Dr. C. Norman Sellers, ThD · SellersBibleInstitute.com · used by permission

Download PDF

① The Biblical Text

New King James Version (NKJV) · Galatians 3:15-29

15 Brethren, I speak in the manner of men: Though it is only a man’s covenant, yet if it is confirmed, no one annuls or adds to it.

16 Now to Abraham and his Seed were the promises made. He does not say, “And to seeds,” as of many, but as of one, “And to your Seed,” who is Christ.

17 And this I say, that the law, which was four hundred and thirty years later, cannot annul the covenant that was confirmed before by God in Christ, that it should make the promise of no effect.

18 For if the inheritance is of the law, it is no longer of promise; but God gave it to Abraham by promise.

19 What purpose then does the law serve? It was added because of transgressions, till the Seed should come to whom the promise was made; and it was appointed through angels by the hand of a mediator.

20 Now a mediator does not mediate for one only, but God is one.

21 Is the law then against the promises of God? Certainly not! For if there had been a law given which could have given life, truly righteousness would have been by the law.

22 But the Scripture has confined all under sin, that the promise by faith in Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe.

23 But before faith came, we were kept under guard by the law, kept for the faith which would afterward be revealed.

24 Therefore the law was our tutor to bring us to Christ, that we might be justified by faith.

25 But after faith has come, we are no longer under a tutor.

26 For you are all sons of God through faith in Christ Jesus.

27 For as many of you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ.

28 There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is neither male nor female; for you are all one in Christ Jesus.

29 And if you are Christ’s, then you are Abraham’s seed, and heirs according to the promise.

② Devotional

Personal preparation · For all group members before the study

“The Law said, ‘Do this and you will be blessed.’ The Promise says, ‘You have been blessed, do this.’”

God’s promise to Abraham never had a string attached. He simply said what He would do — land, a nation, a blessing reaching the whole world through one Seed — and Abraham did nothing but sleep while God alone walked through the pieces of the covenant. That is how sure a promise made on God’s word alone can be: sure enough that He swore by Himself, because there was no one greater to swear by.

Four hundred and thirty years later the Law arrived, but it could not touch what was already settled. It was never meant to save; it was a tutor, holding the guilty until the true Seed came, so that everyone who has ever believed God the way Abraham did would be counted righteous the same way he was — by faith alone.

That is why sons of God through faith stand higher than the Law could ever lift them, why one Spirit-baptism binds them to Christ deeper than any other bond, why the ground is level at the foot of the cross regardless of nationality, class, or sex, and why the inheritance that is theirs in Abraham’s Seed reaches as far into the future as it reaches back into the past.

Reflect

  1. Where in your walk with God have you quietly slipped back into “do this and you will be blessed” instead of resting in “you have been blessed, do this”?
  2. Abraham’s only part in the covenant was to be asleep and to believe. What would it look like this week to stop trying to “hold up your end” of a promise God already made unconditionally?
  3. Galatians 3:28 says the ground is level at the cross. Is there someone you unconsciously treat as having less standing before God than you do?

Lord, thank You that the promise never depended on me — only on believing You.

③ Commentary

Teacher preparation resource · Full text organized under outline headings

PROPOSITION The Mosaic Law never touched, altered, or rose to the level of the unconditional promise God made to Abraham; it served God's purpose for its appointed time and then, having done its job, gave way to the promise of righteousness by faith in Jesus Christ.

So far, Paul in chapter 3 has been proving that the Law has been set aside. And this is a hard nut to crack with some people. It's hard for people to hear that. You say to people, "The Law is done away." Really? The Mosaic Law is gone? Yep, it's gone. He said that in the first fourteen verses. Now he's going to show them why. And the key problem you have here is that if the Law has been done away, then why was it given in the first place? He's going to talk about that in this section. He's going to say four things about the Law, and the first one is that the Law is not a part of the promise. It never was. It is not a part of the promise. He uses an illustration here, and then he applies that illustration.

I. The Law is Not Part of the Promise — 3:15-18

A. The Illustration — 3:15

Brethren, I speak in the manner of men: Though it is only a man's covenant, yet if it is confirmed, no one annuls or adds to it. Galatians 3:15

The first illustration is in verse 15. He says, "Brethren, I speak as the manner of men, though it is only a man's covenant, yet if it is confirmed, no one annuls or adds to it." No one ends it, no one tears it up or adds to it, except the people who made the covenant.

The Judaizers were teaching that the Law changed the promise by adding works to it. The Judaizers had been saying the Law changed the promise — the Law changed the promise, it added to it. And he says, "No, no, you can't do that." But look how you do contracts. If you make a contract, say to buy a car, you sign it, and the owner says, "We'll do these things," and you do these things, and you sign it, and nobody changes that. My neighbor came to me and said, "Well, I'm going to change your contract with the dealer down here" — can't do that. That's what he's saying.

One of the best methods for teaching is to go from the known to the unknown. See, the best method for teaching, one of the best, is to go from the known to the unknown. They know what a contract is. They know what it's like to sign your name on something and say, "All right, this is what we'll do." Nobody can change that except the two people who signed it, or as many as there were. So he's going from that and saying, okay, now you understand that — that's what you do.

See, men — in covenants of men, if it is confirmed, no one changes it but the parties who made it. They would cut an animal in two pieces. See what they would do — they didn't just shake hands. They would take an animal and cut it in half, and they would lay it out like this. And then when you made this covenant, the two of you would walk between the pieces. And the idea was, I'm going to do this, you do this, I'll do this, you'll do this, as you walk through, reciting the terms of the covenant. And then they would say, "If I don't keep this covenant, may I be like these animals? May I be like them?" Shedding the blood there says, this is true, this is what's going to happen. Only they could change the covenant. That's the way men did it. So he says, okay, if that's the way you do it here among men, that's exactly what God does. See, that's the illustration.

B. The Explanation — 3:16

Now to Abraham and his Seed were the promises made. He does not say, 'And to seeds,' as of many, but as of one, 'And to your Seed,' who is Christ. Galatians 3:16

Now the explanation is verse 16. "Now to Abraham and his Seed were the promises made. He does not say, 'And to seeds,' as of many, but as of one, 'And to your Seed,' who is Christ." The stress on "Seed," not "seeds," is to remind the readers — the faithful in Israel had always recognized that the covenant with Abraham, "I will bless the world by your seed," was not saying all the Jewish people were going to bless the world. One seed, and that Seed is Christ. So he's saying the blessing ultimately comes through that single individual, the Messiah (3:19).

The first verse in the New Testament makes that point. If you open your New Testament, the first verse is this — Matthew chapter 1, verse 1: "The book of the genealogy of Jesus Christ, the Son of David, the Son of Abraham." Why did Matthew start that way? Well, because he's introducing this Messiah. He's introducing the one, the Seed from the Abrahamic covenant, way back there, two thousand years before Christ. So he's introducing Him and saying, Abraham spoke of a seed, and the Seed is here in Messiah.

And the promise was made to Abraham, way back in Genesis chapter 12, verses 1 to 3.

1. God Told Him — Genesis 12:3

There were no requirements placed upon Abraham. God told Abraham — He just said, "Here's what I'm going to do." Now, how many times does God have to say something before it's true? One. One time, right? It doesn't have to say more than once, just once. God says, "This is what I'm going to do." No requirements upon Abraham. He doesn't say to Abraham, "Now you have to do this. If you do this and this and this, then I'll do this for you." Or, "I'm not going to do that unless you do yours. If you stop doing yours, then it's all over, I quit." Not that at all. It was unconditional.

He promised him three things: land, nation, and blessing — and all of those are unconditional.

1) Land — the land is expanded in the Palestinian Covenant, Deuteronomy 29 and 30.

Abrahamic Covenant — 2) Nation — the nation promise is expanded in the Davidic Covenant, 2 Samuel 7.

Genesis 12:1-3

3) Blessing — the blessing promise was, first of all, given through [the Mosaic Covenant — Exodus 19-20]. Problem: the Mosaic Covenant is not an unconditional covenant. The Mosaic Covenant is conditional. And so with the Mosaic Covenant, you say, well, why then was the Mosaic Covenant brought in? We'll see in a minute. But the Mosaic Covenant is there, and then later on, God does away with the Mosaic Covenant. What? Yeah, He does away with it. It's gone. The Mosaic Covenant is no longer here. Can I say that often enough? I'm going to keep on saying it, because that's what the Bible teaches. You're going to see it no clearer anywhere than here in this passage. So the New Covenant comes in and takes the place of the Mosaic Covenant — New Covenant, Jeremiah 31 and Hebrews 8. We've talked about this before.

Now, that's the — Abraham says, "Okay, I heard You. You said You were going to give me a son." If he's going to have a multitude of ancestry, if he's going to bless the world, he's going to have to have at least one son to begin with. You can't have ancestors unless you have a son. You understand, don't you, that in order to have your ancestry as the stars of the heavens, one has to come first — it's like getting olives out of a bottle. The first one has to come out, then the rest of them will come out. He doesn't have one.

2. Abraham Asked for Confirmation — Genesis 15:8

Heifer — goat — ram / dove — pigeon.

And so Abraham asked for a confirmation. He says, "How do I know?" What? You're talking to the God of the universe. But Abraham doesn't know Him very well yet. He believed God, but — okay, but how are You going to show me this? And the way they used to do that in Abraham's day was the cutting of the animals. God said, "Okay, I'll tell you what we'll do. You get yourself a heifer, a goat, and a ram, and cut them in half and lay them out like you do in a man's covenant" — and the dove and the pigeon, don't cut them up, they're too small, just lay them out there.

God put Abram into a deep sleep. Abram could hear what God was saying, but he was asleep — like a vision. And during that time, instead of Abraham walking through here with God, Abraham said, "I'll do this," God said, "I'll do this, I'll do this" — no, Abraham's not there. Where's Abraham? He's asleep on the side. So He's making this point: if you have a covenant among men, you walk through saying, "I'll do this, I'll do this." That's it. But we find in chapter 15 of Genesis that a smoking furnace and a burning lamp passed through the pieces. Those are symbols for God — just God. God the Father saying to God the Son, "This is what we're going to do. I'm going to send You there. You're going to die on the cross. You're going to save men. They'll die — they'll have salvation by faith in Jesus Christ." That's what they were doing. And Abraham didn't have a thing to do. Abraham was just there.

What was Abram doing? Taking a nap. He could not be a party to the covenant! That means that the Abrahamic Covenant was unconditional. He didn't have to do a thing except believe.

And in Hebrews chapter 6, verse 17 — very interesting, this is looking back at that covenant — the writer of Hebrews says God took an oath to Abraham. First of all, God said it, and that's enough. But God, willing to make the covenant even more plain, He swore an oath. He took an oath by Himself, that by these two things, His word and His oath, it might be settled.

Now, did you ever see the movie Oh, God!? What's the guy's name? ... George Burns — thank you. George Burns was God, and he appeared to — whatever — and they came into court, and they say, "Do you say You're God?" He's on the witness stand, and they say, "Do you promise to tell the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth?" What did George Burns, playing God, say? "So help me, Me." You can't swear by anybody higher. And so God, willing to make His covenant sure to them, said, "I'll swear by Myself that this is true." Now, so that's Genesis 12 — He told him. Genesis 15 — He gave him the covenant. So that's the explanation.

C. The Application — 3:17-18

Now, the application — He's saying, why say all this? What's he talking about? He's talking about the fact that the Law has been done away. The Law can't change the Abrahamic Covenant.

1. The Law Came too Late — 3:17
And this I say, that the law, which was four hundred and thirty years later, cannot annul the covenant that was confirmed before by God in Christ, that it should make the promise of no effect. Galatians 3:17

So he says the Law came too late. Verse 17 says this: "And this I say, that the law, which was four hundred and thirty years later, cannot annul the covenant that was confirmed before by God in Christ, that it should make the promise of no effect." It can't do that. The Law came 430 years later. It's not a part of that original thing, and only the parties who made the covenant can change the covenant. So it's impossible — it came too late. The Law was not a party in the Abrahamic Covenant. It came along 430 years later.

2. The Law Contradicted the Promise — 3:18
For if the inheritance is of the law, it is no longer of promise; but God gave it to Abraham by promise. Galatians 3:18

Also, the Law contradicts the promise. For he says, "If the inheritance is of the law, then it's no longer of promise; and God gave it to Abraham by promise." You can't mix promise and the Law together. They are contradictory. See, Abraham's covenant was unconditional, and no requirements from him were needed to produce it. And the Law was conditional.

The Law said: "Do this and you will be blessed."

The Promise says: "You have been blessed, do this."

Conclusion: the Law cannot, did not, and will not change the promise. Just as Abraham believed God and it was counted to him for righteousness — that's Genesis chapter 15, verse 6, God told him that, and then it says Abraham believed God and that was counted to him for righteousness — what is he saying? He's saying that you don't have to do anything except believe. If God says, "I'm going to do this," you believe that — that's all there is to it. You don't have any part of it. You know, that's the way salvation is — that's where he's going with this. Salvation is like that. God has already made salvation; God has already paid, in Jesus Christ, for your sins on the cross. What do you have to do? Well, "you have to do this and this" — there are lists of things like that, that people say you have to do. Wrong. And you know what they do? They leave out "believe." That's it. You believe, you have eternal life. Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him for righteousness. You believe God — "For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son, that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have everlasting life" (John 3:16). Do you believe that? You believe that, and you have eternal life. See, nothing to do. You believe it, just as Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him for righteousness — you believe God, He'll count it for righteousness as well.

So also anyone in any age who believes God has the assurance that God accepts him on the basis of that faith. So some indignant Judaizer might respond with these objections when Paul insisted that the Law:

Could not give the Holy Spirit (vv. 1-5);

Could not bring justification (vv. 6-9);

Could not alter the permanence of faith (vv. 15-18); but does bring a curse (vv. 10-12). (see note below)

Paul's been saying all of that. They might object to that. Well, Paul's going to talk about that as he goes on.

So the Law is not a part of the promise. And he says also, the Law is not on a par with the promise — it's not as high as the promise.

II. The Law is Not on a Par with the Promise — 3:19-20

A. The Law Came because of Disobedience — 3:19a

(The Promise Came because of Obedience)

What purpose then does the law serve? It was added because of transgressions, Galatians 3:19a

The Law came because of disobedience. The promise came because of obedience — Abraham believed God. So what purpose then does the Law serve? It was added because of the transgression, added because of the sin. As we went down from history — down 2,000 years, Abraham; 1,500, Moses — at that time sin was so ripe, they had to have some law codification that says, "Here's what you have to do. Do this, do that, and you'll be — not saved, not justified — but you will be at least in a right relationship with God." You do that in order to, as someone has said, "keep body and soul together." Because many times the Law says, "If you do this, you die" — you know, pick up sticks on the Sabbath, and you'd be killed. So you do that to please God.

The Law was given as a means of checking sin. It served as a restrainer of sins by showing them that transgressing God's Law would bring God's wrath. Keep it from happening — to restrain sin by showing them that transgressing God's Law brings God's wrath.

B. The Law is Temporary; the Promise is Permanent — 3:19b

till the Seed should come to whom the promise was made; Galatians 3:19b

But the Law is also temporary; the promise is permanent. The Law, the entire Law, was given only until the Seed, to whom the promise was given (v. 16), should come. The Law was temporary — it says, "until the Seed should come." Who's the Seed? Jesus Christ. The Law came about 1500 BC, until Christ came, until the Seed came. And when Christ died on the cross, the Law died. What? The Law died. The Law is no more.

I know someone came to me and asked this question, and I was so happy, because that person had been listening, thinking: "Well, if the Law's done away, didn't the Law say don't steal? Is it now okay to steal?" Very good question. And I said, "No, no." The Law, "thou shalt not steal," is also in the New Testament. The New Covenant says, "Don't steal." The New Covenant says, "Don't commit adultery." All ten of the commandments are reiterated, restated in the New Covenant, the New Testament — except for one. If you don't understand this, you should have been here yesterday. The fourth commandment is, "Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy." Not in the New Testament, not in the New Covenant. It's not a part of the New Covenant. It was a part of the Old Covenant. So we don't keep that one. And unless you understand the Law has been done away, you should have been here yesterday with your animal, so I could kill it. I was here — nobody came. Why? Because you understand the Law has been done away.

C. The Law Required a Mediator; The Promise has None — 3:19c-20

So the Law was brought by disobedience, the Law was temporary, and the Law required a mediator, in verses 19c and 20.

1. The Law had a Mediator — 3:19c
and it was appointed through angels by the hand of a mediator. Galatians 3:19c

Why do you have to have a mediator? Well, some here say two mediators — the angels and Moses. I think not; I think that Moses was the mediator. Remember, up on Mount Sinai, God gave the Ten Commandments to Moses, and Moses brought them down to the people. There was a mediator between God and the people. That was it, because you had two parties — you have God, you have the people, and you have to have a mediator between the two. In any case, the Law was not a direct revelation as the promise was.

2. The Promise has No Mediator — 3:20
Now a mediator does not mediate for one only, but God is one. Galatians 3:20

Now, the promise — he says in verse 20, the promise had no mediator. He says, "Now, a mediator does not mediate for only one, but God is one." So there was no mediator in the promise. God just spoke to Abraham — no mediator between them. Well, wait a minute — God spoke to Abraham, no mediator? Why? Well, because the promise is not conditional. The promise is unconditional. And so, no mediator, because God is one. God made it. Remember, God's walking through here — the Father and the Son walking through. Abraham is not a part of it. There is no mediator because God is one. There's no mediator; He didn't need one. God said, "I'm going to do this," and that's it. Once God said "I'm going to do it," that's all there is to it. So He's saying, "I'm going to do this for you." Remember that the two persons walking through the slain animals were the Father and the Son, one God. In effect they were saying, "This is what we will do."

So the Law is not a part of the promise. The Law is not on a par with the promise. And the Law had a purpose in the promise. The Law is not totally irrelevant, in other words.

III. The Law Had a Purpose in the Promise — 3:21-25

A. The Law was Given to Demonstrate Man's Weakness — 3:21

Is the law then against the promises of God? Certainly not! {μὴ γένοιτο} For if there had been a law given which could have given life, truly righteousness would have been by the law. Galatians 3:21

The Law then — verse 21 says, "Is the law then against the promise of God? Certainly not!" One of my favorite expressions in Greek — μὴ γένοιτο. Now, don't say it wrong — the Greeks say it this way. Sometime learn that, so that if your husband says, "Let's do this," and you say, "Mē genoito," he'll know that you mean no way, not going to happen. That's what he's saying here. Is the Law against the promises of God? No. The Law had a part in that. "For if there had been a law given which could have given life, truly righteousness would have been by the law." So he's saying, if God had given man a part in salvation, then man couldn't do it.

You know, some people would think that when we get to heaven, God is going to have a big scale up there, and all your good deeds go on this side and all the bad deeds go on that side, and if the good deeds are more, they go down — and, well, I think maybe it would go the other way. But in any case, even if God said, "Okay, I'll judge you on your good works" — remember, He requires perfection. How many here are going to make it? You're not going to make it, I'll guarantee you — I'm not either, of course. Nobody, because man couldn't perform it if God had done it that way. God said, "No, I'll do it, I'll take care of it." Do you believe that? Okay, that's it.

"And you He made alive, who were dead in trespasses and sins, ... and were by nature children of wrath" (Ephesians 2:1, 3). You can't do anything to please God. All your righteousness — Isaiah says — are as filthy rags (Isaiah 64:6). You have nothing on the good side. You have all on the bad side, because everything righteous you try to do, it's not good — it's as filthy rags to God. So the Law was given to demonstrate man's weakness. You can't do it.

B. The Law was Given to Display Man's Sinfulness — 3:22a

But the Scripture has confined all under sin, Galatians 3:22a

And the Law was given to display man's sinfulness. "But the Scripture has confined all under sin." The whole world is trapped under the dominion of sin. Romans 3:9 says this: "For we have previously charged both Jews and Greeks that they are all under sin." Everybody. Everybody's under sin, no matter who you are.

Romans 3:23 — we know that one, don't we? We don't know it quite the way it ought to be, but you know part of it. You've probably heard it said, "For all have sinned and come short of the glory of God." Wrong. What? "All sinned" — not "all have sinned." All sinned, and are coming short of the glory of God.

for {have} sinned and fall short of the glory of God," (see also 5:12) Romans 3:23

What's the difference? Well, what it means is this: all sinned — when? Let's look at it as one event. It happened at one time. When did it happen? When did all sin — in Adam? Whoa — in Adam. When Adam sinned, we all sinned. That's what he's saying. He said the same thing in Romans 5:12. All sinned — when Adam sinned, you sinned. So we're all under sin. Hebrews — sometimes read Hebrews 7:16 and 17; that explains that a little bit. But we all sin — not all have committed sin, but all sinned in Adam. Then he says we then continually fall short of the glory of God — that's personal sin.

Now, here's a word: you are not a sinner because you sin. You sin because you're a sinner. You started out that way. You were born that way. You sin. So the Law displayed that. The Law showed that every person is a sinner.

This is not saying that "all have committed sin." It is saying that "all sinned in Adam." Then he adds that all continually fall short of God's standard — His glory. That is personal sin. You are not a sinner because you sin. You sin because you are a sinner. The Law displayed that.

C. The Law Was Given To Drive Man to Christ — 3:22b-25

that the promise by faith in Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe. Galatians 3:22b

Now, so the Law was given to demonstrate man's weakness. It was given to demonstrate man's sinfulness. And the Law was also given to drive man to Christ. It says this — the Law was given "that the promise by faith in Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe."

But before faith came, we were kept under guard by the law, kept for the faith which would afterward be revealed. Therefore the law was our tutor to bring us to Christ, that we might be justified by faith. But after faith has come, we are no longer under a tutor. Galatians 3:23-25

Verse 23 says: "But before faith came, we were kept under guard by the law, kept for the faith which would afterward be revealed. Therefore, the law was our tutor, our teacher, to bring us to Christ, that we might be justified by faith. But after faith has come, we are no longer under the tutor, no longer under the Law." Now, he'll give more about this in chapter 4. But what he's saying is that Israel — not we Gentiles, but Israel — was under the Law, way back here, under the Law, 1,500 years. Israel was under this tutor to teach them — teach them, "You're a sinner. And if you will turn to Jesus Christ, to God in Christ, then you will receive salvation. You're a sinner, you're disobedient, and you have to go to Christ in order to be justified." That's what he's saying.

Knowing that we are sinners, we are now in a place to receive salvation by believing — just as Abraham did. So knowing that we're sinners, then, we're now in a place to receive salvation. You can't be saved until you know you need to be saved. So just as Abraham did — Abraham believed God, it was counted to him for righteousness. So Abraham was declared righteous by faith. You know what that means, "declared righteous"? It means that Abraham believed God, even though he wasn't righteous personally — he believed God, and God said, "Okay, I'll count that as righteousness. You are My friend, you are in relationship with Me, because you believe God, you believe Me." And this is what happens at salvation. At salvation, when you believe that Jesus Christ died in your place on the cross, you believe Him — when you do that, God says, "Okay, you're righteous." It's a bookkeeping term — credit and debit. All you have is debits, and God gives you a credit that covers all the debits. That's what he's saying.

"God made Him, Jesus Christ, the one who knew no sin, to be sin for us, in order that we might be made the righteousness of God in Him" (2 Corinthians 5:21). What happened? Well, when I believed on Jesus Christ, He took my sin — all my sin — and placed that on Jesus Christ. My sin is paid for on the cross. He took His righteousness and placed it on me. So now I'm righteous. You want to see a righteous person? Here I am! Because I do righteous things? No. But because I have Jesus Christ as my Savior — He died in my place on the cross, He took my sins, He gave me His righteousness. And everyone who believes in Jesus Christ gets that righteousness. See? That's it.

Abraham was declared Righteous by faith; so are we! What do we have to do to be saved? Believe — or else throw away the Gospel of John. And that is the only way anyone can be saved, and we are driven to that conclusion by the Law.

And so we are all sons of God through faith. So now that faith has come, we're no longer under that tutor. The Law is not a part of the promise. The Law is not on a par with the promise. The Law had a purpose in the promise. Now, one more thing: the Law's prospects were inferior to the promise.

IV. The Law's Prospects were Inferior to the Promise — 3:26-29

The prospects of the promise are these — first, it has higher promises.

A. Higher — 3:26

For you are all sons of God through faith in Christ Jesus. Galatians 3:26

Verse 26: "For you are all sons of God through faith in Jesus Christ." We're all sons of God through faith which is in Christ Jesus. Now, that's pretty high. If you were sons of the devil — excuse me for saying so — now you're sons of God. That's higher. The Law could not get you up there. The Law could get you excluded from the wrath of God, but the Law can't get you up there as sons of God. You're all sons of God through faith in Jesus Christ. Under the Law we — Israelites — were children. Now we are all sons of God. They were in the household of Israel, but they weren't in God's elite group, which reaches to the throne of God. You just can't get much higher than that.

"Sons of God" implies inheritance — Paul uses that term in two ways:

1] Passive inheritance, which is possessed by all believers. It includes eternal life, entrance into the Kingdom, justification, sonship, and so forth. Ephesians 1 tells us this — we are blessed with all the spiritual blessings in the heavenlies in Christ (Ephesians 1:3).

2] Active inheritance, which is possessed by faithful believers. It includes rulership with Christ — if we suffer with Him, we'll reign with Him — and all the privileges that go with it. That's earned; that is earned when we serve the Lord Jesus Christ.

Paul uses the former in Galatians except in 5:19-20, and possibly 6:7-9 (cf. Galatians 4:5; Ephesians 1:14).

B. Deeper — 3:27

For as many of you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ. Galatians 3:27

It's also deeper. "As many of you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ." Baptism here is certainly Spirit baptism. We do not enter the church, the family of God, by water baptism — we enter by Spirit baptism. "By one Spirit we are all baptized into one body" (1 Corinthians 12:13). That's Spirit baptism.

This relationship is deeper than any other relationship. Jesus said — remember, they said to Him, "Your mother and Your brothers are outside," and He looked around at the people who were with Him, His disciples, and said, "Here are My mother and My brothers" (Matthew 12:49). That's the relationship we have with Him. We are brothers with Christ.

C. Wider — 3:28

There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is neither male nor female; for you are all one in Christ Jesus. Galatians 3:28

Now it's much deeper, but it's also wider. Verse 28 says, "There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is neither male nor female, but you are all one in Christ Jesus." Wow — all one. All of us who know Jesus Christ as Savior are in one group.

No national differences: Jew/Greek. There's no national difference — Greek or Jew, no difference. Now, it doesn't mean that they stop being Jewish or stop being Greek — they remain Jew and Greek, but they have no special place with God, no matter what nationality, what color, what creed, or whatever you have. You are no better with God than the one next to you.

No social differences: bond or free. Back at that time they had slaves, and the slaves were in the church, but the slave and the owner are on the same level. Philemon is a good example of that. Both slave and owner are on the same level.

No sexual differences: male or female. The males are regarded no higher than females. So the ground is level — just as the ground is level when you come to Christ, just so the ground remains level in the church.

Now, you might ask, "Wait a minute — we don't have women preachers." Well, I didn't say that the equality means there's no difference in station in the church. It's like in the home — there's one male, one female, hopefully; one is the leader. The husband is the head of the home. Why? Because he's a man? No — because God said he's the head. And so there are different ways in which we serve the Lord, but we are no different with the Lord. We are still on one level there.

For who makes you differ from another? And what do you have that you did not receive? Now if you did indeed receive it, why do you boast as if you had not received it? 1 Corinthians 4:7

D. Longer — 3:29

And if you are Christ's, then you are Abraham's seed, and heirs according to the promise. Galatians 3:29

And then verse 29 says, it's also longer. "If you are Christ's, then you are Abraham's seed, and heirs according to the promise." The Law went from about 1450 or so up to the cross — 1500 years total. That's not very far. But he says, concerning the promise, Abraham's seed — we're Abraham's seed way back to Genesis 12, and all the way as far as you can see, as far as you can imagine — forever. Because Abraham's seed are the sons of God.

Now, all who are of faith belong to Christ. All who belong to Christ are Abraham's seed. They are also heirs according to the promise. Heirs — this again refers to the passive inheritance, which has been granted to the sons by adoption (cf. Galatians 4:5; Ephesians 1:14).

Conclusion

The Law has been done away, but the Law was important. It was important to bring us to Christ, to show our sinfulness, to show our need for a Savior, and then to show who that Savior is — Jesus Christ. So we're not putting down the Law. The Law was holy, it's good and righteous, but it did its job, and now it's gone. We are under grace. And the salvation that we have is salvation by grace, by grace through faith, plus not one thing. That's his point.

Note: Walvoord, J. F., Zuck, R. B., & Dallas Theological Seminary. (1983–c1985). *The Bible Knowledge Commentary: An Exposition of the Scriptures* (2:599). Wheaton, IL: Victor Books.

Part 2 — Study Questions

1. Paul says a confirmed human covenant cannot be added to or annulled (3:15). Why is this such a devastating argument against the Judaizers’ claim that the Law added conditions to God’s promise to Abraham?

Show Leader Answer

Paul reasons from the lesser to the greater. If even human contracts are legally untouchable by a third party once both signers have confirmed them, then God’s own covenant — confirmed by His oath alone — is even more untouchable. The Law was never one of the original parties, so it has no legal standing to modify the promise. This exposes the Judaizers’ position as not just theologically wrong but logically incoherent by the standards of ordinary human agreements.

2. What does it mean that Abraham was asleep while God alone passed through the pieces of the covenant animals (Genesis 15)?

Show Leader Answer

In a normal ancient Near Eastern covenant ceremony, both parties walked through the pieces, each pledging their own obligations. Here, only God — pictured as the smoking furnace and burning lamp, the Father and the Son — passed through. Abraham took no oath and made no pledge; he was asleep. This dramatizes that the Abrahamic covenant was entirely unilateral and unconditional: God bound only Himself, and Abraham’s sole part was to believe.

3. Paul says the Law was "added because of transgressions" and functioned as a "tutor" (paidagōgos) until Christ came (3:19, 24). What was the Law’s God-given purpose, and what was it never meant to do?

Show Leader Answer

The Law was meant to define sin as chargeable transgression, restrain outward sin, and expose humanity’s helplessness and guilt — driving people to see their need for a Savior. It was never meant to impart life or righteousness (3:21) — only a law that could give life could justify, and none can. Like a household guardian who escorts a child to school and enforces discipline until the child comes of age, the Law’s custody was always meant to be temporary, ending when faith in Christ was revealed.

4. Explain the difference between "all have sinned" and "all sinned" as the sermon draws it out from Romans 3:23 and 5:12.

Show Leader Answer

"All sinned" (a completed past action) points to one representative event — humanity’s fall in Adam, in whom every person is reckoned to have sinned. "Continually fall short of the glory of God" describes the ongoing, personal sin that flows from that fallen nature. The point: a person does not become a sinner by sinning; a person sins because they are already a sinner by nature, inherited from Adam. Both truths — inherited guilt and personal practice — are held together under "confined... under sin" (3:22).

5. Galatians 3:26-29 lists four ways believers’ standing in Christ surpasses anything the Law offered (higher, deeper, wider, longer). Which of these four is most meaningful to you right now, and why?

Show Leader Answer

Answers will vary. Guide the group to connect each: higher (sonship, not mere servanthood under the Law); deeper (Spirit-baptism uniting us to Christ more intimately than any other bond, even family); wider (no barrier of nationality, class, or sex changes anyone’s standing before God); longer (an inheritance reaching back to Abraham and forward without end, unlike the Law’s brief 1,500-year span). Encourage personal, specific application rather than only doctrinal restatement.

⑤ Application

Closes the study · Personal and corporate application

Rest in “You Have Been Blessed, Do This” — Not “Do This and You Will Be Blessed”

Galatians 3:18 · Source: Commentary, §I.C.2

Every performance-based approach to God — even a subtle, well-meaning one — quietly rewrites the promise back into a law. Paul will not allow the two to mix: inheritance is either of the Law or of promise, never both. If your daily sense of standing with God rises and falls with how your obedience went that day, you have slipped out of promise-thinking and back into law-thinking, however sincerely.

Reflection: Where this week did you treat God’s acceptance of you as something you had to earn back after a bad day, rather than a settled fact you were living out of?

Practical Step: Before you pray tonight, say out loud, “I have already been blessed” before you say anything you plan to do for God — and notice which one felt more natural to say first.

Let the Law Finish Its Work — Don’t Argue With Your Guilt, Let It Point You to Christ

Galatians 3:24 · Source: Commentary, §III.C

The paidagōgos — the household guardian who walked a child to school — was never meant to be loved, only obeyed until the child came of age. In the same way, the Law’s exposure of your sin is not meant to be argued with, minimized, or outrun. It is meant to do exactly what it did for Israel: escort you to Christ, then step aside.

Reflection: Is there a specific sin you keep trying to manage or explain away rather than letting it drive you back to the cross?

Practical Step: Name that sin honestly to God this week — not to earn forgiveness, but because you already have it in Christ, and confession is simply agreeing with what the Law was always trying to show you.

Live the Wide Ground — Treat No Believer as Having Less Standing at the Cross

Galatians 3:28 · Source: Commentary, §IV.C

Jew and Greek, slave and free, male and female — Paul names the three deepest social divides of his world and levels them all at the cross. This does not erase distinctions of role or gifting, but it does erase any distinction in standing before God. Whatever hierarchy your culture assigns, it has no vote in how God views a fellow believer.

Reflection: Whom do you unconsciously treat as “less” — spiritually, socially, or in your own estimation — even though Christ has made you equally His?

Practical Step: This week, deliberately extend a specific honor (a word of encouragement, an invitation, a request for their input) to a believer you might ordinarily overlook.

Hold the Longer View — Your Inheritance Outlasts Every Law-Shaped Season of Life

Galatians 3:29 · Source: Commentary, §IV.D; FGR.docx

The Law’s reign lasted about 1,500 years — remarkable in human terms, but brief against the promise, which reaches back to Abraham and forward “as far as you can imagine.” As Abraham’s seed and heirs according to the promise, believers possess (per the Free Grace framework in FGR.docx) a passive inheritance — eternal life, sonship, and standing — that is settled the moment faith is placed in Christ, entirely apart from ongoing performance.

Reflection: Are you living out of the confidence of a forever inheritance, or out of the anxiety of a temporary, Law-shaped scorecard?

Practical Step: Write down one decision facing you this month, and make it in light of eternity rather than in light of this week’s pressures.
