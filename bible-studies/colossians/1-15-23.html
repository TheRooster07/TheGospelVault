① The Biblical Text

NASB95 · Colossians 1:15–23

1:15 He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation.

16 For by Him all things were created, both in the heavens and on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities—all things have been created through Him and for Him.

17 He is before all things, and in Him all things hold together.

19 For it was the Father’s good pleasure for all the fullness to dwell in Him,

20 and through Him to reconcile all things to Himself, having made peace through the blood of His cross; through Him, I say, whether things on earth or things in heaven.

22 yet He has now reconciled you in His fleshly body through death, in order to present you before Him holy and blameless and beyond reproach—

② Devotional

Personal preparation · All group members

Who Is This Jesus?

“so that He Himself will come to have first place in everything” — Colossians 1:18 (NASB95)

Every age asks the same question: who is Jesus? The false teachers in Colossae had an answer. He was important, perhaps, but He was one spiritual power among many, a step on a ladder toward God. Paul's answer is one of the most breathtaking passages in all of Scripture. Jesus is the image of the invisible God. He made everything, including the angels the false teachers revered, and He holds everything together right now.

This same Jesus is Head of the Church and the first to rise from the dead never to die again. All the fullness of God dwells in Him. And this exalted Lord came near. He made peace through the blood of His cross, and He reconciled people like us, once alienated and hostile in mind, in His real body through a real death.

Why did He do it? To present us before Him holy and blameless. Reconciliation is His gift, settled at the cross. Being presented complete is the goal He is working toward in us, as we continue steady in the faith and are not moved away from the hope of the gospel.

Reflect

  1. Is there any area of your life where Jesus does not yet have "first place"?
  2. How does knowing that Christ holds "all things" together change the way you face this week?
  3. What might it look like for you to stay "firmly established and steadfast" in the hope of the gospel?

He is before all things, and in Him all things hold together, including you.

Introduction

Paul has just spoken of "the kingdom of His beloved Son, in whom we have redemption" (1:13–14). Now he tells us who that Son is. Against a teaching that made Christ one link in a chain of spiritual powers between God and man, Paul gives one of the greatest statements of Christ's deity and supremacy found anywhere in the Bible. Many believe verses 15–20 may have been an early hymn of the Church, but whatever its form, the content is clear: Christ is first in creation, first in the Church, and the only way of reconciliation.

I. Christ and Creation (1:15–17)

A. He Is the Picture of God (1:15a)

"He is the image of the invisible God." How can anyone be the image (eikōn) of a God who is invisible? An image implies both representation and manifestation. When man was created in the image of God, that image included qualities like holiness and righteousness, which cannot be seen with the eye. But the Son became visible. In Him the invisible God has shown us what He is like.

“And the Word became flesh, and dwelt among us, and we saw His glory, glory as of the only begotten from the Father, full of grace and truth.”
— John 1:14 (NASB95)
“Jesus said to him, “Have I been so long with you, and yet you have not come to know Me, Philip? He who has seen Me has seen the Father; how can you say, ‘Show us the Father’?”
— John 14:9 (NASB95)

B. He Has Priority over Creation (1:15b)

"The firstborn of all creation." Some groups seize on this phrase to argue that Christ was the first being God created. That reading collides with the very next verse, which says all things were created by Him. "Firstborn" (prōtotokos) speaks of priority and preeminence, the rank and rights of the heir. God said of David, who was the youngest of his brothers, "I also shall make him My firstborn, the highest of the kings of the earth" (Ps. 89:27). The title is about status, not origin.

It helps to compare "firstborn" with "only begotten" (monogenēs, John 3:16). "Only begotten" stresses uniqueness. Isaac is called Abraham's "only begotten son" (Heb. 11:17), even though Abraham had other sons (Gen. 25:1–2). Isaac was unique as the son of promise through Sarah. Jesus is the unique Son, and He is the Firstborn who holds first rank over all creation.

C. He Is the Creator of the Universe (1:16)

"For by Him all things were created." The Son is the Creator of all things (John 1:3; Heb. 1:2). Paul then sweeps through the whole of creation:

  • In the heavens and on earth. There is nothing "out there" that lies beyond His authority.
  • Visible and invisible. Even what we cannot see is His handiwork and under His control.
  • Thrones, dominions, rulers, authorities. These describe the ranks of the angelic world. The list is not exhaustive, but it covers the angelic sphere in a general way.

That last point would have hit the Colossian heresy hard. The very angels they were tempted to worship (2:18) were created by Christ. They belong in the same category as sticks and stones: creatures. Then Paul adds, "all things have been created through Him and for Him." He is the agent of creation and its goal. Compare what Paul says of the Lord in Romans 11:36: "For from Him and through Him and to Him are all things." What is said of God is said of Christ.

D. He Is the Sustainer of the Universe (1:17)

"He is before all things, and in Him all things hold together." Someone might ask, "What if creation runs down, or rebels, or flies apart?" The answer is that the One who made the universe still upholds it. The verb (synistēmi, perfect tense) means to stand together, to cohere. The universe is not held together by chance or by impersonal forces alone. It holds together in Christ.

II. Christ and the Church (1:18–20)

A. He Is the Head of the Body (1:18a)

"He is also head of the body, the church." Here "the church" is the universal body of all believers. Every believer is baptized by the Holy Spirit into that body the moment he believes (1 Cor. 12:13). This work of the Spirit began on the Day of Pentecost (Acts 1:5; 2:1–4; 11:15–16). The Church is "neither Jew nor Greek" (Gal. 3:28) but a new creation of God, "one new man" (Eph. 2:15). It is the "mystery" not made known in earlier generations (Eph. 3:4–6; Col. 1:26). And Christ is its Head, its source of life, authority, and direction.

B. He Is the Firstborn from the Dead (1:18b)

"And He is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead." Others had been raised from the dead before, but they all died again. Christ was the first to rise to resurrection life, never to die again. His resurrection marked the defeat of death and the one who held its power:

“Therefore, since the children share in flesh and blood, He Himself likewise also partook of the same, that through death He might render powerless him who had the power of death, that is, the devil, and might free those who through fear of death were subject to slavery all their lives.”
— Hebrews 2:14–15 (NASB95)
“For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive. But each in his own order: Christ the first fruits, after that those who are Christ’s at His coming,”
— 1 Corinthians 15:22–23 (NASB95)

The purpose is stated plainly: "so that He Himself will come to have first place in everything." First in creation, first in resurrection, first in the Church, first in everything.

C. He Is the Fullness of God (1:19)

"For it was the Father's good pleasure for all the fullness to dwell in Him." The false teachers likely used "fullness" (plērōma) for the whole range of spiritual powers between God and man. Paul says the fullness is not spread out among angels; it dwells in Christ. The verb (katoikeō) means to settle down and be at home permanently. All that the Father is, the Son is. Paul will say it again even more plainly in 2:9.

D. He Is the Reconciler Through the Cross (1:20)

"And through Him to reconcile all things to Himself, having made peace through the blood of His cross." Paul obviously does not mean that everyone will be saved. Only those who believe in Christ are reconciled to God and receive eternal life (John 3:16, 36). "All things" refers to the whole created order being brought back under its rightful Head. The earth itself will be redeemed and restored to its intended purpose (Rom. 8:19–22). Those who refuse the salvation Christ secured will face judgment. And all of this is accomplished "through the blood of His cross," His sacrificial death.

Notice also the direction of reconciliation. It is man who is reconciled to God, not God to man. God did not need to change; we did.

III. Christ and the Cross (1:21–23)

A. The Requirement of the Cross (1:21)

"And although you were formerly alienated and hostile in mind, engaged in evil deeds." This is the condition of every person before Christ: alienated in relationship, hostile in attitude, and evil in action. Christ reconciles sinners, not righteous people. No one is too sinful to receive it, and no one is too righteous to need it.

B. The Remedy of the Cross (1:22a)

"Yet He has now reconciled you." The atonement is presented in Scripture from three directions:

  • Redemption is the sin-ward aspect: the price paid to free us (Matt. 20:28).
  • Reconciliation is the man-ward aspect: the barrier removed so we are brought back to God (2 Cor. 5:18–20).
  • Propitiation is the God-ward aspect: God's justice satisfied (1 John 2:2).

And notice the means: "in His fleshly body through death." Gnostics, take notice! It was not a phantom body, and it was not a mere man. The eternal Son died in a real body of flesh.

C. The Reason for the Cross (1:22b)

"In order to present you before Him holy and blameless and beyond reproach." These words can describe the believer's position before God, which is already perfect in Christ (Rom. 8:1; Eph. 1:4), or the believer's condition, his actual spiritual maturity. God envisions both for His children: holy and blameless before Him (Eph. 1:4) and conformed to the image of His Son (Rom. 8:29).

In this context Paul appears to be speaking of the believer's spiritual condition and of a future presentation. Paul uses the same verb, "present" (paristēmi), in 1:28 for presenting every believer "complete in Christ," and in 2 Corinthians 11:2 of presenting the church as a pure virgin. This presentation looks ahead to the Judgment Seat of Christ, where believers' lives and works will be evaluated (2 Cor. 5:10). Not every believer will be presented in this way. The letters to the churches in Revelation 2–3 make clear that not every believer is an overcomer.

D. The Condition for Being Presented (1:23)

"If indeed you continue in the faith firmly established and steadfast, and not moved away from the hope of the gospel that you have heard." Here is a crucial verse, and it needs to be read carefully.

  • The "if" clause is a first class condition (ei ge), which assumes the condition for the sake of argument. Paul expresses confidence that the Colossians will continue, though that is his expectation and earnest hope, not a guarantee about every individual.
  • The condition modifies "present," not "reconciled." Reconciliation is already accomplished: "He has now reconciled you" (v.22). That is settled, past, and permanent. The condition attaches to the purpose clause: being presented holy, blameless, and beyond reproach.
  • Paul is not making eternal salvation conditional on perseverance. One does not have to live a perfect life, or even a persevering life, to be reconciled to God. Remember John 3:16. But believers do need to continue in the faith, grounded and steadfast, in order to be presented in this way at the Judgment Seat of Christ.

Free Grace interpreters such as Joseph Dillow, in The Reign of the Servant Kings, have argued at length that verses like this one concern the believer's future reward and approval, not whether he possesses eternal life. That is the reading followed here: the gift is secure; the presentation is the prize.

Paul closes the section by saying that this gospel "was proclaimed in all creation under heaven, and of which I, Paul, was made a minister." That had obviously not happened in the literal sense of every individual. Some take it as a deliberate overstatement to show how far the gospel had already gone. Others understand it to mean that the gospel had gone to all without distinction, to every kind of people, though not yet to all without exception. Either way, Paul's point is that the Colossians' gospel was not a local novelty but the one worldwide message.

Conclusion

Who is Jesus? He is the image of the invisible God, the Firstborn over creation, the Creator of all things, including the angels, and the One in whom all things hold together. He is the Head of the Church, the first to rise from the dead, the dwelling place of all the fullness of God, and the One who made peace through the blood of His cross.

If He is all that, then He is enough. We do not need to climb a ladder of angels or add a secret knowledge to reach God. Through His death in a real body, believers are already reconciled. The question now is whether we will continue firmly established and steadfast, so that He may present us holy, blameless, and beyond reproach in that day.

Lord, thank You.

Part 2 — Study Questions

1. How does verse 16 answer those who say "firstborn" means Christ was the first created being?

Show Leader Note

Verse 16 says all things were created by Him, through Him, and for Him, including all invisible powers. If He created all things, He cannot Himself be a created thing. "Firstborn" denotes rank, as with David (Ps. 89:27).

2. Why would Paul emphasize that Christ created the angelic ranks?

Show Leader Note

The false teachers promoted the worship of angels as mediators (2:18). If Christ created thrones, dominions, rulers, and authorities, they are His creatures, and no believer needs them to reach God.

3. Does "reconcile all things" (v.20) teach that everyone will be saved? Explain.

Show Leader Note

No. Only believers are reconciled to God and receive eternal life (John 3:16, 36). "All things" refers to the created order restored under its rightful Head; those who reject Christ face judgment.

4. Why does Paul stress "in His fleshly body through death" (v.22)?

Show Leader Note

To refute the error that Christ had no real body (Docetism) and to show that a real death in a real body accomplished reconciliation.

5. According to this study, what is conditional in verses 22–23, and what is not?

Show Leader Note

Reconciliation is not conditional; "He has now reconciled you" is complete. What is conditional is being presented holy, blameless, and beyond reproach, which depends on continuing firmly established in the faith. That presentation is linked with the Judgment Seat of Christ, a matter of reward, not of eternal life.

⑤ Application

Closes the study · Personal & corporate

Application 1 — Give Christ First Place

Colossians 1:18

Draws from ③ Commentary, II.B, and ② Devotional

God's purpose is that Christ "will come to have first place in everything." He already has first place in creation and in the resurrection. The question is whether He has first place in our schedules, our money, our relationships, and our thoughts.

Reflection: Where in your life does Jesus share first place with something else?

Practical Step: Identify one area where Christ does not have first place and make one concrete change this week.

Application 2 — Rest in the One Who Holds Things Together

Colossians 1:17

Draws from ③ Commentary, I.D

When life feels like it is coming apart, remember that "in Him all things hold together." The same Lord who sustains galaxies sustains His people. Trust is not denial of trouble; it is confidence in the One who holds it.

Reflection: What feels most "unglued" in your life right now?

Practical Step: Pray verse 17 over that situation each day, thanking Christ that He is holding it together.

Application 3 — Reconciled Now, Presented Then

Colossians 1:22–23; 2 Corinthians 5:10

Draws from ③ Commentary, III.C–D; cf. Dillow, The Reign of the Servant Kings (FGR)

Your reconciliation is settled. Nothing you do or fail to do can undo it. But the Lord also desires to present you holy, blameless, and beyond reproach, and that presentation depends on continuing steady in the faith. Assurance and accountability belong together: we serve not to be saved but because we are saved and will one day give an account.

Reflection: Is there anything currently moving you away from "the hope of the gospel"?

Practical Step: Name one influence that weakens your steadiness in the faith and take one step to limit it this week.

End of Study · Colossians 1:15–23
