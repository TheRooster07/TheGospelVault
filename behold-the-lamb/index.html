Vault 1 — The Gospel

Behold the Lamb

A Gospel presentation from John 16. Jesus said the Holy Spirit would convict the world concerning sin, righteousness and judgment — and each one points to Jesus as the answer. The plain and simple truth of God’s promise in the Gospel: “believe” is the one word that describes it best.

Back to Vault 1 — The Gospel Next: God’s Free Gift (6 Panels)

Why the Spirit Came

On the night before the cross, Jesus told His disciples that when the Holy Spirit came, "He will convict the world concerning sin and righteousness and judgment" (John 16:8). Then He said what the Spirit's whole aim would be: "He will glorify Me" (16:14).

The Spirit doesn't convict people just so they see their guilt. He does it to bring them to Jesus. Each point below raises a problem, and each one is answered by a Person.

1. Sin: The Problem Is Unbelief

John 16:9 — “concerning sin, because they do not believe in Me”

Jesus does not list our wrongs here, though we all have plenty. He names the one sin that keeps a person from eternal life: not believing in Him. "Unless you believe that I am He, you will die in your sins" (8:24).

The answer is the Lamb. On the night of the first Passover, God told Israel, "when I see the blood I will pass over you" (Exodus 12:13). Judgment came to every house in Egypt that night. What saved the people inside was not how good they were. It was the blood on the door.

Paul tells us who that lamb pictured: "For Christ our Passover also has been sacrificed" (1 Corinthians 5:7). John the Baptist pointed to Him: "Behold, the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!" (John 1:29). On the cross Jesus said, "It is finished!" (19:30). The price was paid in full. The question is no longer "What will I do about my sin?" It is "Will I believe the One who took it away?"

2. Righteousness: The Standard Is Jesus

John 16:10 — “concerning righteousness, because I go to the Father and you no longer see Me”

God accepts only perfect righteousness, and none of us has it. Jesus went back to the Father because He was the Righteous One. The Father raised Him and received Him, which proved that His life was perfect and His sacrifice was accepted.

The answer is the I AM. Jesus is not just a good teacher. "Before Abraham was born, I am" (8:58). He took the name God gave Himself at the burning bush. The eternal God became a man, lived the righteous life we couldn't live, and gives His life to anyone who believes.

"I am the resurrection and the life; he who believes in Me will live even if he dies, and everyone who lives and believes in Me will never die" (11:25–26). Then He asks Martha the question He asks each of us: "Do you believe this?"

3. Judgment: The Verdict Has Already Fallen

John 16:11 — “concerning judgment, because the ruler of this world has been judged”

At the cross, Satan was judged and defeated. That shows that God's judgment is real and certain. If the ruler of this world couldn't escape it, no one will escape it by their own effort.

The answer is a promise. "Truly, truly, I say to you, he who hears My word, and believes Him who sent Me, has eternal life, and does not come into judgment, but has passed out of death into life" (5:24). The believer has eternal life now. He does not come into judgment. He has passed out of death into life. It's already done.

4. The Judgment to Come: Real, but Not for Those Who Believe

God's heart comes first. "For God did not send the Son into the world to judge the world, but that the world might be saved through Him" (John 3:17). Even so, a line runs through every life: "He who believes in the Son has eternal life; but he who does not obey the Son will not see life, but the wrath of God abides on him" (3:36). For "not obey" the NKJV reads "does not believe." The Greek word means to refuse to be persuaded, and the verse sets it as the opposite of believing.

It is a real appointment. Paul spoke to Felix "about faith in Christ Jesus" and about "the judgment to come." Felix was frightened, but he put it off: "Go away for the present" (Acts 24:24–25). John saw where that appointment ends: "a great white throne" (Revelation 20:11), and "if anyone's name was not found written in the book of life, he was thrown into the lake of fire" (20:15). Hell is real. But the one who believes in Jesus will never stand there, because he "does not come into judgment" (John 5:24).

The Invitation: A Gift, Not a Wage

"For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord" (Romans 6:23). We earn wages, but a gift can only be received. Heaven isn't paid for by our efforts, our promises or our improvement. Jesus paid for it, just as the lamb's blood was enough for every house that had it on the door.

"For God so loved the world, that He gave His only begotten Son, that whoever believes in Him shall not perish, but have eternal life" (John 3:16). Jesus made it as simple as it can be: "Truly, truly, I say to you, he who believes has eternal life" (6:47).

To believe is to be persuaded that Jesus is telling the truth, and to trust Him, not yourself, for the eternal life He promises. Living as His disciple comes after, as the response of a person who already has life. It is never the price of it.

Your Decision of Faith

John says why he wrote his whole Gospel: "these have been written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing you may have life in His name" (John 20:31).

The Spirit has shown you the sin of unbelief, the righteousness of Jesus, and the certainty of judgment, and in each case He has pointed you to the Lamb, the I AM. No one can make this decision for you, and no one can avoid making it. Like Felix, you can put it off, but putting it off is still a decision.

Jesus asks you what He also asked Martha: "Do you believe this?" If you are persuaded that Jesus is the Christ (Messiah), the Son of God, who paid for your sin and promises eternal life to all who believe in Him, then having believed Him at His word, you also have His promise. The moment you believe, you have eternal life and He says you will never come into judgment for the eternal consequence of sin. Instead, you have the promise of eternity in heaven.

The question is not whether you will decide, but what you will decide. Do you believe Him?

“Truly, truly, I say to you, he who believes has eternal life.”

John 6:47
