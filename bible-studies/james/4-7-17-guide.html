James · Study 13 · Leader / Learner Guide

James 4:7–17 — Leader / Learner Guide

Submit, Draw Near, and Say “If the Lord Wills” · a one-page guide for leaders and learners

James: Living Wisely Under Pressure  ·  TheGospelVault.com  ·  @iJohn.316 (Instagram)

Full Study 13

Big Idea and Context

Big idea: The cure for worldliness is humility: get right with God (submit, resist, draw near, cleanse, grieve, humble yourself), get right with your brother (stop speaking against and judging him), and plan your life saying, “If the Lord wills.”

After diagnosing worldliness (4:1–6), James gives ten commands to believers (4:7–10), forbids critical judging of brethren (4:11–12), and rebukes self-confident planners who leave God out (4:13–17).

  • Toward God (4:7–10): submit, resist, draw near, cleanse, grieve, humble yourselves.
  • Toward a brother (4:11–12): no slander; one Lawgiver and Judge.
  • Toward tomorrow (4:13–17): life is a vapor; say “If the Lord wills.”

The Path of Humility — Three Anchor Verses

VerseWhat it saysTeaching point
James 4:8“Draw near to God and He will draw near to you.”A call to fellowship for believers, not a test of salvation.
James 4:12“There is only one Lawgiver and Judge.”Condemning a brother puts us in God’s seat.
James 4:15“If the Lord wills, we will live and also do this or that.”Plan, but hold plans with an open hand.

Submit before resisting (4:7): we stand against Satan only as we stand under God.

“Vapor” (atmis, 4:14): steam or mist, here a moment and gone.

Teaching Flow

  1. Introduction. The cure after the diagnosis; why 4:7–10 addresses believers.
  2. Get right with God (4:7–10). Submit, resist, draw near, cleanse, grieve, humble yourselves.
  3. Get right with a brother (4:11–12). Slander breaks the royal law; one Lawgiver and Judge.
  4. The worldly planner (4:13–16). Their plan; God left out; life a vapor; “If the Lord wills.”
  5. The sin of omission (4:17). Knowing the good and not doing it.

Clarify While Teaching

  • Not a salvation invitation: some commentators read 4:7–10 that way; context (4:5, 11) shows believers.
  • Repentance: called for in the believer’s walk, not a condition of eternal life (John 6:47).
  • “Able to save and to destroy” (4:12): read here of preserving or taking life, as “save” is used in James.
  • 4:14 wording: NASB “You are just a vapor”; NKJV “For what is your life? It is even a vapor.”

Discussion and Close

  1. Which of the ten commands in 4:7–10 do you most need this week?
  2. How can we disagree with a brother without speaking against him?
  3. What plans do you need to hold more loosely?

Close: Self-confidence about tomorrow often rests on money. Study 14 (James 5:1–12) turns to the rich who oppress and to the believers who wait patiently for the Lord’s coming.
