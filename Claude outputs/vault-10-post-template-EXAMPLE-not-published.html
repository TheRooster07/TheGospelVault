What Assurance Looked Like the Week My Father Died

Submitted by J.M.  ·  September 17, 2026

I always thought I understood eternal security. I could explain it, defend it, point to the verses. Then my father died on a Tuesday morning, and for about four days I couldn’t have told you my own name, let alone recited a doctrine. Grief doesn’t care how well you know your theology. It just sits on your chest.

What surprised me wasn’t that the grief was heavy — I expected that. It was that the assurance held anyway, not because I reached for it, but because it had never actually been resting on my grip in the first place. Romans 8 kept surfacing in pieces, not as something I worked to remember, but almost the way a familiar voice does in a dark room. “Who shall separate us from the love of Christ?” I hadn’t earned that peace that week. I couldn’t have. I just hadn’t lost the thing the peace was actually standing on.

I don’t have a tidy conclusion. I’m not further along in some five-step grief process than anyone else. But I wanted to write this down for whoever else is finding out, the hard way, whether their assurance was ever really theirs to lose.

A Note from The Gospel Vault

We're grateful J.M. was willing to share this. Grief and assurance don't arrive on a tidy timeline, and Scripture never asks them to — Romans 8:38–39 doesn't say "nothing will trouble you," it says nothing "will be able to separate us from the love of God." Those are different promises. If you're walking through something similar right now, that passage is worth sitting with slowly rather than reading past.
