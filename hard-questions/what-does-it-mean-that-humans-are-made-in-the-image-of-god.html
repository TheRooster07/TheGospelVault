What Does It Mean That Humans Are Made in the Image of God?

Translation: NKJV · Free Grace / Dispensational · Source: The Gospel Vault

In Brief

Every person bears God’s image, which grounds human dignity. The Fall marred that image; in Christ, believers are being renewed into it.

Key versesGenesis 1:26–27 · Genesis 9:6 · 2 Corinthians 3:18

“Then God said, ‘Let Us make man in Our image, according to Our likeness’ ... So God created man in His own image; in the image of God He created him; male and female He created them” (Genesis 1:26–27). No other creature in the six days of creation receives this designation, and the repetition across three clauses in a single sentence signals how much weight the text places on it: humanity, uniquely among everything God made, bears a likeness to its Maker that no animal, plant, or celestial body shares.

The image of God does not mean physical resemblance — God is spirit (John 4:24) — but a bundle of capacities and roles that reflect who God is: rational thought, moral responsibility, relational capacity, creativity, and the delegated authority to “have dominion ... over all the earth” (Genesis 1:26, 28) as God’s representative steward over creation, ruling it under Him rather than as its ultimate owner. Being made in God’s image is also inseparably relational and social from the very beginning: God’s own triune nature (see Q23, “How Can God Be Three Persons in One — Why Does the Trinity Matter?”), is reflected in the fact that humanity is made in the image of a God who exists eternally in relationship, and that image is expressed specifically as “male and female” together (Genesis 1:27), not in isolation.

This doctrine carries enormous practical and ethical weight throughout Scripture. It is the explicit foundation for the sanctity of human life and the prohibition of murder: “Whoever sheds man’s blood, by man his blood shall be shed; for in the image of God He made man” (Genesis 9:6). James grounds the seriousness of cursing another person in the very same doctrine: people are “made in the similitude of God” (James 3:9), which makes contempt for any human being — regardless of ability, age, background, or moral failure — an offense against God’s own image-bearer, not merely an interpersonal wrong.

Crucially, the Fall damaged but did not erase this image. Genesis 9:6 and James 3:9 are both written long after the Fall, and both continue to ground human dignity in an image that sin has corrupted but not destroyed — every person, believer or not, saint or sinner, still bears it. This is part of why the gospel is offered to every person without exception (John 3:16), and why Scripture calls for the same basic dignity and respect to be extended to every human being, since dignity here rests on being God’s image-bearer rather than on personal merit or achievement. This conviction runs through historic Christian teaching on the image of God.1

For the believer, the image of God is also being actively restored, not merely preserved: those in Christ are “being transformed into the same image from glory to glory” (2 Corinthians 3:18), renewed “in knowledge according to the image of Him who created him” (Colossians 3:10). The image marred at the Fall finds its full restoration not in human effort but in growing conformity to Christ, “who is the image of the invisible God” (Colossians 1:15) in perfect, undiminished form.
