50 Hard Questions · Question 45 of 50

What Is the Role of the Holy Spirit in a Believer’s Life?

Translation: NKJV · Free Grace / Dispensational · Source: The Gospel Vault

In Brief

The Spirit indwells and seals every believer at the moment of faith, guaranteeing the believer’s inheritance. He teaches, empowers, produces fruit, and gives gifts for serving the church.

Key verses1 Corinthians 6:19 · Ephesians 1:13–14 · Galatians 5:22–23

The Holy Spirit is not an impersonal force but the third Person of the Trinity, fully God, who takes up permanent residence within every believer at the moment of salvation. “Do you not know that your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you, whom you have from God?” (1 Corinthians 6:19). This indwelling is not a later, separate experience some believers receive and others lack; it occurs for every believer at the instant of belief: “In Him you also trusted, after you heard the word of truth ... in whom also, having believed, you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise” (Ephesians 1:13).

This sealing is directly connected to the doctrine of eternal security (see Q20, “Can a Genuine Christian Lose Their Salvation?”) — the Spirit is explicitly called “the guarantee of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession” (Ephesians 1:14), a down payment guaranteeing the full payment will follow. Believers are warned not to “grieve the Holy Spirit of God, by whom you were sealed for the day of redemption” (Ephesians 4:30) — a believer’s sin can grieve the Spirit and disrupt present fellowship, but the seal itself, tied to a future day of redemption rather than to present behavior, is never described as breakable.

The Spirit’s ongoing ministry in the believer’s life touches nearly every dimension of Christian experience: He illuminates and teaches Scripture (John 16:13; 1 Corinthians 2:12–14), He produces Christlike character described as “fruit” — “love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control” (Galatians 5:22–23) — as an organic outgrowth of His presence rather than a checklist achieved by willpower, and He “bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God” (Romans 8:16), confirming rather than replacing the assurance grounded in God’s promises (see Q19, “How Can I Know I’m Truly Saved?”). He also intercedes for believers in prayer beyond what their own words can express: “the Spirit Himself makes intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered” (Romans 8:26).

The Spirit also equips every believer with at least one spiritual gift for the building up of the church (1 Corinthians 12:7, 11) (see Q47, “What Are Spiritual Gifts, and Do They Still Operate Today?”), and empowers ordinary Christian living and witness in a way natural human effort alone cannot produce: “you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you” (Acts 1:8). Being “filled with the Spirit” (Ephesians 5:18) — a repeatable, commanded experience of yielding control of one’s life to the Spirit’s influence, distinct from the one-time, unrepeatable indwelling every believer already possesses — is presented as the normal, ongoing means by which a believer walks in the power and fruitfulness Scripture describes rather than merely in their own strength.

On this basic doctrine of the Spirit’s Person and indwelling ministry, the Reformed and Free Grace traditions are in substantial agreement, even where they differ on the precise relationship between the Spirit’s work and the certainty of a believer’s assurance (see Q19, “How Can I Know I’m Truly Saved?”).
