Vault 3 · New Testament Book Introductions · Paul’s Letters

Galatians

Justified by faith alone; free in Christ.

Author
Paul
Date
AD 48–49, from Syrian Antioch, before the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15)
Key verses
Galatians 2:16; 3:3; 5:1

nevertheless knowing that a man is not justified by the works of the Law but through faith in Christ Jesus, even we have believed in Christ Jesus, so that we may be justified by faith in Christ and not by the works of the Law; since by the works of the Law no flesh will be justified.

— Galatians 2:16 (NASB95)

Theme

Justification by faith alone, and freedom in Christ. Written to the churches of South Galatia (Pisidian Antioch, Iconium, Lystra, Derbe) planted on the first journey, it answers teachers who added circumcision and the Law to faith in Christ. It is likely Paul’s earliest letter.

Outline

  1. Introduction: no other gospel (1:1–10)
  2. Personal: Paul’s gospel came by revelation, not from men (1:11–2:21)
  3. Doctrinal: justification by faith, not works of the Law (3:1–4:31)
  4. Practical: liberty, walking by the Spirit, bearing burdens (5:1–6:10)
  5. Conclusion: boasting only in the cross (6:11–18)

More key verses

Are you so foolish? Having begun by the Spirit, are you now being perfected by the flesh?

— Galatians 3:3 (NASB95)

It was for freedom that Christ set us free; therefore keep standing firm and do not be subject again to a yoke of slavery.

— Galatians 5:1 (NASB95)
