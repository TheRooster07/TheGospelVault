Panel 1 of 5 · The Question

Salvation is a free gift — so what does it take to receive it?

Ephesians 2:8-9; Romans 4:5; Romans 5:1 (NASB)

Eph 2:8-9 For by grace you have been saved through faith; and that not of yourselves, it is the gift of God; not as a result of works, so that no one may boast. Rom 4:5 But to the one who does not work, but believes in Him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is credited as righteousness. Rom 5:1 Therefore, having been justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ.
Salvation is a free gift — so what does it take to receive it?

Ask almost anyone whether salvation is a free gift, and the answer comes fast: of course it is. It's usually backed by exactly these verses — by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God, not of works, so that no one may boast (Ephesians 2:8-9). To the one who does not work, but believes, his faith is credited as righteousness (Romans 4:5). Having been justified by faith, we have peace with God (Romans 5:1). So far, so good.

Then comes the second question, and it's where the trouble starts: what does one actually have to do in order to be saved? Ask around, and the free gift quietly grows a price tag. Repent of your sins. Confess Jesus before others. Ask Him into your heart. Make Him Lord of your life. Promise to serve Him all your life. Go forward at church. Join a church. Be baptized. Make a decision for Christ. Accept Him as Lord and Savior. Some of these may be good things a believer eventually does — but is any one of them the condition for receiving eternal life, or is it something else entirely?

That's the question this series answers, one panel at a time — not by weighing each item against tradition or personal experience, but by asking what the Bible itself actually says the condition is. The best place to start is the one Gospel that tells you, in so many words, exactly why it was written.

For reflection

  1. Which of the answers above have you personally heard — or believed — was necessary for salvation?
  2. If salvation really is a free gift, what would it mean for something to be added to "gift" as a condition?

Hold that question. The rest of this walkthrough is built to answer it from Scripture, not tradition.
