Declaring the End from the Beginning · Study 4 · Leader / Learner Guide

Titus 1:1–3 — Leader / Learner Guide

God, Who Cannot Lie, Promised · a one-page guide for leaders and learners

TheGospelVault.com  ·  @iJohn.316 (Instagram)

Download PDF Full Study 4

Big Idea and Context

Big idea: The objections to fulfilled prophecy can be answered honestly, without overclaiming — and the God whose word has proven true has promised eternal life to all who believe, a promise that can be known.

This study weighs four objections (written after the fact, vague, self-fulfilled, failed), gives a Reformed counterpoint, and lands on Titus 1:2 and 1 John 5:13.

  • Posture: a defense “with gentleness and reverence” (1 Peter 3:15); examine like the Bereans (Acts 17:11).
  • Honesty: say when a text is pattern, not prediction (Hosea 11:1); acknowledge Psalm 22:16’s textual question; skip inflated odds.
  • Goal: not a debate won, but a promise trusted.

A God Who Cannot Lie — Three Anchor Verses

VerseWhat it saysTeaching point
Titus 1:2Eternal life, which God, who cannot lie, promised long ages ago.The oldest promise rests on God’s character.
Numbers 23:19Has He said, and will He not do it?Every fulfilled prophecy witnesses to this.
1 John 5:13Written so that you may know you have eternal life.Assurance = taking God at His word (John 5:24).

Late dating: even 165 BC for Daniel leaves Rome and the Messiah’s death before AD 70 (Daniel 9:26) as prediction; Acts ends c. AD 62.

Failed prophecy? Tyre came in waves (Ezekiel 26:3, 12); some warnings are conditional (Jeremiah 18:7–8); “not yet” is not “failed.”

Teaching Flow

  1. Weighing the evidence fairly (1 Peter 3:15; Acts 17:11): a fair test cuts both ways.
  2. Four objections: after the fact; vague; self-fulfilled; failed (Daniel, Isaiah, AD 70, Tyre).
  3. A caution for believers: do not overclaim (probability figures; pattern vs. prediction).
  4. A Reformed counterpoint: method (Spirit’s witness) and meaning (preterist vs. literal fulfillment).
  5. God, who cannot lie, promised (Titus 1:2; 1 John 5:9–13; John 5:24) — series review table.

Clarify While Teaching

  • Presuppositional view: the Spirit alone persuades — true; yet Scripture itself presents prophecy as evidence (Isaiah 45:3; John 13:19).
  • Preterism: covenant theology often places Daniel’s 70th week and the Olivet Discourse in AD 70; dispensationalists expect literal future fulfillment.
  • Josephus on Titus and Cyrus: report these as Josephus’s accounts, not as Scripture.
  • Assurance: eternal life is a present possession from the moment of faith (“has,” “has passed”), not contingent on what follows.

Discussion and Close

  • Why does honesty about hard texts strengthen our witness?
  • How would you answer “prophecies were written after the events”?
  • Can you say, on the basis of God’s promise alone, that you have eternal life?

Close: Series review: Josiah and Cyrus, the empires, the cities, the temple, Israel, the Messiah — God kept His word every time. He will keep His promise of eternal life.
