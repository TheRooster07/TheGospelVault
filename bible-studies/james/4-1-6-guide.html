James · Study 12 · Leader / Learner Guide

James 4:1–6 — Leader / Learner Guide

Wars Within and a Greater Grace · a one-page guide for leaders and learners

James: Living Wisely Under Pressure  ·  TheGospelVault.com  ·  @iJohn.316 (Instagram)

Full Study 12

Big Idea and Context

Big idea: Quarrels among believers come from worldly desires at war within; friendship with the world is spiritual adultery and hostility toward God, who jealously longs for His people’s devotion, yet gives a greater grace to the humble.

After wisdom from above that sows peace (3:17–18), James asks why there is fighting among believers (4:1). He traces it to inner desires (4:1–3), condemns friendship with the world (4:4–5), and promises greater grace to the humble (4:6).

  • The source (4:1): pleasures waging war within produce wars among believers.
  • The marks (4:2–3): craving, hatred, envy, fighting, prayerlessness and selfish prayer.
  • The charge and the cure (4:4–6): spiritual adultery; God’s jealous love; a greater grace.

Worldliness and Grace — Three Anchor Verses

VerseWhat it saysTeaching point
James 4:1“Your pleasures that wage war in your members.”The conflict among us begins with the conflict within us.
James 4:4“Friendship with the world is hostility toward God.”Spiritual adultery by believers; a broken walk, not lost salvation.
James 4:6“He gives a greater grace.”Grace is greater than the world’s pull, and it flows to the humble.

Worldliness: not the possession of things but the obsession with things.

“Hedonism” (hēdonē): the same word as Luke 8:14, pleasures that choke the Word.

Teaching Flow

  1. Introduction. A church at war; what worldliness is and is not (Colossians 2:20–23).
  2. The conflicts (4:1). Wars and fights; pleasures at war within; the thorns of Luke 8:14.
  3. The marks (4:2–3). Lustful, hateful, envious, quarrelsome, prayerless, selfish in prayer.
  4. The condemnation (4:4–5). “You adulteresses”; friendship and enmity; God’s jealous longing.
  5. The grace (4:6). A greater grace; God opposes the proud and gives grace to the humble.

Clarify While Teaching

  • David’s sin is 2 Samuel 11 (the outline has chapter 10); the bride passage is Ephesians 5:22–27 (the outline has 4:22–24).
  • 4:4 variant: NASB “adulteresses”; the Majority Text “adulterers and adulteresses.”
  • 4:5: translations differ on who is jealous; this study reads God longing over His people.
  • Avoid overstatement: “enemy of God” describes a believer’s walk, not loss of eternal life (Romans 5:10).

Discussion and Close

  1. Where do the conflicts in our homes and churches really begin?
  2. Which mark of worldliness in 4:2–3 do you recognize in yourself?
  3. How does God’s jealous love change the way you see worldliness?

Close: Grace flows to the humble. Study 13 (James 4:7–17) shows how to humble ourselves: submit to God, resist the devil, draw near, stop speaking against a brother, and say, “If the Lord wills.”
