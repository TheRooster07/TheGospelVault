Declaring the End from the Beginning · Study 1 · Leader / Learner Guide

Isaiah 46:9–10 — Leader / Learner Guide

The God Who Tells the Future · a one-page guide for leaders and learners

TheGospelVault.com  ·  @iJohn.316 (Instagram)

Download PDF Full Study 1

Big Idea and Context

Big idea: God stakes His claim to be the only true God on declaring the future before it happens — and then goes on record, naming Josiah and Cyrus long before they were born.

Isaiah 40–48 reads like a courtroom: the idols of Babylon on trial, Israel facing exile. God sets the test — declare the future — and takes it Himself.

  • The test: “Declare the things that are going to come afterward, that we may know that you are gods” (Isaiah 41:23).
  • The standard: a prophet of the LORD is right every time (Deuteronomy 18:21–22; 1 Kings 22:28).
  • The purpose: “so that you may know” (Isaiah 45:3); “so that … you may believe” (John 13:19).

God Goes on Record — Three Anchor Verses

VerseWhat it saysTeaching point
Isaiah 46:9–10God declares the end from the beginning; His purpose will stand.Prophecy is God announcing His own plan, not guessing.
1 Kings 13:2A son of David, Josiah by name, will burn bones on Bethel’s altar.Named about 300 years ahead; fulfilled in 2 Kings 23:15–16.
Isaiah 44:28–45:1Cyrus, God’s shepherd and anointed, will say Jerusalem shall be built.Fulfilled in Cyrus’s decree, 538 BC (Ezra 1:1–3).

Specific, not vague: names, places and outcomes make these prophecies checkable — unlike ancient oracles that fit any result.

Prophecy and promise: the God who said “My purpose will be established” also said “he who believes has eternal life” (John 6:47).

Teaching Flow

  1. God puts the idols on trial (Isaiah 41:21–23; 46:1–4): idols are carried; God carries His people.
  2. God takes His own test (Isaiah 46:9–10; 48:3–5): prophecy as His signature on history.
  3. The test for a prophet (Deuteronomy 18:21–22): Micaiah stakes his life on it (1 Kings 22:28).
  4. Named before birth: Josiah and Cyrus; why specific prophecy matters.
  5. A more sure word (2 Peter 1:19–21) and where it leads: rest in His promise (John 6:47; 13:19).

Clarify While Teaching

  • Second Isaiah: many scholars date Isaiah 40–66 after Cyrus; that assumes prophecy is impossible. John 12:38–41 credits both halves to Isaiah. Fuller answer in Study 4.
  • Cyrus Cylinder: it records Cyrus’s policy of returning peoples and sanctuaries; it does not name the Jews. Say so.
  • “Anointed” (Isaiah 45:1): used of Cyrus for a task — not a claim that Cyrus was the Messiah.
  • Evidence and salvation: prophecy removes barriers and steadies faith; eternal life comes by believing in Jesus for it.

Discussion and Close

  • Why does it matter that the Bible stakes its claim on checkable predictions?
  • How would you answer someone who says the Cyrus prophecy was written after the fact?
  • How does God’s track record strengthen your assurance of eternal life?
