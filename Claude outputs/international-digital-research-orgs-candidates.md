# International & Digital Faith-Research Organizations — Candidate List

Companion to `claude/faithalone-org-resource-candidates.md` (which covers Free Grace/GES-aligned ministry links for Vault 6) and `Research1.docx` (missions/translation/persecution/financial-accountability sources). This list covers a different category: **culture, trends, and demographic research** — Barna-style survey and statistical organizations — with a deliberate focus on international (Europe, Asia) and digital-ministry coverage, which the existing research base is thin on.

**Two intended uses:**
1. **Vault 6 (Resource Links)** — currently a "Coming Soon" placeholder; any of these could be added as a curated external link.
2. **White-paper citation trail** — when drafting or revising a white paper (e.g., "The Messianic Hope Fulfilled" or future pieces), it's worth a quick check of these sources for a supporting data point. It only takes one relevant finding — a statistic on generational belief shift, a regional trend, a digital-engagement figure — to justify citing one, even in a paper that isn't primarily about trends research.

---

## 1. Global / Baseline Sources

**Pew Research Center — Religion**
*https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/*

**What it provides:** The premier non-partisan source for global religious demographics. "The Global Religious Landscape" and the ongoing "Global Religious Futures" project (joint with the Berkley Center) break down affiliation, switching, and growth by country — the strongest available baseline for any claim about religion outside the U.S.

**Update cadence / access:** Continuously updated; major landscape reports every few years (most recent major update June 2025). Free, publicly accessible.

**Center for the Study of Global Christianity (Gordon-Conwell Theological Seminary)**
*https://www.globalchristianity.org*

**What it provides:** Maintains the World Christian Database and publishes the annual "Status of Global Christianity" report — the academic standard for worldwide Christian population data by region and country. Pairs naturally with Joshua Project and IMB People Groups (already in Research1.docx) for demographic cross-checking.

**Update cadence / access:** Annual report; database updated continuously. Some content behind academic access, summary reports free.

**Operation World**
*https://operationworld.org*

**What it provides:** Country-by-country prayer and missions profiles with religious demographic breakdowns. Long-standing, widely trusted in evangelical mission circles — a natural companion to the unreached-people-groups sources already gathered.

**Update cadence / access:** Periodic full-edition updates; free country profiles online.

**Lausanne Movement**
*https://lausanne.org*

**What it provides:** Global evangelical research network; publishes a "State of the Great Commission" report with regional breakdowns including Europe and Asia. Research-oriented rather than advocacy-oriented, with credibility across denominational lines.

**Update cadence / access:** Periodic major reports tied to Lausanne congresses; free online.

## 2. Europe

**Evangelical Alliance (UK)**
*https://www.eauk.org*

**What it provides:** Publishes an annual "Missional Trends" research series — practical, church-health-focused survey data for the UK, with some broader European relevance. Closest UK/European analogue to Barna's U.S. state-of-the-church work.

**Update cadence / access:** Annual. Free.

**European Evangelical Alliance**
*https://europeanea.org*

**What it provides:** Continent-wide evangelical umbrella body. Less a research shop than a network and advocacy hub, but a useful entry point for country-level European evangelical contacts and occasional survey data.

**Update cadence / access:** Ongoing; free.

---

## 3. Asia / Asia-Pacific

**Pew Research — Asia religion studies**
*https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/* (topic-filtered)

**What it provides:** Country-level religion data for East and South Asia, including religious-switching research on Korea, Hong Kong, and Taiwan. Caveat: some Pew "Asian" reports are specifically about Asian Americans in the U.S. rather than Asia itself — check the geography before citing.

**Update cadence / access:** Ongoing; free.

**National Church Life Survey (Australia / NZ)**
*https://ncls.org.au*

**What it provides:** A respected, long-running regional church-health survey covering the Asia-Pacific region — attendance, engagement, and congregational-health metrics.

**Update cadence / access:** Periodic survey waves; some content free, detailed reports may require purchase.

**Asia Evangelical Alliance**
*https://asiaea.org*

**What it provides:** Regional network body for evangelical churches across Asia. Primarily a hub/contact point rather than a primary data source, but useful for locating country-specific research bodies.

**Update cadence / access:** Ongoing; free.

---

## 4. Digital Ministry & Tech Trends

**Barna Group — Tech, Media & Faith research**
*https://www.barna.com*

**What it provides:** Barna's own state-of-the-church reporting increasingly tracks digital engagement, media consumption, and online faith practice alongside its core U.S. survey work — the most rigorous entry in this category.

**Update cadence / access:** Rolling releases tied to annual "State of the Church" cycle. Summary content free; full datasets by subscription.

**Note on vendor/industry sources:** Church-tech vendors (Subsplash, Church Media Squad, and similar) publish "digital trend" roundups that are useful for practical awareness of what churches are doing online, but they are marketing/industry content, not research-grade — treat them as secondary color, not citation-grade like Barna or Pew.

## How to Use This List

For **Vault 6**, any entry above is a plausible curated link once BRC decides which fit the site's tone (Free Grace/GES material stays the anchor; these are more general-culture/trends resources, so framing should make clear they're not doctrinally aligned or vetted the way the FGR.docx bibliography is).

For **white papers**, the standard should be the same cross-checking approach already used in Research1.docx ("no single source in any category is fully authoritative on its own"): before finalizing a paper, a quick pass through the relevant regional/topical sources above — checking for one supporting statistic or trend finding — is worth the time even when the paper's core argument doesn't depend on trends data. A single well-placed, properly cited data point (e.g., a generational-belief-shift figure from Pew, a digital-engagement statistic from Barna) can materially strengthen a cultural-relevance argument without requiring the paper to become a statistics-driven piece.

International and digital-trend coverage is noticeably thinner and less frequently updated than the U.S.-focused Barna/Lifeway ecosystem — Pew's global demographic work is the most reliable baseline outside the U.S., and claims about Europe or Asia should lean on it or the Center for the Study of Global Christianity rather than industry blog posts.
