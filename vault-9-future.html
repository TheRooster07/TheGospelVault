Missions and Diaspora Groups

A ranked master list of 17 unreached diaspora people groups, sized by population and mapped across every host country, so the pattern of where the need and opportunity are greatest is visible at a glance.

Mission Research Project · August 2026, corrected September 2026

Purpose

Mission Research Project · 17 unreached diaspora people groups — Afghan, general plus the 16 others flagged elsewhere in this project — each shown with every host country, its population, whether that host country is Free / Partly Free / Not Free (Freedom House), and the New Testament / Bible status in the group’s language · August 2026

Correction · September 2026

Correction (September 2026): the Afghan, general entry’s diaspora total and 7 of its 21 host-country population figures (Germany, United States, United Kingdom, Canada, France, Australia, Sweden) were updated from Joshua Project’s undated tracked figures to current, dated diaspora estimates, after a presentation-deck consistency review found Joshua Project’s own pages carry no year or source for these figures and read as stale pre-2021-refugee-surge snapshots. See the Afghan, general entry below (§11) and Presentation_Consistency_Review.md for the full comparison and methodology. The other 16 entries in this document have not been re-verified and remain Joshua Project figures as originally sourced, August 2026.

Caveat: Seven of these 21 country figures (marked * above) were corrected from Joshua Project’s tracked “Afghan, general” figures after a presentation-deck consistency review (see Presentation_Consistency_Review.md) found the gap between this document and the CSG17 white papers’ own population tables could not be waved off as two sources counting different things. Checked directly: Joshua Project’s own per-country pages for this entry (joshuaproject.net/people_groups/19314/GM, /US, /UK, /CA, /FR, /AS, /SW) carry no year or source citation for any of these seven figures. Wikipedia’s “Afghan diaspora” article gives dated, sourced figures for the same countries — Germany 442,020 (December 2024, up from 425,000 at end of 2022 and 377,000 Afghan citizens specifically), United States ~300,000 (2023), United Kingdom 93,296 (2023), Canada 132,015 (2023), France 124,830 (2023), Australia 59,797 (2021), and Sweden 67,858 (2023) — drawn from national census and statistics-office data. Germany’s documented growth trajectory is consistent with the well-known post-2021 surge in Afghan refugee and asylum arrivals across these same host countries, which most plausibly explains why Joshua Project’s undated figures read low across the board rather than measuring a genuinely different population. Guidance carried over from the Content & Channel Plan: use the JP Tracked Figure only when citing Joshua Project’s own Unreached/Frontier classification and Progress Scale for this population, since that classification is not sensitive to the exact count; use the Population column above for anything about audience size, pilot-market comparison, or total addressable population. The other 14 countries in this table were not part of this review and remain straight Joshua Project figures as originally sourced (August 2026) — they may carry the same staleness risk and have not yet been checked against a current, dated source. This entry also still excludes an estimated 2.5M in Iran and 1.8M in Pakistan, folded into the Pashtun/Hazara/Tajik/Uzbek entries above instead of this aggregate row.

Worth Knowing

A Recurring Data-Quality Pattern

Five groups (Gujarati, Hindi, Punjabi, Tamil, Telugu) have no homeland row at all in their Joshua Project entry — India’s population of each language is fragmented across dozens of caste- or community-specific people-group profiles instead of one aggregate row. Three groups (Pashtun, Tajik, Uzbek) have a large population counted under a differently-named JP entry rather than appearing where a search would naturally find it. Sri Lanka’s roughly 3.1 million Tamils are folded into three Sri Lanka-specific entries rather than the main Tamil profile. This is the same pattern the Afghan pilot first identified for Iran/Pakistan — worth checking for every group above before any one of them becomes the subject of a full Support-vs-Create or Content & Channel pass.

The Afghan, general entry (§11) was the first in this list checked against current, dated diaspora estimates rather than Joshua Project’s tracked figures alone; 7 of its 21 host-country figures were corrected as a result (see that entry’s caveat and Presentation_Consistency_Review.md). Joshua Project’s own per-country pages for this entry carry no year or source for any figure, so the same undated-snapshot risk plausibly applies to some unknown share of the other 16 groups’ host-country figures above — this has not yet been checked group-by-group, and no other entry’s figures have been changed.
