Hard Questions · Part IV, Question 8

Hebrews 6:4–6: Can a Believer Fall Away?

Translation: NKJV · Free Grace / Dispensational · Source: The Gospel Vault

In Brief

Hebrews 6 speaks to genuine believers, but it does not teach that they can lose eternal life. It warns believers who drift from the life of faith that they face God’s discipline and the loss of reward, and that they cannot “start over” by having Christ crucified again.

Key versesHebrews 5:12 · Hebrews 6:4–8 · 1 Corinthians 3:15

Three ways the passage is read. (1) These people heard the gospel but never truly believed. (2) They were genuine believers who lost their salvation. (3) They are genuine believers who drift from fellowship and face God’s discipline. The words of the passage point to the third.

Hebrews 5 sets the scene. Just before the warning, the writer says, “For though by this time you ought to be teachers, you need someone to teach you again the first principles of the oracles of God; and you have come to need milk and not solid food” (5:12). You can only fall behind where you have already been. These readers had believed and grown, and then stalled.

These are believers. They were “once enlightened,” “tasted the heavenly gift,” and “became partakers of the Holy Spirit” (6:4). “Partaker” (metochos) describes genuine believers everywhere else in the New Testament (Hebrews 3:1, 3:14; 12:8). “Tasted” means to fully experience, as Christ “tasted death for everyone” (2:9). “Enlightened” describes real spiritual sight (10:32). The writer is not describing people who only came close.

What “fall away” means. The Greek word parapiptō (6:6) appears only here in the New Testament, so the Greek Old Testament (the Septuagint) is the best guide to its meaning. There it translates the Hebrew ma’al, “to act unfaithfully” or “to trespass.” God says, “when a land sins against Me by persistent unfaithfulness” (Ezekiel 14:13). The word describes God’s own people breaking faith with Him. It never describes someone ceasing to belong to Him.

Two Old Testament examples. Moses “trespassed against Me” at the waters of Meribah (Deuteronomy 32:51); he lost the privilege of entering the land, but not his relationship with God. King Uzziah “trespassed” by burning incense in the temple (2 Chronicles 26:16–18); he was disciplined with leprosy, yet he remained king of God’s people. Both are serious discipline and lost privilege, not loss of standing with God, and that is the warning of Hebrews 6.

What is “impossible”? Verse 6 says it is impossible “to renew them again to repentance, since they crucify again for themselves the Son of God.” This is not about being saved a second time. Christ’s sacrifice was offered “once for all” (10:10) and cannot be repeated. A believer who abandons the life of faith cannot start over from the beginning, as if the cross had to happen again.

The burned field. Land that bears thorns and briers is “near to being cursed, whose end is to be burned” (6:7–8). The picture matches the wood, hay and straw burned at the Judgment Seat of Christ: “If anyone’s work is burned, he will suffer loss; but he himself will be saved, yet so as through fire” (1 Corinthians 3:12–15). What burns is the unfruitful work, not the believer.

Why it matters. The warning is real, and it is meant to keep believers going on with Christ. But eternal life rests on God’s promise and Christ’s finished work, not on our perseverance. Whoever believes in Jesus has eternal life and will never perish (John 10:28).
