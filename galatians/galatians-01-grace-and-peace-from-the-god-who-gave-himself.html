Grace and Peace from the God Who Gave Himself

Galatians 1:1–5  ·  NASB95

Key Verse

“Who gave Himself for our sins, that He might deliver us from this present evil age, according to the will of our God and Father, to whom be glory forever and ever. Amen.”

Galatians 1:4–5

Paul opens this letter differently than any other he wrote. There’s no thanksgiving, no warm word of commendation before he gets to business — because there’s no time to lose. But before the confrontation begins, he anchors everything in one sentence: Christ “gave Himself for our sins.” Not was given. Gave Himself — a voluntary, personal, total surrender to the Father’s will. Whatever correction is coming in the chapters ahead, it’s built on this foundation first: grace, freely given, at enormous cost to the Giver.

And that gift wasn’t only about where you go when you die. Paul says Christ died to “deliver us from this present evil age” — a rescue that starts now, in the actual world you’re living in this week, not just a ticket stamped for later. The letter that follows will spend six chapters defending that single truth against people trying to add conditions to it. But it starts here, with Paul unable to even finish his greeting without breaking into praise: “to whom be glory forever and ever. Amen.” Before there’s a single word of argument, there’s worship.

Questions to Carry Into the Passage

  1. Paul says Christ's death delivers us from "this present evil age" — not just from future judgment, but from something happening now. What would it look like to actually live like you've been rescued from the pull of this age this week?
  2. The gift was total and voluntary — Christ "gave Himself." Is there an area where you're still treating grace as something you have to help earn or maintain, rather than something already completely given?
  3. Paul can't get through his greeting without erupting into praise. When was the last time simply remembering the gospel moved you to worship rather than just information?
