Galatians 05 of 16

Crucified with Christ

Galatians 2:11–21  ·  NASB95

Key Verse

“I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me; and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave Himself for me.”

Galatians 2:20

Peter knew better. He’d already seen the vision of the sheet full of unclean animals, already declared before the Jerusalem church, “who was I that I could withstand God?” and already shaken hands with Paul in agreement over the grace gospel. And yet, when certain men arrived from James, Peter quietly withdrew from eating with Gentile believers — not from conviction, but from fear of what the circumcision party would think. It wasn’t a doctrinal failure; it was hypocrisy, acting contrary to what he actually believed. And it was contagious — even Barnabas, Paul’s own partner in preaching grace to the Gentiles, got swept along by the peer pressure. So Paul confronted Peter publicly, “before them all,” because a private correction wouldn’t undo public damage.

Out of that confrontation comes one of the most personal verses Paul ever wrote. Justification, he insists, has never come by works of the law — not for Jews, not for anyone — but by faith in Jesus Christ alone. And then he makes it intensely his own: “I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me.” When Christ died, Paul died with Him — his old identity, his old debt, settled at the cross. And the life he now lives, he lives by faith in the Son of God who loved him personally and “gave Himself for me” — not a vague, general love for humanity, but a love with Paul’s own name on it. Paul closes with the stakes laid bare: “if righteousness comes through the law, then Christ died in vain.” There is no alternate route. Grace alone, or the cross accomplished nothing.

Questions to Carry Into the Passage

  1. Peter knew the truth of the gospel but acted contrary to it out of fear of what others would think. Where is fear of others' opinions currently causing a gap between what you believe and how you're actually living?
  2. Paul makes the gospel radically personal — Christ "loved me and gave Himself for me," not just humanity in general. Does your own sense of the gospel feel that personal, or does it stay more abstract?
  3. Paul says if righteousness could come through the law, Christ's death would have been pointless. Is there any part of your life where you're quietly relying on your own performance to secure what Christ already secured?
