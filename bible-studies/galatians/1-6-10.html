① The Biblical Text

Galatians 1:6–10 (NKJV)

6 I marvel that you are turning away so soon from Him who called you in the grace of Christ, to a different gospel,

7 which is not another; but there are some who trouble you and want to pervert the gospel of Christ.

8 But even if we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel to you than what we have preached to you, let him be accursed.

9 As we have said before, so now I say again, if anyone preaches any other gospel to you than what you have received, let him be accursed.

10 For do I now persuade men, or God? Or do I seek to please men? For if I still pleased men, I would not be a bondservant of Christ.

② Devotional

Heart preparation · read in the days before your group meets

“To reject the grace gospel is to reject the One who called — Jesus Christ Himself.”

The Galatians were deserting the gospel of grace almost as soon as they had received it — “turning away,” Paul says, “so soon.” The word pictures a soldier abandoning his post in the middle of battle: willful, already in progress, without excuse. Desertion rarely happens all at once. It usually begins with one small, quiet step away from what we already know is true.

Paul reminds them that in leaving his gospel, they had not simply parted ways with a preacher they had grown tired of. They had turned from Christ Himself — the One who had graciously called them in the first place. Every time we hear the gospel and hesitate, or share it and are brushed aside, the same reality is at work: it was never really about the messenger.

Worse still, they had traded the true gospel for a counterfeit — a message with additions and requirements that, in the end, was no good news at all. Anything added to grace stops being grace.

Reflect

  1. Where have you been tempted to add “something more” to simple faith in Christ?
  2. When someone brushes off the gospel you've shared, whose invitation are they actually turning down?
  3. What area of your life still needs to rest on grace instead of your own effort?

He called you by grace. Stay there.

Study Questions

Part 1 — From the Commentary

Commentary PointKey Idea
I.A — The Galatians' DesertionTurning from grace is described as military desertion — voluntary and already underway.
I.B — Deserted from ChristRejecting Paul's gospel is rejecting Christ Himself, the One who called them.
I.C — Deserted to a Perverted GospelAdding law to grace doesn't modify the gospel — it replaces it with a different, false gospel.
II.A — Unlikely ScenarioEven Paul or an angel preaching differently would be accursed — Scripture alone is the test of truth.
II.B — Likely ScenarioPaul names the Judaizers directly: those preaching a different gospel now stand under a curse.

Part 2 — Study Questions

1. What does the word “turning away” (μετατίθεσθε) reveal about how the Galatians' desertion happened — and how quickly?

Show Leader Answer

The word is a military term for desertion; present tense shows it in progress, middle voice shows it voluntary. And it happened within about a year of Paul's first visit.

2. According to 1:6b, whose call were the Galatians actually rejecting when they abandoned Paul's gospel — and why does that matter?

Show Leader Answer

They were rejecting Christ Himself, who had graciously called them — Paul was only the messenger. Rejecting the gospel is never merely rejecting a person; it's rejecting the One who sent the message (cf. 2 Corinthians 5:20).

3. What is the difference between ἕτερος and ἄλλος in verses 6–7, and why does Paul's choice of ἕτερος matter?

Show Leader Answer

ἄλλος means another of the same kind; ἕτερος means a different kind altogether. Paul's choice signals that the Judaizers' message isn't a variation of the true gospel — it's an entirely different, false gospel.

4. Why does Paul pronounce the same curse (ἀνάθεμα) on both an unlikely messenger (himself or an angel) and a likely one (the Judaizers)?

Show Leader Answer

To establish that the authority of the gospel rests in the message itself, tested against the Word of God — not in the credentials or apparent spiritual authority of the messenger, however impressive.

5. How does understanding grace — that Jesus has already paid the debt in full — change a believer's motive for serving Him?

Show Leader Answer

It moves a believer from fear (uncertain whether enough has been done to be saved or to keep salvation) to love (serving out of gratitude because the debt is already paid) — “the love of Christ compels us” (2 Corinthians 5:14).

⑤ Application

Personal and corporate response — 3–4 applications drawn from the sections above

Grace Cannot Be Improved Upon

Galatians 1:6–7

Source: Commentary ③, I.C

Whenever we add a requirement to the gospel — however sincere it feels — we don't strengthen it, we replace it with something Paul calls no gospel at all. The moment faith in Christ needs a qualifier, it has stopped being the grace gospel.

Reflection: Is there a hidden “and” in how you present or understand the gospel — grace, and something else?

Practical Step: This week, write out in one sentence how a person receives eternal life. Check it against Ephesians 2:8–9 and John 3:16.

Rejection of the Message Is Rejection of the Lord

Galatians 1:6b; 1 Samuel 8:7

Source: Commentary ③, I.B.1; Devotional ②

When someone turns away from the gospel you've shared, they have not rejected you — they have rejected the One who sent the message. Carrying that rejection personally misplaces a burden that was never yours to carry.

Reflection: How do you typically respond, emotionally, when someone dismisses your witness?

Practical Step: The next time someone declines a gospel conversation, consciously hand the rejection to the Lord rather than absorbing it personally.

Test Every Message Against the Word

Galatians 1:8–9

Source: Commentary ③, II.A

Paul's warning applies to every voice claiming spiritual authority — a dream, a television personality, even a respected pulpit. None carries weight unless it can be shown from Scripture. Our one criterion of truth is the Word of God, properly understood.

Reflection: What sources currently shape your theology the most — and do you regularly test them against Scripture?

Practical Step: Pick one teaching you've recently accepted secondhand (a podcast, book, or sermon clip) and trace it back to see whether it holds up against the Word.

Serve Out of Love, Not Fear

Galatians 1:10; 2 Corinthians 5:14–15

Source: Commentary ③, III.C; FGR.docx

A gospel of works keeps a believer wondering whether enough has been done. The gospel of grace settles that question permanently — Jesus paid it all — and frees the believer to serve Christ out of gratitude rather than fear of loss.

Reflection: Are you currently serving Christ because you're afraid of losing something, or because you're grateful for what's already secure?

Practical Step: Choose one act of service this week and do it explicitly “because He first loved,” rather than to prove or protect anything.
