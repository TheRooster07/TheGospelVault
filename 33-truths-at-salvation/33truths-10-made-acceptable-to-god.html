33 Truths at Salvation for Every Believer

Made Acceptable to God

Key Verse: Ephesians 1:6  ·  NASB1995

Key Verse

“to the praise of the glory of His grace, which He freely bestowed on us in the Beloved.”

Ephesians 1:6

This truth moves you from outsider to fully acceptable — and it means God now views you through Christ’s own righteousness, not your performance on any given day.

What This Means for You

I.

Made the Righteousness of God in Christ

“even the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ for all those who believe; for there is no distinction;”

Romans 3:22 · NASB1995

“But by His doing you are in Christ Jesus, who became to us wisdom from God, and righteousness and sanctification, and redemption,”

1 Corinthians 1:30 · NASB1995

“He made Him who knew no sin to be sin on our behalf, so that we might become the righteousness of God in Him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21 · NASB1995

“and may be found in Him, not having a righteousness of my own derived from the Law, but that which is through faith in Christ, the righteousness which comes from God on the basis of faith,”

Philippians 3:9 · NASB1995

God doesn’t just forgive your unrighteousness — He credits you with Christ’s own. Romans 3:22 describes “the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ for all those who believe,” and 2 Corinthians 5:21 explains the trade at the heart of the cross: “He made Him who knew no sin to be sin on our behalf, so that we might become the righteousness of God in Him.” First Corinthians 1:30 says Christ Himself “became to us...righteousness,” and Philippians 3:9 makes clear this righteousness is “not...my own derived from the Law, but that which is through faith in Christ.” You stand before God as righteous, not merely “not guilty.”

II.

Sanctified Positionally

“But by His doing you are in Christ Jesus, who became to us wisdom from God, and righteousness and sanctification, and redemption,”

1 Corinthians 1:30 · NASB1995

“Such were some of you; but you were washed, but you were sanctified, but you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and in the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11 · NASB1995

The moment you believed, God set you apart as holy in His sight. First Corinthians 1:30 lists “sanctification” alongside righteousness and redemption as something Christ already “became to us,” and 1 Corinthians 6:11 places it in the same breath as being washed and justified — a settled position, secured “in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ,” distinct from the day-by-day growth in holiness that follows.

III.

Perfected Forever

“For by one offering He has perfected for all time those who are sanctified.”

Hebrews 10:14 · NASB1995

Under the old covenant, sacrifices had to be offered again and again because they could never fully deal with sin. Hebrews 10:14 says Christ’s sacrifice did what no repeated offering ever could: “by one offering He has perfected for all time those who are sanctified.” No further payment is owed, and none is coming due.

IV.

Made Accepted in the Beloved

“to the praise of the glory of His grace, which He freely bestowed on us in the Beloved.”

Ephesians 1:6 · NASB1995

“you also, as living stones, are being built up as a spiritual house for a holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.”

1 Peter 2:5 · NASB1995

God’s acceptance of you isn’t based on your own merit at all. Ephesians 1:6 says grace was “freely bestowed on us in the Beloved” — the acceptance is His Son’s, and you share in it only because you are “in” Him. First Peter 2:5 pictures you as a living stone being built into a spiritual house, “to offer up spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ” — every approach you make to God travels through Christ’s own acceptability, not yours.

V.

Made Meet (Qualified)

“giving thanks to the Father, who has qualified us to share in the inheritance of the saints in Light.”

Colossians 1:12 · NASB1995

You didn’t have to make yourself fit for what God has for you. Colossians 1:12 gives thanks to “the Father, who has qualified us to share in the inheritance of the saints in Light.” He is the one who did the qualifying — the credential is His work, not your résumé.

Reflect

  1. Which is harder to believe: that God sees you as fully righteous in Christ, or that He has already qualified you for everything He has for you?
  2. How would your prayer life change if you truly believed you were already “accepted in the Beloved” before you said a word?
