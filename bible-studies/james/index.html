Vault 3 · New Testament

James

James: Living Wisely Under Pressure — a small group Bible study series through the letter of James, the Lord’s brother, on a faith that holds steady in trials and shows itself in how we hear, speak, and love. Each study includes the biblical text, a devotional, a full commentary, a self-study guide with Greek word study, and personal application. All sixteen studies are posted.

Studies 1–16

Choose a Passage

  1. 01The Brother Who Became a ServantJames 1:1James, once an unbeliever in Jesus’ own household, writes as a servant of the risen Lord to Jewish believers scattered by persecution, and his one-verse greeting sets the tone for a letter about living wisely when life is hard.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  2. 02Joy in the FurnaceJames 1:2–4Because God uses trials to prove our faith and build endurance, believers can meet every hardship with a settled joy, letting Him finish the work of making them mature, complete, and lacking nothing.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  3. 03Wisdom from Above, Wealth from BelowJames 1:5–11When trials reveal what we lack, God invites us to ask Him for wisdom with an undivided heart, and that wisdom teaches rich and poor alike to measure life by what lasts forever.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  4. 04The Crown, the Trap, and the Good GiverJames 1:12–18Believers who endure trials will be approved and rewarded with the crown of life, but they must never blame God for temptation, which springs from their own desires, because God is the unchanging Giver of every good gift, as their new birth proves.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  5. 05The Word That Saves a LifeJames 1:19–21Believers who were born by the Word must keep receiving it, with a listening, humble and peaceful heart, putting away moral filth, because the implanted Word is able to rescue their lives from the deadly consequences of sin.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  6. 06Mirror, Mirror: Hearing That Becomes DoingJames 1:22–27Hearing God’s Word is where growth begins but not where it ends; the believer who looks intently into the Word and does it will be blessed, and his religion will be seen in a controlled tongue, care for the helpless, and a life unstained by the world.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  7. 07Favorites in the PewJames 2:1–7Believers who trust in the glorious Lord Jesus Christ must not show favoritism, because judging by outward appearance contradicts His glory, dishonors those God has chosen to be rich in faith and heirs of His kingdom, and honors those who oppose Him.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  8. 08The Royal Law and the Triumph of MercyJames 2:8–13Believers fulfill the King’s royal law when they love their neighbor; favoritism breaks that law as truly as any other sin, and since believers will give an account at the Judgment Seat of Christ, they should speak and act with mercy, because mercy triumphs over judgment.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  9. 09What Good Is Faith That Does Nothing?James 2:14–17James does not teach that works are needed to receive or prove eternal life, which is a free gift received by faith alone; he teaches that a believer’s faith without works is useless, “dead,” unable to rescue a needy brother or the believer’s own life from sin’s ruin.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  10. 10The Objector, the Patriarch, and the HarlotJames 2:18–26James answers the objection that faith and works have no connection by showing, from Abraham and Rahab, that believers already declared righteous by faith are justified before others by works, which bring their faith to maturity and make it useful; faith without works is as useless as a body without the spirit.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  11. 11Bits, Sparks, and the Wisdom from AboveJames 3:1–18James warns those who would teach that they face a stricter evaluation at the Judgment Seat, because the tongue, though small, steers the whole life, spreads destruction like fire, cannot be tamed by human effort, and blesses and curses from the same mouth; the believer who would teach must show true wisdom, not the bitter, self-seeking wisdom from below, but the pure and peaceable wisdom from above that sows a harvest of righteousness.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  12. 12Wars Within and a Greater GraceJames 4:1–6Quarrels among believers come from worldly desires at war within them: worldliness covets, hates, envies, fights, neglects prayer and prays selfishly; such friendship with the world is spiritual adultery and hostility toward God, who jealously longs for the devotion of believers in whom He has made His Spirit dwell; yet He gives a greater grace to all who humble themselves.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  13. 13Submit, Draw Near, and Say “If the Lord Wills”James 4:7–17James prescribes the cure for worldliness: believers are to get right with God by submitting to Him, resisting the devil, drawing near, cleansing their lives, grieving over sin and humbling themselves; to get right with their brothers by refusing to speak against or judge them, since God alone is Lawgiver and Judge; and to plan their lives humbly, remembering that life is a vapor and saying, “If the Lord wills,” for to know the right thing and not do it is sin.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  14. 14Rusted Gold and the Patient FarmerJames 5:1–12James announces judgment on the rich who hoard wealth, cheat their workers, live in luxury and condemn the righteous, and he calls believers who suffer under such injustice to be patient like a farmer until the Lord’s coming, to strengthen their hearts because His coming is near, not to grumble against one another, to take the prophets and Job as examples of endurance, and above all to speak simple truth without oaths.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  15. 15Prayer for Every SeasonJames 5:13–18James calls believers to pray in every circumstance: personally, by praying in suffering and singing in joy; pastorally, by calling the elders to pray over the sick, anoint them and trust the Lord to raise them up and forgive their sins; and congregationally, by confessing sins to one another and praying for one another, because the effective prayer of a righteous person accomplishes much, as Elijah, a man with a nature like ours, proved.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
  16. 16Bring Them Back AliveJames 5:19–20James closes his letter by calling believers to go after any brother who strays from the truth and turn him back, because the one who does so saves the wanderer’s life from the physical death that sin brings and covers a multitude of his sins; anyone can wander, and anyone can be used to bring him back.→Leader / Learner GuideOne page
