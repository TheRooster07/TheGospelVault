Panel 3 of 5 · The Condition

Believe. That's the one condition — pictured six ways.

John 5:24; 3:14-15; 10:9, 16, 27; 4:14; 5:40; 6:37; 7:37; 1:12 (NASB)

Jn 5:24 Truly, truly, I say to you, he who hears My word, and believes Him who sent Me, has eternal life, and does not come into judgment, but has passed out of death into life. Jn 3:14-15 As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of Man be lifted up; so that whoever believes will in Him have eternal life. Jn 10:9 I am the door; if anyone enters through Me, he will be saved. Jn 10:16, 27 I have other sheep… they will hear My voice. My sheep hear My voice, and I know them, and they follow Me. Jn 4:14 Whoever drinks of the water that I will give him shall never thirst; but the water that I will give him will become in him a well of water springing up to eternal life. Jn 5:40; 6:37; 7:37 You are unwilling to come to Me so that you may have life. The one who comes to Me I will certainly not cast out. If anyone is thirsty, let him come to Me and drink. Jn 1:12 As many as received Him, to them He gave the right to become children of God, even to those who believe in His name.
Believe. That's the one condition.

John uses the word believe 98 times — more than the other three Gospels combined (34 times), and more than six times the rest of the New Testament (16 times). It's the word he's built his entire Gospel around. Believe means trust in someone, and it assumes you're convinced that what he said is true — you can't believe in someone without believing that what he claims is true; the two aren't really separable. Faith, in John's Gospel, is trusted reliance on Christ's promise to give eternal life to those who believe.

What does believing actually get you? Not a probationary status, not a maybe — eternal life, present tense, right now. He who hears My word, and believes Him who sent Me, has eternal life, and does not come into judgment, but has passed out of death into life (John 5:24). That's not a future hope you're waiting to find out about. It's a settled fact the moment you believe.

John pictures that one act of believing six different ways, so no one mistakes it for something complicated. Look — as Moses lifted up the serpent, so the Son of Man was lifted up, that whoever believes in Him would have eternal life (John 3:14-15). Hear — My sheep hear My voice, and they follow Me (John 10:16, 27). Enter — I am the door; if anyone enters through Me, he will be saved (John 10:9). Drink — whoever drinks of the water I give will never thirst (John 4:14). Come — the one who comes to Me I will certainly not cast out (John 5:40; 6:37; 7:37). Receive — as many as received Him, to them He gave the right to become children of God (John 1:12). Six pictures, one act: simple trust in what Christ has promised.

For reflection

  1. Which picture — looking, hearing, entering, drinking, coming, or receiving — best describes what happened when you first believed?
  2. John 5:24 says the one who believes "has" eternal life and "has passed" from death into life — both settled, not pending. Does your own sense of assurance match that tense?

Simple trust in Christ's promise. That's the whole condition — nothing hidden, nothing added.
