50 Hard Questions · Question 28 of 50

How Should Christians Think About Evolution and the Age of the Earth?

Translation: NKJV · Free Grace / Dispensational · Source: The Gospel Vault

In Brief

God created purposefully. This answer takes the straightforward reading of Genesis — six ordinary days, a young earth, and death entering through Adam’s sin. Not all believers agree, and the question does not decide anyone’s eternal life.

Key versesGenesis 1:1 · Exodus 20:11 · Romans 5:12

Scripture settles, without ambiguity, that God created the universe and everything in it purposefully, not by unguided chance: “In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth” (Genesis 1:1); “All things were made through Him, and without Him nothing was made that was made” (John 1:3). The question is how to read the details Genesis gives — the days, their order, and the age they imply.

This paper takes the straightforward reading of Genesis 1–11: God created in six ordinary days, rested on the seventh, and did so thousands rather than billions of years ago. The Hebrew word for “day” (yom), joined to a number and to “evening and morning” (Genesis 1:5, 8, 13), describes an ordinary day. The fourth commandment grounds Israel’s weekly Sabbath in that same six-day pattern: “For in six days the LORD made the heavens and the earth, the sea, and all that is in them, and rested the seventh day” (Exodus 20:11). And the genealogies of Genesis 5 and 11 tie the first man to the line of Abraham.

This reading also keeps the Bible’s account of death consistent. Scripture presents death and the disorder of creation as entering through Adam’s sin (Romans 5:12; 8:20–21; see Q1, “Why Does God Allow Evil and Suffering?” and Q2, “Why Does God Allow Natural Disasters?”), after God had called His finished creation “very good” (Genesis 1:31). Views that place long ages of death, disease, and extinction before Adam — including theistic evolution — make death part of God’s original “very good” design rather than the intrusion Christ came to destroy, “the last enemy” (1 Corinthians 15:26).

Naturalistic evolution, as commonly taught, goes further and denies any directing intelligence at all — a claim that cannot be squared with Genesis 1’s repeated refrain that God made each kind and saw that it was good, or with Paul’s insistence that creation itself testifies to its Maker’s “eternal power and Godhead” (Romans 1:20). Creation ministries have worked through the scientific questions in detail for readers who want to go further.1

Not all Bible-believing Christians hold this view; some who affirm inerrancy and a historical Adam read the days of Genesis differently, and the question does not decide anyone’s eternal life, which rests on faith in Christ alone (John 3:16). What Scripture will not permit is a purely impersonal, purposeless account of human origins, or a denial that Adam and Eve were real individuals through whom sin and death entered the world (see Q27, “Did Adam and Eve Really Exist, and Does It Matter?”).
